Full Civil Symbol Reference

Every symbol above, with its full meaning and typical usage in one scannable table — useful for a quick page search or side-by-side comparison instead of clicking through each tile individually.

SymbolCategoryMeaningWhere it's used
Column (Plan)StructuralA filled or outlined square at a grid intersection — represents a vertical structural column in plan view.Structural framing plans, at every column location on the grid.
Beam (Section)StructuralA rectangular outline in cross-section — represents a beam's profile when cut through.Structural section drawings showing beam depth and width at a given cut line.
FootingStructuralA stepped profile — a wider base slab supporting a narrower column stem above it.Foundation plans and sections at isolated or strip footing locations.
Rebar MarkStructuralA wavy line representing the deformed ribs on a reinforcing bar, distinguishing it from a smooth structural line.Reinforcement drawings and bar bending schedules, wherever a rebar run is shown.
North ArrowDrawing ConventionsAn arrow with the letter N — orients every plan drawing to true or project north.Placed once per plan sheet, usually in a corner, to establish drawing orientation.
Section Cut MarkerDrawing ConventionsA line with flagged ends and arrows — marks exactly where a section or detail is cut from the plan.Placed on plan views to reference a corresponding section or detail drawing elsewhere in the set.
Grid BubbleDrawing ConventionsA circle enclosing a letter or number — labels a structural grid line for coordinate reference across the drawing set.At the end of every column grid line on structural and architectural plans.
Expansion JointStructuralTwo parallel lines with a dashed gap between them — marks a joint that allows structural movement.Long slabs, pavements and building separations where thermal or seismic movement must be accommodated.
Slope / Grade ArrowSite &amp; CivilAn arrow pointing downhill, often labeled with a percentage — indicates the direction and rate of ground or pavement slope.Grading and drainage plans, showing which way surface water is meant to flow.
ManholeSite &amp; CivilTwo concentric circles — the plan symbol for a manhole chamber, with the inner circle representing the cover.Utility and drainage plans at every access chamber along a buried pipe run.
Shear WallStructuralA solid wall panel with diagonal hatching — a wall specifically designed to resist lateral (wind/seismic) load, not just gravity.Marked on structural plans wherever a wall is a primary lateral load-resisting element, distinct from a non-structural partition.
TrussStructuralA triangulated line diagram — a framework of members in pure tension and compression, no bending, spanning between two supports.Roof and long-span floor framing plans showing truss layout and spacing.
Bracing (X-Brace)StructuralDiagonal members crossing within a structural bay — resists lateral sway by acting in tension and compression.Steel-framed buildings and towers where diagonal bracing, not moment frames, provides lateral stability.
PileStructuralA vertical shaft with a pointed tip — a deep foundation element transferring load past weak surface soil to a competent bearing layer.Foundation plans wherever shallow footings can't reach adequate bearing capacity.
Retaining WallStructuralAn L-shaped or gravity wall section with soil implied on the high side — holds back a difference in ground level.Site sections and civil drawings anywhere a change in grade needs to be structurally retained.
Slab EdgeStructuralA slab cross-section terminating at a thickened or turned-down edge — the boundary of a floor or roof slab.Structural sections showing where a concrete slab ends and how its edge is detailed.
JoistStructuralA series of parallel horizontal lines with regular cross-marks — closely spaced secondary members supporting a floor or roof deck.Framing plans showing joist spacing and direction between primary beams.
Moment ConnectionStructuralA beam-to-column joint with added plates or stiffeners shown — transfers bending moment, not just shear, between members.Steel connection details wherever a frame relies on rigid, moment-resisting joints for lateral stability.
Elevation MarkerDrawing ConventionsA circle with a tail pointing down to a level — references a drawing showing that vertical face or viewpoint.Plans indicating where a building or wall elevation drawing is taken from.
Detail CalloutDrawing ConventionsA circle split into a detail number and sheet reference — points to a larger-scale drawing of that specific area.Any drawing where a small area needs a magnified, more detailed drawing referenced elsewhere in the set.
Match LineDrawing ConventionsA long-dashed line across a drawing — marks where one sheet's plan continues onto an adjoining sheet.Large site or floor plans split across multiple sheets, showing exactly where they align.
Revision CloudDrawing ConventionsA scalloped cloud outline around an area of a drawing — flags exactly what changed since the previous issue.Revised drawing issues, paired with a revision number so reviewers can find changes quickly.
Center LineDrawing ConventionsA long-dash, short-dash alternating line — marks a symmetrical or reference centerline, not a physical edge.Column grid lines, road centerlines and axes of symmetry across civil and structural drawings.
Property LineDrawing ConventionsA line with a distinct long-short dash pattern — the legal boundary of a parcel of land, not a construction element.Site plans establishing setbacks and confirming a building sits within its legal boundary.
Contour LineSite &amp; CivilA curved line connecting points of equal elevation — reveals a site's existing or proposed ground shape without spot elevations everywhere.Topographic and grading plans showing how the land surface rises and falls across a site.
Catch BasinSite &amp; CivilA square grated inlet symbol — collects surface stormwater runoff and directs it into the underground drainage system.Site drainage and grading plans marking every point stormwater enters the piped network.
CulvertSite &amp; CivilA circular pipe symbol shown crossing beneath a roadway or embankment — carries a watercourse under a raised crossing.Road and site drainage plans wherever a stream or ditch passes beneath an embankment.
CurbSite &amp; CivilA raised edge line along a roadway — separates the pavement from the shoulder, verge or sidewalk.Road and parking layout drawings defining the pavement edge and drainage direction.
SidewalkSite &amp; CivilA paved strip with expansion joint marks — a pedestrian walkway alongside a road or building.Site and streetscape plans showing pedestrian circulation and required accessible paths.
Fire HydrantSite &amp; CivilA circle on a stem with side outlet marks — a fixed water supply point for firefighting.Site utility plans confirming fire hydrant coverage and hose-lay distance to every building.
Utility PoleSite &amp; CivilA vertical pole with crossarm lines — carries overhead electrical or communication lines.Site and utility plans marking existing overhead line routes and clearances.
Bench MarkSite &amp; CivilA triangle over a short vertical tick — a fixed, known-elevation reference point a survey ties all other levels back to.Survey and grading plans establishing the vertical datum every other elevation on the drawing is measured from.
Construction JointConcrete DetailsA break line across a concrete element — marks a planned interruption between two separate concrete placements.Large slabs and walls poured in stages, showing exactly where one pour stopped and the next began.
Control JointConcrete DetailsA dashed groove line — a deliberately weakened line that encourages shrinkage cracking to occur in a controlled, hidden location.Slabs-on-grade and pavements, spaced to control where random shrinkage cracks would otherwise appear.
Rebar HookConcrete DetailsA bar bent through 90° or 180° at its end — develops anchorage in a shorter embedment length than a straight bar.Beam and column reinforcement details wherever a straight bar can't achieve the required development length.
Rebar SpliceConcrete DetailsTwo bar ends overlapping with cross-ties — joins two lengths of reinforcement so they act as one continuous bar.Reinforcement schedules and details wherever bar length exceeds standard stock length or a construction joint interrupts it.
StirrupConcrete DetailsA closed loop of smaller-diameter bar wrapping the main reinforcement — resists shear and holds the main bars in position.Beam cross-sections showing shear reinforcement spacing along the member's length.
Dowel BarConcrete DetailsA short bar crossing a joint, bonded on one side and free to slide on the other — transfers load across a joint while allowing movement.Pavement and slab construction joints designed to transfer wheel loads without locking the joint rigid.
ChamferConcrete DetailsA small diagonal cut at a concrete corner — a triangular strip formed to prevent a fragile 90° arris from chipping.Exposed concrete edges and corners on columns, beams and walls.
WaterstopConcrete DetailsA ribbed strip embedded across a construction joint — blocks water from tracking through the joint.Below-grade walls, water-retaining structures and any joint that must remain watertight.
Anchor Bolt GroupConcrete DetailsA cluster of vertical bolts cast into a concrete base — the connection point for a steel column base plate.Foundation plans at every steel column base showing bolt pattern and projection.
Embedded PlateConcrete DetailsA steel plate cast flush into a concrete face with anchors behind it — a weld-ready connection point cast directly into concrete.Precast and cast-in-place concrete elements needing a later steel connection welded on site.
Water LineUtilitiesA solid line labeled W — represents a buried potable water main on a utility plan.Site utility drawings tracing the water supply route and connection points.
Sewer LineUtilitiesA solid line labeled SS (sanitary sewer) — carries wastewater by gravity or force main to treatment.Site utility plans tracing the sanitary drainage route and manhole spacing.
Storm DrainUtilitiesA solid line labeled SD — carries stormwater runoff, kept physically separate from the sanitary sewer.Site drainage plans tracing the piped stormwater route from catch basins to an outfall.
Gas LineUtilitiesA solid line labeled G — a buried fuel gas distribution main.Utility coordination plans showing gas main routing and required clearance from other services.
Electrical Duct BankUtilitiesA concrete envelope containing several conduits — routes multiple underground electrical circuits together as one buried structure.Site electrical distribution plans wherever several circuits are routed together underground.
CleanoutUtilitiesA capped vertical stub off a drainage line at grade — provides access for rodding out a blockage.Sanitary and storm drainage plans at every required access point along a buried pipe run.
Valve BoxUtilitiesA protective sleeve at grade over a buried valve's operating nut — lets the valve be operated from the surface without excavation.Water and gas utility plans marking the location of buried isolation valves.
Meter BoxUtilitiesA protective enclosure at grade housing a utility meter — the metering point between the utility main and a service connection.Site utility plans at each property's water, gas or electrical service connection point.
Utility TrenchUtilitiesA cross-section showing a shared excavated trench — several utilities routed together to minimize separate digging.Utility coordination sections showing horizontal and vertical separation between co-located services.
Backflow PreventerUtilitiesAn assembly inserted in a water line — allows flow in one direction only, preventing contaminated water from siphoning back into the main.Fire sprinkler and irrigation service connections wherever cross-contamination of the potable supply must be prevented.
