Refrigerant Charge — Superheat / Subcooling

Actual superheat or subcooling vs target, from field-measured temperatures
A correct refrigerant charge is checked, not guessed — fixed-orifice / cap-tube systems are charged to a target superheat, while TXV / EEV systems are charged to a target subcooling. Superheat — Suction Side

Measured with a clamp/strap-on thermocouple on the suction line, close to the compressor inlet.
Read directly off the low-side gauge's temperature scale for the refrigerant in use (most digital manifolds display this automatically).
Fixed-orifice systems don't have one fixed number — the real target comes from the manufacturer's superheat chart (varies with outdoor temp and indoor wet bulb). 4–8°C (7–14°F) is a common ballpark; use the chart or nameplate spec when available.
2

Subcooling — Liquid Side

Measured at the liquid line, near the condenser outlet or just before the metering device.
Read directly off the high-side gauge's temperature scale for the refrigerant in use.
TXV/EEV systems have a fixed factory target — commonly 4–7°C (8–12°F), but always confirm against the specific unit's nameplate or manufacturer spec.

How this is calculated
Superheat = Tsuction line − Tevaporator sat.  ·  Subcooling = Tcondenser sat. − Tliquid line
Both are simple temperature differences once you have the saturation temperature (from the gauge manifold) and the actual line temperature (from a clamp-on thermocouple) — no refrigerant P-T chart lookup needed if your manifold already displays saturation temperature.
i

Reading the result

  • ✓Superheat method: superheat noticeably above target usually points to undercharge (not enough liquid reaching the evaporator, so it boils off early); superheat noticeably below target usually points to overcharge or a metering restriction issue.
  • ✓Subcooling method: subcooling noticeably below target usually points to undercharge (not enough liquid backed up in the condenser); subcooling noticeably above target usually points to overcharge.
  • ✓A ±2°C (≈±3–4°F) band around target is treated as close enough here — but always weigh it against other symptoms (compressor amps, sight glass, discharge pressure) rather than this number alone.
A charging check, not a substitute for weighing in refrigerant to nameplate charge on a new/reclaimed system, and not a substitute for a qualified technician's full diagnosis — superheat/subcooling alone can be misleading on a system with other faults (restriction, non-condensables, bad airflow) present at the same time.
