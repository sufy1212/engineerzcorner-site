Refrigeration / Chiller Capacity

Cooling duty and COP → compressor power and heat rejection
HVAC Index HV.01 Duct Sizing HV.02 Cooling Load HV.03 Chiller Capacity HV.04 Notes
A refrigeration cycle moves heat out of the cooled space using compressor work — COP is the ratio of cooling delivered to electrical input needed. This sheet works out that input power and the total heat the condenser has to reject.

Cooling Duty & Efficiency

Typical COP: air-cooled chiller 2.8–3.5, water-cooled chiller 4–6, VRF 3–4, packaged rooftop unit 2.5–3.2. Higher COP means less electrical input for the same cooling.
2

Refrigerant Side (optional)

Varies by refrigerant and operating pressure — R410A and R32 are commonly in the 160–220 kJ/kg range at typical AC evaporating conditions; check the refrigerant's own P-h chart for an exact figure.

How this is calculated
Pcompressor = Qcooling / COP  ·  Qreject = Qcooling + Pcompressor
ṁrefrigerant ≈ Qcooling / hlatent  (a simplified estimate — ignores subcooling/superheat, which shift the real figure)
Condenser heat rejection is always higher than the cooling duty — it has to reject both the heat removed from the space and the compressor's own work input, which is exactly why a condenser is sized above the evaporator-side cooling load, not equal to it.
