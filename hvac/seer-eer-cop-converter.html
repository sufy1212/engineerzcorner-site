SEER / EER / COP Converter

Three efficiency ratings for the same equipment, three different test conditions
COP, EER and SEER all describe how much cooling (or heating) output you get per unit of electrical input — they just measure it differently. COP and EER convert to each other exactly (they're the same ratio in different units). SEER is a seasonal average across many outdoor conditions, so there's no exact formula linking it to EER — only a commonly used approximation.

How this is calculated
EER = COP × 3.412  (exact — 1 kW = 3412 BTU/h)
SEER ≈ EER × 1.2   /   EER ≈ SEER ÷ 1.2  (rule-of-thumb approximation, not a physical identity)
COP and EER describe the same efficiency at one specific full-load test condition, just in different units (dimensionless vs BTU/h per W) — the 3.412 factor is exact. SEER (or SEER2, the updated test procedure) is a seasonal weighted average across a range of outdoor temperatures per the governing test standard, so it doesn't reduce to a fixed multiple of EER for every unit — real equipment can deviate meaningfully from the 1.2 approximation used here.
A quick-reference conversion, not a substitute for the manufacturer's AHRI-certified performance data — SEER/SEER2, EER, and COP are each measured under a different governing test standard, and only COP↔EER converts exactly.
