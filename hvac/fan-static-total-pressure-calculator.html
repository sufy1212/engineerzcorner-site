Fan Static & Total Pressure

Field-test FSP and FTP from duct static pressure and velocity readings
Fan static pressure (FSP) and fan total pressure (FTP) are what the fan is actually working against — not just the static pressure difference across it. This follows the standard AMCA field-test relationship: measure static pressure just outside the fan's inlet and outlet, work out the velocity pressure at each point, and combine them properly.

How this is calculated
VP = ½ × ρ × V²  (velocity pressure at each point)
FTP = (SPout + VPout) − (SPin + VPin)
FSP = FTP − VPout  =  SPout − SPin − VPin
FSP excludes the outlet velocity pressure because that energy is considered "used up" once the air leaves the fan discharge — it's why FSP is always lower than FTP, and why a fan curve plotted in static pressure isn't the same thing as one plotted in total pressure.
i

Reading the result

  • ✓If the inlet is drawing straight from an open plenum or room, inlet velocity is effectively 0 — FSP then simplifies to just the static pressure difference (SPout − SPin).
  • ✓Compare the calculated FSP against the fan's published performance curve at the measured airflow — a big gap usually means excess system resistance (dirty filter, undersized duct, closed damper) or a fan speed/selection issue.
  • ✓A negative FSP result almost always means a measurement or tap location error, not a real fan condition — double-check probe placement and manometer sign convention before trusting the number.
Field-test relationship per the standard AMCA static/total pressure definitions — actual fan performance still depends on the specific fan curve, system effect at the inlet/outlet connections, and air density at the test conditions.
