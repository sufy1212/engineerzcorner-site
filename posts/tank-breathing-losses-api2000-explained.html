Tank Breathing Losses

A tank that's never filled or emptied still has to vent — the sun rising and setting is enough on its own. That's normal breathing, and it's a separate calculation from filling and emptying entirely.

Two separate reasons a tank vents

An atmospheric storage tank needs a way to breathe simply because its vapor space isn't a fixed volume of gas — it expands and contracts, and something has to give. There are two distinct, additive mechanisms behind that movement, and API 2000 treats them separately before combining them.

1Thermal Breathing

  • Day-night ambient temperature swings heat and cool the vapor space, expanding it during the day (outbreathing) and contracting it overnight (inbreathing)
  • Happens with zero liquid movement — an idle, sealed tank still needs to vent purely from this effect
  • API 2000's simplified method gives thermal breathing rate as a function of tank capacity alone, from standard lookup tables (Table 1)

2Working Losses

  • Filling: the incoming liquid displaces vapor, pushing it out through the vent — outbreathing driven directly by the fill rate
  • Emptying: the falling liquid level draws air in to replace the departing volume — inbreathing driven by the pump-out rate
  • API 2000's simplified method (Table 2) approximates the working venting rate as proportional to the maximum filling or emptying rate, in air-equivalent volume

3Normal Vent (PV Valve) Sizing

  • Total outbreathing = thermal outbreathing + working outbreathing; total inbreathing = thermal inbreathing + working inbreathing — both totals matter, sized separately
  • The normal (pressure/vacuum relief) vent is sized to the larger of the two combined totals, at its rated set pressure and set vacuum
  • These are normal operating conditions — not an emergency or upset case, which is why this is called "normal" venting

4Fire-Case Venting (Separate Check)

  • External fire exposure boils liquid inside the tank at a rate far exceeding anything normal breathing or working losses produce
  • Sized by an entirely different method (API 2000's fire-exposure equations, based on wetted surface area) and usually requires a much larger emergency vent or relief device
  • A tank's normal vent being correctly sized says nothing about whether its fire-case venting is adequate — the two are independent checks against independent requirements

Working through the simplified method

1Determine tank shell capacity (and diameter, for larger tanks) as the basis for the thermal breathing lookup.
2Read thermal outbreathing and inbreathing rates from API 2000 Table 1 for that capacity.
3Calculate working outbreathing from the maximum filling rate, and working inbreathing from the maximum emptying (pump-out) rate, per API 2000 Table 2.
4Add thermal and working requirements separately for each direction: total outbreathing and total inbreathing.
5Size the normal vent (PV valve) to the larger of the two totals, at its rated set pressure and set vacuum.
6Check fire-case venting separately, using the wetted-surface-area method — never substitute the normal vent sizing for it.

Common terms, decoded

TermMeaning
Thermal breathingVapor space expansion and contraction from ambient temperature changes, occurring even with no liquid movement
Working lossVapor displaced (outbreathing) or air drawn in (inbreathing) as a direct result of filling or emptying the tank
Outbreathing / InbreathingVapor leaving the tank (outbreathing, from heating or filling) versus air entering it (inbreathing, from cooling or emptying)
Normal ventThe pressure/vacuum relief device sized for everyday thermal and working venting, as distinct from emergency or fire-case venting
Fire-case ventingEmergency venting sized for the much higher vapor generation rate caused by external fire exposure, calculated separately from normal venting

What each mechanism is sensitive to

Capacity Dependent
Fill/Empty-Rate Dependent
Ambient-Swing Dependent

A tank in an intermittent or idle service is easy to underestimate — with no scheduled fills or draws, it's tempting to assume it needs no venting attention at all. Thermal breathing keeps happening regardless, driven purely by daily ambient temperature swings, which is exactly why API 2000's thermal table has no filling or emptying rate as an input at all.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Thermal breathing happens with the tank completely idle — ambient temperature swings alone are enough to require normal venting.
  • ✓Working losses from filling and emptying are additive to thermal breathing, not alternatives to it.
  • ✓The normal vent is sized to the larger combined total — outbreathing or inbreathing — not to either mechanism in isolation.
  • ✓Fire-case venting is a completely separate calculation with much higher flow requirements — a correctly sized normal vent doesn't imply fire-case adequacy.
  • ✓An idle tank in intermittent service still needs its normal vent checked — thermal breathing doesn't stop just because the tank isn't being filled or emptied.
