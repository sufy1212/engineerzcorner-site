Flame Retardant vs Flame Resistant Cables: Know the Difference

Both names show up on the same cable schedules, both sound like "fireproof cable," and they get swapped for each other constantly. They test for two completely different things — and specifying the wrong one can leave a life-safety circuit dead exactly when it's needed most.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Electrical / Cables
01
Flame retardant (IEC 60332) is about stopping fire from spreading along a cable run — it says nothing about the circuit still working.
02
Flame — or more precisely fire — resistant (IEC 60331) is about the circuit staying alive and carrying power or signal while it burns.
03
Retardant cable is the default for general power, control, and instrumentation runs.
04
Resistant cable is mandatory wherever a system has to keep operating during a fire — alarms, emergency lighting, smoke extraction, fire pumps.

The core distinction

Both terms describe how a cable behaves in a fire, and both get tested by exposing the cable to flame — but they're answering different questions. Flame retardant asks: if this cable is on fire, does the fire stop here, or does it climb the tray and spread to everything else? Flame resistant asks something much harder: while this cable is actually burning, does it keep conducting? A cable can easily pass one test and have no bearing on the other — they aren't degrees of the same property, they're two separate properties.

The two standards

IEC 60332
Flame Retardant Designed to limit the spread of fire along the cable. May stop burning once the flame source is removed. Says nothing about whether the circuit keeps functioning.
IEC 60331
Flame Resistant Designed to maintain circuit integrity during a fire — the cable is tested while energized, and power or signal must keep passing through it as it burns.

What each one is actually protecting

Flame retardant — prevents fire from spreading
  • 1Prevents fire from spreading along the cable route
  • 2Helps contain the fire and minimize damage to nearby cables and equipment
  • 3May self-extinguish once the flame source is removed
Flame resistant — keeps the circuit alive
  • 1Continues to operate and conduct while actively burning
  • 2Maintains power or signal transmission through the fire event
  • 3Essential wherever the connected system is itself a life-safety function

Common cable designations

  1. 1
    FR (Flame Retardant)Standard flame-retardant sheath compound — the baseline for most LT power and control cable.
  2. 2
    FRLS (Flame Retardant Low Smoke)Adds reduced smoke and acid-gas emission on top of flame retardance — common in occupied buildings.
  3. 3
    LSZH / LSOH (Low Smoke Zero Halogen)Minimizes toxic and corrosive smoke, prioritized where evacuation and equipment protection both matter.
  4. 4
    FS / Fire SurvivalBuilt and tested to IEC 60331 — keeps circuit continuity through direct flame exposure at a stated temperature and duration.
  5. 5
    E30 / E60 / E90Fire-resistance duration rating in minutes — the circuit must keep functioning for that stated period under fire conditions.
Fire-resistant cable isn't a "better" version of flame-retardant cable — it's solving a different problem, usually at a higher cost, and it's typically specified for a defined circuit list rather than an entire installation.

Easy way to remember

Flame RetardantStops the fire from spreading.
Flame ResistantKeeps the system operating during the fire.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Flame retardant (IEC 60332) limits fire spread along the cable route — it's a property of the sheath, not the circuit.
  • ✓Flame resistant (IEC 60331) keeps the energized circuit functioning while it burns — a property of the whole cable construction, tested under load.
  • ✓Passing IEC 60332 does not imply passing IEC 60331, and vice versa — check the actual certificate for the standard you need.
  • ✓Any circuit that's part of the life-safety response — alarms, emergency lighting, smoke extraction, fire pumps — needs fire-resistant cable, not just flame-retardant cable.
