Alarm Setpoint Bands: Why High and High-High Are Different Alarms

A four-level alarm band isn't redundancy — High and High-High trigger different responses, and each needs its own reset point to avoid flooding the alarm list.

High and High-High are two separate alarms with different responses, not one alarm with a safety margin.

Setpoints are usually configured as %FS of the signal's engineering range, then converted to real units.

One alarm isn't enough for a real process

A tank running high on level is a warning. A tank about to overflow is an emergency. Using a single "High" alarm for both means either the warning fires too late to matter, or the emergency response fires on every routine high reading. Splitting the band into High and High-High — and Low and Low-Low on the other side — lets each threshold carry its own meaning and its own response, from "notify the operator" up to "trip the interlock."

Setpoints are configured as a percentage of full scale so they stay meaningful if the transmitter's range is ever re-calibrated — the %FS positions don't need to change, only the EU output does.

Why each alarm needs its own reset point

Without a reset deadband, an alarm that trips at exactly 80% and clears at exactly 80% will chatter on and off as the signal hovers around that line — the same chattering problem a simple on/off switch has. A reset deadband gives each alarm a lower point it has to drop back through before it clears, separate from the point that tripped it in the first place.

AlarmTrips when signal rises aboveClears when signal falls below
HighHigh setpointHigh setpoint − deadband
High-HighHigh-High setpointHigh-High setpoint − deadband
LowLow setpoint (falling)Low setpoint + deadband
Low-LowLow-Low setpoint (falling)Low-Low setpoint + deadband
This is the same deadband/hysteresis mechanism used for any single switch, applied four times — once per alarm level. A High-High alarm typically drives a hard interlock or shutdown, so its deadband and response logic usually get more scrutiny than the informational High alarm.

Key takeaways

  • ✓High/High-High and Low/Low-Low are four distinct alarms, each with its own response, not one alarm repeated.
  • ✓Each alarm needs its own reset deadband to avoid chattering near its trip point.
  • ✓Setpoints configured as %FS survive a re-ranging of the transmitter without needing to be re-tuned.
  • ✓Spacing High and High-High too closely defeats the point of having an escalation at all.
