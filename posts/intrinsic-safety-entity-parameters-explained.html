Intrinsic Safety Entity Parameters

An intrinsically safe loop stays safe because the energy that can reach the hazardous area is limited. The entity concept lets you check that from the nameplates of a barrier and a field device, plus the cable between them, without a system-level test.

Engineerz CorneRReference · IEC 60079-11
01
The barrier (associated apparatus) must supply no more voltage, current and power than the field device can accept: Uo ≤ Ui, Io ≤ Ii, Po ≤ Pi.
02
The energy that can be stored in the loop must also stay within the barrier's limits: Ci + Ccable ≤ Co and Li + Lcable ≤ Lo.
03
Cable capacitance is usually the limit that runs out first. It sets a maximum cable length, and the length can be much shorter than the datasheet suggests.
04
The nameplate values are certified for a gas group and zone. Passing the five checks is necessary, not sufficient, for an approved installation.

The idea: limit the energy

A spark or hot surface ignites a flammable atmosphere only if it has enough energy. Intrinsic safety (IEC 60079-11) keeps the energy in the field circuit below that level, even under fault, by limiting voltage, current and power, and by limiting the energy that can be stored in cable and device capacitance and inductance. The levels of protection are:

LevelSafe withZones
Ex iaTwo faults0, 1 and 2
Ex ibOne fault1 and 2
Ex icNormal operation only2

The entity concept is how you combine devices from different manufacturers. The barrier is certified with output limits (Uo, Io, Po, Co, Lo) and the field device with input limits (Ui, Ii, Pi, Ci, Li). If the two sets are compatible, the combination is safe without being tested together.

The five checks

CheckRuleWhat it protects against
VoltageUo ≤ UiThe barrier's fault voltage exceeding what the device is certified to see
CurrentIo ≤ IiToo much fault current into the device
PowerPo ≤ PiToo much power dissipated in the device
CapacitanceCi + Ccable ≤ CoStored electrical energy released as a spark
InductanceLi + Lcable ≤ LoStored magnetic energy released when the circuit is broken

For a linear (resistive) source the barrier's maximum output power is Po = Uo × Io / 4, which is the most any load can draw from it. A barrier of 28 V and 93 mA is therefore about 650 mW. If a device has several parts in the loop, use the sum of their Ci and Li.

Cable adds capacitance and inductance

Use the cable datasheet values. If they are not known, the figures commonly specified on control drawings are 60 pF/ft (about 197 nF/km) and 0.2 µH/ft (about 0.66 mH/km). Real shielded instrument cable is often lower, roughly 45–200 nF/km, but the defaults are safe when you do not have data.

Worked example

The values below are illustrative, not a particular product. Barrier: Uo = 28 V, Io = 93 mA, Po = 650 mW, Co = 80 nF, Lo = 3.0 mH. Field device: Ui = 30 V, Ii = 100 mA, Pi = 750 mW, Ci = 10 nF, Li = 0.1 mH. Cable run of 200 m using the default values.

CheckWorkingResult
Voltage28 V ≤ 30 VPass
Current93 mA ≤ 100 mAPass
Power650 mW ≤ 750 mWPass
Capacitance10 + 39.4 = 49.4 nF ≤ 80 nFPass
Inductance0.1 + 0.131 = 0.231 mH ≤ 3.0 mHPass

Rearranged, the cable length limits are: by capacitance, (80 − 10) / 0.197 = 356 m; by inductance, (3.0 − 0.1) / 0.00066 = 4,420 m. Capacitance binds long before inductance does, so the usable run is about 356 m, not the length that the cable's other properties would allow. Better cable (lower nF/km) lengthens the limit directly.

The combined L and C reduction

Capacitance and inductance are both present in the loop at once, and the allowable values for each are certified separately. Many control drawings and the approval rules behind them therefore apply an additional condition: if the external Ci and Li (excluding cable) are both more than 1% of Co and Lo, the allowed Co and Lo are reduced to 50% of the marked values. If either one is under 1%, the marked values can be used in full.

In the example, Ci is 10 / 80 = 12.5% of Co and Li is 0.1 / 3.0 = 3.3% of Lo. Both exceed 1%, so where this condition applies the effective limits would be 40 nF and 1.5 mH, and the capacitance limit on the cable drops to (40 − 10) / 0.197 = 152 m, less than half of the first answer. Whether it applies, and its exact wording, depends on the certificate and control drawing in front of you, so read them.

What the five checks do not cover

  • ✓Gas group and temperature class. Co and Lo are certified per gas group, with the lowest values for IIC (hydrogen, acetylene). Use the column for the group of the area.
  • ✓Zone and level of protection. The barrier and field device must both be certified for the zone, and an Ex ic device cannot go in Zone 0 or 1.
  • ✓Multiple devices on one barrier. Add their Ci and Li (and check the combined Pi), as the entity rules are written for the total of the loop.
  • ✓Fieldbus. FISCO and FNICO use their own model-specific limits instead of entity values.
  • ✓Installation rules. Cable segregation, shielding, earthing of barriers and documentation are separate requirements.

Mistakes that catch people out

MistakeWhat goes wrong
Ignoring the cableLong runs of high-capacitance cable can exceed Co by themselves
Checking voltage and current but not Ci and LiThe stored-energy limits are what make the loop safe under fault
Using the wrong gas-group columnAllowed values for IIB are considerably higher than for IIC
Using typical values instead of the certificateOnly the certified values count
Overlooking the combined L and C ruleThe passing result at full Co and Lo can fail when reduced to 50%
Substituting a similar barrier or deviceAny change needs the entity values re-checked

Key takeaways

  • ✓Barrier outputs must not exceed device inputs: Uo ≤ Ui, Io ≤ Ii, Po ≤ Pi.
  • ✓Stored energy: Ci + Ccable ≤ Co and Li + Lcable ≤ Lo, with cable data from the datasheet or the 60 pF/ft and 0.2 µH/ft defaults.
  • ✓Work out which limit binds. Capacitance usually sets the maximum cable length.
  • ✓Check the control drawing for the combined L and C reduction and the gas-group values.
  • ✓This is a design check, not certification. Follow IEC 60079-11 and the certificates for the real installation.
