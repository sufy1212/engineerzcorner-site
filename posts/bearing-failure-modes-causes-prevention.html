Bearing Failures: Root Causes & Prevention

A bearing rarely fails for a single, mysterious reason. Almost every premature failure traces back to one of five recognizable mechanisms — each with its own signature, its own root cause, and its own fix.

Five failure modes, five different mechanisms

Bearings don't just "wear out" in one generic way. Each of the mechanisms below damages the raceway and rolling elements differently, leaves a different physical signature at teardown, and is stopped by a different fix — which is exactly why correctly identifying which one you're looking at matters more than simply noticing "the bearing is bad."

1Rolling Contact Fatigue (Spalling)

  • Dynamic overload beyond the bearing's rated life
  • Reduced or marginal lubrication film thickness
  • Misalignment or excessive internal clearance
Solution: L10 life calculation & vibration monitoring

2Lubrication Breakdown

  • Incorrect grease type or viscosity for the application
  • Over-greasing, causing churning and heat, or under-greasing
  • Grease that has aged or oxidized past its service interval
Solution: oil analysis & automatic lubrication

3Contamination Wear

  • Poor seal selection or a degraded, worn-out seal
  • Dirty or dusty operating environment
Solution: improved sealing & ISO cleanliness targets

4Electrical Erosion (VFD Motors)

  • Circulating shaft currents from VFD switching
  • Electrical discharge machining (EDM)-type pitting and fluting
Solution: insulated bearings & shaft grounding rings

5False Brinelling

  • Vibration transmitted into a bearing while the shaft stands still
  • Common in transit, storage, or standby equipment
Solution: shaft locking & periodic rotation

Catching each one before teardown

Three monitoring methods, three different windows into bearing health

No single monitoring method catches every failure mode equally well. Vibration analysis is generally the earliest warning for developing fatigue and looseness; ultrasound is sensitive to the very first stages of lubrication starvation, often before vibration signatures change; oil analysis reveals wear metal and contamination building up in the lubricant itself. A reliability program that leans on only one of the three has a real blind spot for whichever failure mode that method isn't well suited to catching.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Five mechanisms explain the overwhelming majority of premature bearing failures — each with a distinct cause and fix.
  • ✓Lubrication and contamination together account for roughly 70% of premature failures, and both are largely preventable.
  • ✓VFD-driven motors need a specific answer for shaft currents — insulated bearings or a shaft grounding ring — that a standard bearing doesn't provide on its own.
  • ✓Vibration, ultrasound and oil analysis are complementary, not redundant — each catches different failure modes earliest.
