Load Estimation: The First Step in Electrical Design

Before a single cable is drawn or a panel is specified, every electrical project starts with one question: how much power will this building actually need? Getting that number right — early — is what load estimation is for.

How load is estimated

A quick per-floor method for an early-stage estimate

At concept stage, before any load schedule exists, total demand can be approximated from the building's geometry and a code-based density figure. The idea is simple: multiply the number of floors by the area of one floor, then by a kVA-per-square-metre figure pulled from code — and that gives a first-pass total load in kVA.

Worked example: residential + admin building

Total area: 600 m², made up of 1 basement, 2 admin floors, and 16 residential floors at 5 apartments per floor. Auxiliary loads: 3 elevators at 15 kW each, 3 plumbing motors at 17.5 HP (88% efficiency, 1 held as emergency spare), and 2 sweeping motors at 6.5 HP (87% efficiency, 1 held as emergency spare).

Key takeaways

  • ✓Accurate load estimation ensures smooth project execution and avoids delays.
  • ✓Helps in correct sizing of transformers, generators, cables, panels, and protection devices.
  • ✓Use code values (NEC) and apply diversity factors (0.6–0.7), since not all loads operate at the same time.
  • ✓Estimate early, plan smart, deliver reliable power.
