Pump & Fan Affinity Laws: Why Speed Changes Cube the Power

Slow a pump or fan down by 20% and flow drops 20% too — but power draw drops nearly 50%. That mismatch is the entire business case for VFD-driven pumps and fans.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Mechanical / Rotating Equipment
01
The three affinity laws relate a centrifugal pump or fan's flow, head (or pressure), and power to its rotational speed — valid for the same machine, same impeller/wheel diameter, same system.
02
Flow scales linearly with speed, head/pressure scales with the square of speed, and power scales with the cube of speed — that cube relationship is why small speed reductions save disproportionate energy.
03
A pump or fan running at 80% speed draws only about 51% of full-speed power, even though it's still delivering 80% of full-speed flow — the basis of every VFD energy-savings pitch on variable-flow systems.
04
The laws assume the system curve stays the same and efficiency doesn't change much with speed — both assumptions can break down, especially at very low speeds or on systems with a large static head component.

The three relationships

Q2/Q1 = N2/N1   |   H2/H1 = (N2/N1)²   |   P2/P1 = (N2/N1)³
Q = flow rate · H = head (pumps) or pressure (fans) · P = shaft power · N = rotational speed · subscripts 1/2 = the two operating speeds being compared

These three laws all fall out of the same underlying fluid-dynamics scaling for a centrifugal machine: as the impeller or wheel spins faster, the fluid it flings outward moves faster (flow, linear with speed), the kinetic energy it imparts rises with velocity squared (head, squared with speed), and the work needed to do both of those rises with the product of flow and head — giving power a cubic relationship with speed.

Why the cube law matters more than the other two

Speed (% of full)Flow (%)Head (%)Power (%)
100%100%100%100%
90%90%81%73%
80%80%64%51%
70%70%49%34%
50%50%25%13%

A modest 10% speed reduction only trims flow by 10% but cuts power by nearly 27% — this is the entire economic argument for variable-frequency drives on centrifugal pumps and fans serving variable loads, from HVAC supply fans to chilled-water circulation pumps. A throttling valve or damper, by contrast, wastes that energy as pressure drop instead of not drawing it in the first place.

Where the assumptions hold — and where they don't

Holds wellFully variable-speed systems with a mostly-friction (dynamic) system curve and no significant static head component — e.g. a closed chilled-water loop.
Gets fuzzySystems with a large static head (open cooling towers, elevation lift) — the affinity laws describe the pump curve shifting, not how the fixed system curve interacts with it, so real savings are lower than the pure cube law suggests.
Breaks downVery low speeds (commonly below ~30-40% depending on the machine) where pump/fan efficiency itself drops off, and the constant-efficiency assumption behind the laws no longer holds.

Practical checklist when applying the affinity laws

  • 1Confirm you're comparing the same machine at two speeds — the laws don't apply across different pump models or impeller trims without correction.
  • 2Check the motor's continuous torque/power rating at reduced speed if using a VFD below the drive's rated frequency — some motors need derating below their design cooling speed.
  • 3Separate static head from friction head in the system curve before projecting energy savings — static-head-dominated systems save meaningfully less than the cube law alone predicts.
  • 4Verify NPSH available doesn't become marginal at reduced speed on suction-sensitive pump applications — NPSH required also drops with speed, but not always fast enough to stay ahead of NPSH available margins at every point.
  • 5Use the affinity laws for estimating, not final selection — always verify the actual operating point against the manufacturer's pump or fan curve at the target speed.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Flow scales linearly, head/pressure scales with speed squared, and power scales with speed cubed — for the same machine and system.
  • ✓The cube relationship on power is why VFD speed control on centrifugal pumps and fans delivers outsized energy savings compared to throttling.
  • ✓Static-head-dominated systems and very low speeds are where the simple affinity-law projection starts to diverge from reality.
  • ✓Always confirm projected savings against the actual pump/fan curve and motor rating, not the affinity laws alone.
