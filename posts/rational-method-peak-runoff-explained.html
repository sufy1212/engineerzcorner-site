Rational Method: How Peak Stormwater Runoff Is Estimated

Q = C × i × A takes three numbers — but the rainfall intensity you use depends on the time of concentration, which depends on the drainage path. Get that link wrong and the peak flow is wrong.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Civil / Drainage
01
The Rational Method estimates peak runoff flow only — not a hydrograph, and not a runoff volume.
02
The rainfall intensity i is read from an IDF curve at a duration equal to the time of concentration, not at an arbitrary storm length.
03
A longer time of concentration means a lower design intensity, so a longer flow path lowers the peak flow estimate.
04
Mixed surfaces use an area-weighted composite C — paving and roofs dominate the result even when they are a minority of the area.

What the method assumes

The Rational Method treats a small catchment as if rain fell at a steady, uniform intensity over the whole area, long enough for runoff from the most remote point to reach the outlet. At that moment the entire area contributes and the flow peaks. Peak flow is then just the rainfall rate over the area, reduced by the fraction that actually runs off. Because of those assumptions it is normally limited to small catchments; local drainage manuals set their own size limits, so check the one that applies to your project.

Q  =  C × i × A
US units: Q in cfs, i in in/hr, A in acres (the unit conversion factor of about 1.008 is normally dropped). SI units: Q = C × i × A / 360, with Q in m³/s, i in mm/hr and A in hectares.

The three inputs

InputWhat it representsWhere it comes from
CRunoff coefficient — the fraction of rainfall that becomes peak runoffTables by surface type (roughly 0.1–0.3 for lawns, 0.7–0.95 for pavement and roofs), area-weighted for mixed cover
iRainfall intensity for the design stormIDF curve for the chosen return period, read at duration = time of concentration
AContributing drainage areaCatchment boundary from topography and drainage layout

Composite C for mixed surfaces

Ccomposite  =  Σ(Ck × Ak)  /  ΣAk
Illustrative: 2 acres of pavement (C = 0.90) and 3 acres of lawn (C = 0.25) give (1.8 + 0.75) / 5 = 0.51. Pavement is 40% of the area but contributes about 70% of the runoff-weighted total, which is why impervious cover dominates C.

Time of concentration ties it together

Time of concentration (tc) is the travel time for runoff from the hydraulically most remote point of the catchment to the outlet. It is usually built up from segments — sheet flow over the surface, shallow concentrated flow, then channel or pipe flow — and added together. The design intensity is read from the IDF curve at that duration. A longer, flatter flow path raises tc, which lowers i, which lowers the peak Q. Many agencies also set a minimum tc (commonly around 5–10 minutes) so tiny paved areas are not given an unrealistically high intensity.

Longer tcLower intensity from the IDF curve, so a lower peak flow for the same C and A.
Shorter tcHigher intensity, so a higher peak flow — steep, paved, well-connected catchments respond fastest.

Where it goes wrong

  • !Adding sub-catchment peaks. Peaks from different sub-areas usually do not occur at the same time, so summing their Q values overestimates the flow. Recompute with the combined area and the governing (longest) tc instead.
  • !Using the wrong storm. Intensity must come from the return period the project requires (for example a 10-year storm for a minor drain, longer for major systems) at duration = tc — not a round-number rainfall figure.
  • !Treating C as fixed. C can rise for rarer, more intense storms as soils saturate; some agencies apply a frequency adjustment factor to C for return periods beyond about 10 years, with C capped at 1.0.
  • !Expecting volumes. The method gives a peak rate only. Detention or storage sizing needs a hydrograph or volume method instead.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Q = C × i × A gives peak flow for small catchments; local drainage criteria set the applicability limits.
  • ✓Intensity is read from the IDF curve at the time of concentration for the required return period.
  • ✓Composite C is area-weighted, and impervious surfaces dominate it.
  • ✓Do not add sub-catchment peaks; recompute with the combined area and governing tc.
  • ✓Once Q is known, the pipe or channel is sized separately — for example with Manning's equation.
