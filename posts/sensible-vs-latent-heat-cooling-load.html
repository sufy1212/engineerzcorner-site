Sensible vs. Latent Heat: Why Cooling Load Isn't Just Temperature

A room can hit its setpoint temperature and still feel clammy and uncomfortable. That's what happens when a cooling load calculation only accounts for sensible heat and forgets the moisture half of the equation.

Engineerz CorneRReference · HVAC / Psychrometrics
01
Sensible heat changes air temperature; latent heat changes air moisture content.
02
A thermostat only measures the sensible half of the story.
03
Total cooling load is sensible load plus latent load, not sensible load alone.
04
Undersizing the latent portion is exactly what leaves a "cold but clammy" room.

Two different jobs, one coil

Every cooling coil is doing two jobs on the air passing through it at once. It's removing sensible heat — the heat that you'd feel as a temperature drop on a thermometer. And, if the air's moisture content is above the coil's dew point, it's also condensing water vapour out of the air, which removes latent heat — heat tied up in the state of the water itself, not its temperature.

A thermostat only reports the sensible side. That's exactly why a space can read the correct setpoint on the wall and still feel muggy: the sensible load was matched, but the latent load — the moisture — wasn't removed fast enough to keep up with what people, cooking, or outside air kept adding.

Sensible heat

Solar gain through glass, heat from lighting and equipment, heat conducted through walls and roof, and the dry portion of people's body heat. Drives dry-bulb temperature.

Latent heat

Moisture from people's breathing and perspiration, cooking, showers, and humid outside air brought in by ventilation or infiltration. Drives humidity, not temperature.

A coil and controls sized only against sensible load will hold temperature but let humidity drift upward — the room reads "cool" on a thermostat while still feeling damp.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Temperature and comfort are not the same measurement — humidity is doing half the work.
  • ✓High-occupancy and high-moisture spaces (gyms, restaurants, kitchens) skew heavily latent — size for it deliberately.
  • ✓A thermostat hitting setpoint is not proof the load calculation was right.
  • ✓Ventilation air brought in from outside almost always carries a real latent load — don't treat it as sensible-only.
