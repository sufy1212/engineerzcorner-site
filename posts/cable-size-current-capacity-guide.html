Cable Size & Current Capacity: A Practical Selection Guide

Picking a cable is really answering one question: how much current does it need to carry safely, continuously, without overheating? Everything else in a cable schedule flows from that.

Copper vs. aluminium at a glance

Copper is the better conductor, so a copper cable carries more current than an aluminium cable of the same cross-sectional area — which is why aluminium cable schedules almost always jump to a noticeably larger size to match the same load. Aluminium's advantages are weight and cost, not current density; the two materials aren't interchangeable size-for-size.

Selection checklist

  • 1Size the cable to the actual load current, with margin — not just what happens to be in stock.
  • 2Check voltage drop separately for long runs; a cable can pass on ampacity and still fail on voltage drop.
  • 3Remember copper and aluminium aren't interchangeable at the same size — match the table to the actual conductor material.
  • 4Select the protective device (MCB/MCCB) rating to match the cable size and the load, not the other way around.
  • 5If a cable runs hot in service, treat it as a signal to re-check load, cable size, and every termination — not just the cable itself.
