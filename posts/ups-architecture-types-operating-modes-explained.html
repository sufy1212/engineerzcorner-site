UPS Architecture, Types & Operating Modes Explained

A UPS keeps critical loads alive when the mains can't. Here's how an online double-conversion UPS is actually built, what each block does, the three ways it can be running at any moment, and how it compares to offline and line-interactive types.

What a UPS does

A UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) is an electrical system that provides emergency power to a load when the input power source fails. Beyond just backup, it protects against voltage fluctuations, sags, surges and noise, ensures clean and stable output, enables safe shutdown of equipment when runtime finally runs out, and generally improves power quality and reliability for whatever it's feeding.

Main components

ComponentFunction
Rectifier / ChargerConverts AC input to DC to charge the batteries and power the inverter
Battery BankStores energy and provides DC power during a mains failure
InverterConverts DC power (from mains via the rectifier, or from batteries) back to clean AC for the load
Static SwitchAutomatically transfers the load between bypass and inverter without interruption
BypassProvides power directly from mains in case of overload or a UPS fault
Control SystemMonitors, controls and protects the UPS and all its components

Online double-conversion architecture

In an online UPS, every watt reaching the load has already been converted twice: mains AC → DC through the rectifier/charger, then DC → AC again through the inverter. The battery bank sits on that same DC link, floating and topping up under normal conditions. Because the load is always riding on the inverter's output rather than being switched onto it after a failure, there's zero transfer time when the mains actually drops — the inverter just keeps running, now drawing from the battery instead of the rectifier.

Normal: Mains → Rectifier → Inverter → Load (battery charging). Mains failure: Battery → Inverter → Load (no interruption). Overload/fault: Mains → Bypass → Load.

Operating modes

ModeConditionPower path
Normal modeMains availableMains → Rectifier → Inverter → Load, battery charging in parallel
Battery modeMains failureBattery → Inverter → Load, battery discharging
Bypass modeUPS overload or internal faultMains → Static switch/bypass → Load directly, UPS electronics out of the path
The static switch is what makes bypass mode automatic and (in a healthy unit) uninterrupted — it senses the fault or overload condition and transfers the load in milliseconds.

Battery information

  • ✓Common chemistries: VRLA, AGM, GEL, Li-ion.
  • ✓The battery bank provides DC power during outages, sized for a target runtime.
  • ✓Typical backup time ranges from minutes to several hours, depending on load and battery size.
  • ✓Regular maintenance and load testing is essential — a battery's nameplate capacity is not what a degraded string will actually deliver.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Rectifier/charger, battery bank, inverter, static switch and bypass are the five building blocks of an online UPS.
  • ✓Online double-conversion gives zero transfer time because the load never has to be switched onto the inverter — it's already there.
  • ✓Offline and line-interactive UPS are cheaper and simpler but accept a short transfer time on failure.
  • ✓Regular inspection, battery health checks, alarm testing and firmware upkeep are what keep a UPS's protection promises real.
