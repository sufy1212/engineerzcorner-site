Soil Bearing Capacity: Why It Decides Your Footing Size, Not Just Column Load

Two footings carrying the identical column load can end up completely different sizes. The column tells you the load; the soil underneath tells you how much area is needed to carry it safely.

Footing size is governed by whichever is more restrictive: structural load, or soil bearing capacity.
02
Allowable bearing capacity is the safe design value — always below the soil's ultimate failure capacity.

The same column load needs very different footing areas on clay versus dense gravel.

The load has to go somewhere

A column carries its load down to a footing, and the footing spreads that load out over an area of soil wide enough that the pressure on the soil stays within what it can safely take. That safe pressure limit is the soil's allowable bearing capacity — not its absolute breaking point, but a reduced value with a safety factor already built in against settlement and shear failure.

This is why footing sizing is a two-input problem, not a one-input problem. The column load sets how much force needs to be carried. The soil's bearing capacity sets how much area is required to spread that force out safely. Get the second number wrong and even a perfectly designed column sits on a footing that's simply too small for the ground beneath it.

A heavier column needs more area at a fixed bearing capacity; a weaker soil needs more area at a fixed load. Either input moving in the wrong direction grows the footing.

Typical allowable bearing capacity by soil type

Soil typeTypical allowable bearing capacity
Soft clay50–100 kPa
Stiff clay100–200 kPa
Loose sand100–150 kPa
Dense sand200–300 kPa
Gravel300–600 kPa
Soft rock600–1000 kPa
Sound rock1000 kPa and above
These ranges are for early-stage sizing only. Actual allowable bearing capacity depends on standard penetration values, groundwater level, footing depth and width, and local code requirements — always confirmed by a geotechnical investigation before final design.

What moves the number

FactorTypical effect
Groundwater table close to footing levelReduces allowable bearing capacity
Greater footing embedment depthGenerally increases allowable bearing capacity
Wider footing on loose/granular soilIncreases allowable bearing capacity
Soft or organic soil layers below founding levelReduces allowable bearing capacity, risks settlement
Adjacent footings placed too close togetherOverlapping stress zones can reduce effective capacity

Key takeaways

  • ✓Footing size is a soil question as much as a structural one — both inputs have to be checked.
  • ✓Allowable bearing capacity already carries a safety margin; it isn't the soil's failure point.
  • ✓Table values are for early estimates — a site-specific geotechnical report governs final design.
  • ✓Groundwater level and embedment depth can shift the allowable value significantly from the generic range.
