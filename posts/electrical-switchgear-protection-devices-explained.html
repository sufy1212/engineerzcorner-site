MCB, MCCB, ACB, RCCB & More: Electrical Switchgear & Protection Devices Explained

Sixteen devices you'll find in almost every panel and switchboard — breakers, earth-leakage protection, switching gear and protection accessories — what each one actually does, and how to tell them apart at a glance.

Engineerz CorneRReference · LV/MV Switchgear
01
MCB, MCCB, ACB and VCB are all circuit breakers — the difference between them is mainly current rating and breaking capacity, from small MCBs (≤125 A) up to ACBs (≤6300 A+) for main incomers.
02
RCCB, RCBO and ELCB all target earth-leakage/shock protection, not overload or short-circuit — an RCCB alone still needs an upstream MCB or fuse for that.
03
Isolators, changeover switches and contactors switch circuits on and off but aren't protective devices — they're not rated to break fault current.
04
Relays, fuses, SPDs and busbars round out the panel — sensing, backup protection, surge diversion and current distribution, respectively.

Circuit breakers: MCB, MCCB, ACB, VCB & SF6

All five interrupt current automatically on overload or fault — what separates them is current rating, breaking capacity and the medium used to quench the arc.

MCB
Miniature Circuit Breaker
Fixed thermal-magnetic trip, up to 125 A, ~6–10 kA breaking capacity. The default choice for final circuits — lighting, sockets, small appliances — in domestic and light commercial distribution boards.
MCCB
Molded Case Circuit Breaker
Adjustable thermal-magnetic or electronic trip, 16 A–2500 A, 10–85 kA breaking capacity. Used for distribution boards, sub-mains and larger feeders where the setting needs to be tunable.
ACB
Air Circuit Breaker
Draw-out or fixed frame, 630 A–6300 A+, arc interrupted in open air. The standard main incomer and bus-tie breaker in LV switchboards, with full electronic protection relays built in.
VCB
Vacuum Circuit Breaker
Contacts open inside a vacuum bottle, which quenches the arc almost instantly. The standard medium-voltage breaker (up to ~36 kV) for HV switchgear, ring main units and MV motor starters.
SF6 CB
Sulphur Hexafluoride Circuit Breaker
Uses SF6 gas, an excellent dielectric and arc-quenching medium, to interrupt current. Used for higher-voltage switchgear and outdoor substation breakers where VCBs reach their practical voltage limit.

Earth-leakage & combined protection: RCCB, RCBO, ELCB

These sense a current imbalance between line and neutral — the signature of current leaking to earth, often through a person — and trip fast enough to prevent electric shock.

RCCB / RCD
Residual Current (Circuit Breaker) Device
Trips on earth leakage (typically 30 mA for shock protection) but has no overload or short-circuit protection of its own — always paired with an upstream MCB or fuse.
RCBO
Residual Current Breaker with Overcurrent
An RCCB and MCB combined in one device — earth-leakage and overload/short-circuit protection on a single circuit, without needing a separate upstream breaker.
ELCB
Earth Leakage Circuit Breaker
Legacy voltage-operated device that sensed voltage on the earth conductor rather than current imbalance. Largely superseded by current-operated RCCBs, which are more reliable and don't depend on earth continuity.
RCCB vs RCBOAn RCCB alone protects against shock but not overload — it must sit behind an MCB. An RCBO folds both functions into one DIN-rail device, at higher cost per way but less panel space.
Why ELCB fell out of favorA voltage-operated ELCB only works if the earth conductor is intact and correctly connected — break that path and protection silently disappears. Current-operated RCCBs don't have this weakness.

Switching & isolation: isolators, changeover switches, contactors

These control whether a circuit is live or dead — none of them are rated to interrupt fault current, so they're always used alongside a protective device, not instead of one.

Isolator / Disconnector
Off-Load Isolating Switch
Provides a visible, physical air gap for safe maintenance isolation. Rated for making/breaking normal load current only — never opened under fault or, on many designs, even under full load.
Changeover Switch / ATS
Automatic Transfer Switch
Switches a load between two sources — mains and standby generator, most commonly — either manually or automatically on loss of the primary supply, with mechanical or electrical interlocking to prevent both sources connecting together.
Contactor
Electromagnetically-Operated Switch
A coil pulls the contacts closed when energized, dropping out when de-energized — built for frequent switching (motor starting, lighting contactors, HVAC control) rather than fault interruption.

Protection & panel accessories: relays, fuses, SPDs, overload relays, busbars

The supporting cast — sensing, backup protection, surge diversion and current distribution that make the breakers and switches above actually work as a coordinated system.

Protection Relay
Numerical / Electromechanical Relay
Monitors current, voltage or frequency and issues a trip signal to a breaker's shunt trip coil when a fault condition is detected — the "brain" behind ACBs and MV breakers, running overcurrent, earth-fault and differential functions.
HRC Fuse
High Rupturing Capacity Fuse
A sacrificial element that melts to clear fault current, typically faster than a breaker at high fault levels. Common as backup protection and in fuse-switch combinations on distribution boards and MV ring main units.
SPD
Surge Protection Device
Diverts lightning and switching transient overvoltages to earth before they reach downstream equipment. Installed at the panel incomer (Type 1/2) and sometimes again near sensitive loads (Type 3).
Thermal Overload Relay
Bimetallic Overload Relay
Paired with a contactor to protect motors — a bimetal strip bends as sustained overcurrent heats it, opening an auxiliary contact that drops the contactor out before winding insulation is damaged.
Busbar / Bus Duct
Rigid Current-Carrying Conductor
Copper or aluminium bars distributing current from the incomer to outgoing ways inside a panel, or a busduct running current between floors/switchboards — sized on both continuous ampacity and short-circuit withstand.

Key takeaways

  • ✓MCB → MCCB → ACB is mostly a current-rating and breaking-capacity progression; VCB and SF6 CB take over once you cross into medium/high voltage.
  • ✓An RCCB protects against shock, not overload — it always needs an upstream MCB or fuse, unless you use a combined RCBO instead.
  • ✓Isolators, changeover switches and contactors switch circuits but are not fault-rated — never substitute one for a breaker.
  • ✓Relays, fuses, SPDs, thermal overloads and busbars are the supporting devices that make the breakers above work as a coordinated protection scheme, not standalone gadgets.
