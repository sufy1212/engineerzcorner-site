Chilled Water HVAC Systems: How the Loop Actually Works

A chiller doesn't cool a room directly — it cools water, which cools air, which cools the room. Here's the full loop, from occupied zone to atmosphere and back.

Engineerz CorneRReference · HVAC / Central Plant
01
Chilled water is really just a transport medium — it carries heat away from zones and hands it off to the chiller.
02
Two separate water loops are involved: chilled water (to the zones) and condenser water (to the cooling tower).
03
The cooling tower doesn't cool the building — it's where the chiller's rejected heat finally leaves the system for good.
04
Each zone's own mixing box and dampers let multiple spaces share one central plant while still being controlled independently.

Two loops, not one

It's easy to look at a chilled water system and assume there's a single loop of water doing all the work. There are actually two, and keeping them straight is the key to understanding the whole schematic. The chilled water loop runs between the chiller's evaporator and the air handling unit's cooling coil — this is the water that actually absorbs heat from the air passing through the coil. The condenser water loop runs between the chiller's condenser and the cooling tower — this is a completely separate circuit whose only job is carrying the heat the chiller collected out to the cooling tower, where it's finally rejected to the atmosphere.

Confusing the two is a common source of misunderstanding the diagram: chilled water never goes anywhere near the cooling tower, and condenser water never goes anywhere near the air handling unit. They meet only inside the chiller itself, where the refrigeration cycle transfers heat from one loop to the other.

1Water-Cooled Chiller

  • Produces chilled water via its evaporator, using the refrigeration cycle
  • Rejects the heat it collects into the condenser water loop
  • The one component where both water loops actually meet

2Air Handling Unit (AHU)

  • Pre-filter removes coarse particulate before air reaches the coil
  • Cooling coil is where chilled water actually absorbs heat from the airstream
  • Centrifugal fan pushes the now-cooled, dehumidified air out to the zones

3Cooling Tower

  • Sprays warm condenser water over fill media to maximize evaporative surface area
  • A fan draws air through, evaporating a small fraction of the water to reject heat
  • Cooled condenser water returns to the chiller to absorb heat again

4Chilled & Condenser Water Pumps

  • CHW pump circulates chilled water between chiller and AHU coils
  • CWR pump circulates condenser water between chiller and cooling tower
  • Each loop needs its own dedicated pump — they aren't shared

5Boiler (Optional Heating Source)

  • Provides hot water for the same mixing boxes when heating, not cooling, is needed
  • Not part of the chilled water loop itself — a separate heating source sharing the AHU

How the cycle actually runs

1The chiller produces chilled water and sends it to the AHU's cooling coil.
2As return air and fresh air mix and pass through the coil, chilled water absorbs heat from it.
3The now-warmed chilled water returns to the chiller through the return line.
4The chiller rejects that collected heat into the condenser water loop and on to the cooling tower.
5The cooling tower evaporates a small amount of water to reject the heat to atmosphere, and the cycle repeats continuously.

What each zone controls independently

A single AHU commonly serves several zones at once, and each zone gets its own mixing box, volume air damper (VA), and humidity sensor (H), so its supply air (SA) and return air (RA) can be balanced independently of every other zone on the same air handler. This is why one central plant can serve spaces with genuinely different loads and setpoints without each zone fighting the others for capacity — the shared resource is the chilled water and the air handling equipment; the zone-level control is what actually tailors delivery to each space.

Why this architecture is common

Efficiency
Precise Control
Air Quality

Splitting the system into a chilled water loop and a separate condenser water loop, each with its own dedicated pump, is what lets one central chiller plant efficiently serve multiple air handlers and many zones at once — rather than needing a separate, smaller refrigeration system at every zone. It also isolates maintenance: the cooling tower and condenser loop can be serviced without shutting down the chilled water side, and vice versa, as long as the chiller itself stays online.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Chilled water and condenser water are two separate loops that only interact inside the chiller itself.
  • ✓The cooling coil in the AHU is where heat actually leaves the airstream, not the cooling tower.
  • ✓The cooling tower's job is purely to reject heat to atmosphere via evaporation — it plays no role in conditioning the air.
  • ✓Zone-level dampers and sensors let one shared central plant serve multiple independently controlled spaces.
