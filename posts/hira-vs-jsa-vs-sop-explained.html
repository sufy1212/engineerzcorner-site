HIRA vs. JSA vs. SOP: Which Safety Document Do You Actually Need?

Same industrial safety toolkit, three different questions. HIRA maps hazards across the whole site before anything's scheduled. JSA breaks one specific job into safe steps right before it starts. SOP is the standing method for doing it — every single time.

Three documents, three different scopes

HIRA, JSA and SOP show up on the same industrial safety training slide so often that they start to blur together — three acronyms, all clearly about "doing the job safely." But they answer different questions, at different points in time, at different scopes. Mixing them up isn't just a paperwork mistake: a JSA can't substitute for a site-wide HIRA, and an SOP written before the hazards are even identified is a procedure built on guesses. The table below is the quick way to keep them straight.

1. HIRA — mapping hazards across the whole workplace Planning stage

HIRA happens before any specific job is scheduled. It surveys the whole facility — every area, every process — to identify what could go wrong and score how likely and how severe each hazard is, usually as a probability × severity risk rating. The output is a Risk Assessment Report that becomes the reference document everything else draws from: it's what a JSA checks against for a specific job, and what an SOP is designed around once the method is standardized.

Workplace scenarioHazard identified
Working at HeightFall hazard

2. JSA — planning one job before it starts Before job

A JSA is narrower and more immediate than a HIRA — it's done for one specific job, right before that job starts, not for the site in general. It breaks the job into its individual steps and specifies the safe method and PPE for each step, drawing on the hazards the HIRA already flagged for that area. Where a HIRA might say "welding areas carry a fire hazard," a JSA for today's welding job says exactly what that crew needs before they strike an arc.

JobSafe method
Scaffold WorkUse harness
Welding JobHelmet & gloves
Confined SpaceGas test before entry
Lifting OperationDedicated signalman

3. SOP — the standard method, used every time Daily use

An SOP isn't tied to one job or one day — it's the standing method for a task, written once and followed every time that task recurs. It's less about identifying risk (that's HIRA's job) and more about locking in the sequence that keeps the task safe and consistent, so the outcome doesn't depend on who's doing it or how careful they happen to be feeling that day.

ProcedureStandard sequence
Fire Extinguisher UsePull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep
Welding ProcedurePre-check → Weld → Post-check
Machine OperationStandard startup / shutdown sequence
Emergency ResponseEvacuation routes & assembly points

4. How they chain together on one job

Take welding as a running example — the same activity shows up at all three levels, at three different points in time, and none of the three stands in for the other two.

Welding, tracked through HIRA → JSA → SOP

HIRA — planning stageWelding area flagged site-wide as a fire hazard
JSA — before this jobToday's weld: helmet, gloves, fire watch, extinguisher staged
SOP — every timeStandard sequence: pre-check → weld → post-check

If any one of the three is missing, the gap shows up predictably: no HIRA means the fire risk was never formally identified in the first place; no JSA means today's crew never confirmed the specific precautions for this weld; no SOP means the outcome depends entirely on whoever's holding the torch that day.

Key takeaways

  • ✓HIRA operates at the planning stage across the whole workplace — it identifies hazards and assesses risk before any specific job exists.
  • ✓JSA is job-specific and done immediately before that job starts, breaking it into steps and the safe method for each.
  • ✓SOP is the standing, repeatable method for a task — it's followed every time, not written and referenced only once.
  • ✓They chain in order — HIRA finds the hazards, JSA plans one job around them, SOP locks in the daily method — and one can't substitute for the other two.
