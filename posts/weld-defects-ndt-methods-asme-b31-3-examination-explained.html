Weld Defects and NDT Methods Explained: What Each Test Finds and How Much ASME B31.3 Requires

A weld hides its flaws inside the metal. Here are the six common defects drawn on a cross-section, what VT, PT, MT, UT and RT can each see, and how much examination ASME B31.3 asks for.

A pressure pipe weld hides most of its quality inside the metal. After the welder finishes, the only way to know the joint is sound is to look at it, and for the inside you need instruments. This post draws the defects, shows what each testing method can and cannot see, and explains how much testing a piping code like ASME B31.3 asks for.

What goes wrong inside a weld

Most weld defects fall into a few families: gas (porosity), foreign material (slag), poor bonding (lack of fusion and penetration), shape faults (undercut) and cracks. The diagram marks all six on one cross-section.

Six places a butt weld can go wrong

Cross-section of a single-V butt weld. Numbers match the list below.

5

6

Five ways to examine a weld

The common methods are visual (VT), liquid penetrant (PT), magnetic particle (MT), ultrasonic (UT) and radiographic (RT). They differ mainly in what they can reach.

How deep does each method see?

VT, PT and MT are surface methods. UT and RT examine the full thickness.

SURFACE
VTPTMT
Visual, dye penetrant and magnetic particle find what is open to the surface. MT also finds flaws just under it.
NEAR SURFACE
MT
Magnetic particle works only on ferromagnetic steel, such as carbon steel, and not on austenitic stainless.
FULL THICKNESS
UTRT
Ultrasonic uses sound waves. Radiography uses X-rays or gamma rays and leaves a permanent image. Both are called volumetric methods.
  • VT is the first and cheapest check, and it is the base of every inspection plan.
  • PT applies a liquid that seeps into surface cracks and a developer that draws it out, so it shows surface-breaking flaws in non-porous material.
  • MT magnetises the steel and uses iron powder that gathers over flaws at or just under the surface.
  • UT sends sound through the weld and reads the echoes, and it needs a skilled operator and a written procedure.
  • RT exposes film or a digital detector to radiation and gives a permanent record that anyone can review later.

RT finds volumetric flaws such as porosity and slag well. UT is stronger on planar flaws such as cracks and lack of fusion, which depend on direction. Fillet, socket and branch welds are usually checked by MT or PT because they do not suit radiography.

How much does the code require?

ASME B31.3 sets the amount of examination by fluid service. The more dangerous the service, the larger the share of welds that are examined by RT or UT. Category D, for low-hazard fluids, is limited to visual examination.

How much of the weld work is examined?

ASME B31.3 volumetric examination of circumferential butt welds, by fluid service. All welds also get visual examination, except Category D.

Normal fluidrandom RT or UT5%
Elevated temp.as normal5%
Category Mrandom RT or UT20%
Severe cyclicall girth welds100%

Normal and Elevated Temperature service: at least 5% by random RT or UT. Category M: at least 20%. Severe Cyclic: 100% RT, or UT if the design specifies it. Always use the edition and the engineering design your project cites.

Worked example. A project has 200 circumferential butt welds. In Normal fluid service, 5% means 10 welds examined by random RT or UT, and the sample has to include some welds from every welder used. If the same line were Category M at 20%, 40 welds are examined. Under Severe Cyclic conditions, all 200 are examined. The cost and schedule difference between these is large, so the fluid service has to be settled early.

When a sample fails

A random examination is a sample, so a failure raises a question about the welds that were not tested. The code answers it with progressive sampling, outlined below.

1
A random weld fails. The weld that was picked for examination has a rejectable defect.
2
Examine two more of the same kind. The extra samples come from the same welder or operator and the same type of joint.
3
Both pass. Repair or replace the failed weld, re-examine it, and accept the group it represents.
4
Either one fails. Examine two further samples for each failure. If any of those fail, all the items the sampling represents are fully examined, or repaired or replaced and re-examined.

An outline only. The exact rules are in the code section on progressive sampling.

Making inspection work in practice

  • Plan it with the welding. Set the examination extent, methods and acceptance criteria in the project specification before fabrication starts.
  • Qualify people and procedures. Each method needs a qualified examiner and a written procedure, and each welder needs a qualified procedure and record.
  • Track by welder and joint. A weld map and log let you trace any failure back to a welder, a procedure and a date.
  • Do not replace volumetric with surface. A surface method cannot stand in for a required RT or UT, because it cannot see inside.
  • Pressure test is separate. NDT shows what is in the weld, and the leak test shows that the joint holds. See hydrostatic and pneumatic testing.

Welding quality connects to the wall thickness the weld has to match, covered in pipe wall thickness to ASME B31.3, and to the protection applied afterwards, as in protective coating systems and corrosion basics. Welded joints also see movement, so read pipe thermal expansion.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Know the six defect families: porosity, slag, lack of fusion, incomplete penetration, cracks and undercut.
  • ✓Match the method to the defect, since surface methods cannot see inside and MT needs ferromagnetic steel.
  • ✓Settle the fluid service early, because it sets the extent of RT or UT and the cost.
  • ✓Keep records by welder and joint so any failure can be traced and sampled properly.
