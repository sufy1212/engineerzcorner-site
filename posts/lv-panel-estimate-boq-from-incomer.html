LV Panel Estimating: Why a Budgetary BOQ Starts With the Incomer, Not the Steel

The panel's physical footprint isn't an independent guess — it's a consequence of the electrical sizing happening inside it.

Why two inputs are enough for a first pass

At the concept or budgetary stage of a project, a full panel design isn't available yet — but a surprisingly useful estimate can be built from just two numbers most people already know early: the main incomer rating, and how many outgoing ways the panel needs to feed. Everything else — busbar size, enclosure footprint, sheet metal area, even a rough cost — can be derived from those two using standard assumptions, rather than guessed independently.

Each step down that chain reuses the same sizing logic covered on its own calculator — the busbar sizing is the busbar-and-enclosure check, run automatically from the incomer figure instead of entered separately. That's what makes this an estimate tool rather than a duplicate of the standalone sizing sheets: it links the electrical sizing straight through to a physical footprint and a placeholder cost, with every intermediate figure editable under Advanced if you want to override an assumption.

What a budgetary total is — and isn't — good for

A total that appears the moment you enter the incomer rating is genuinely useful for early-stage comparisons: rough-order-of-magnitude budgeting, comparing panel configuration options, or sanity-checking a vendor quote that seems out of line. It is not a substitute for a real quotation once switchgear selection, form of separation, breaking capacity and specific brand pricing are locked in — those factors can move the real cost meaningfully in either direction from a placeholder-rate estimate.

Key takeaways

  • ✓A useful budgetary panel estimate needs only two real inputs: incomer rating and outgoing way count.
  • ✓Busbar size, enclosure footprint and sheet metal area all cascade from those two inputs using standard sizing logic, not independent guesses.
  • ✓The busbar sizing step here reuses the same current-density method as a standalone busbar-and-enclosure calculation.
  • ✓Treat the total as a rough order-of-magnitude figure for early decisions — confirm against a real vendor quote once switchgear is selected.
