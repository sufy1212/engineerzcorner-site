HVAC & Plumbing Default Design Values: The Quick Reference

The velocities, pressure drops, temperatures and ACH figures that show up in almost every design, gathered in one place for a fast first-pass sizing check — not a substitute for code and manufacturer data.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Design Defaults
01
These are common defaults used at concept and first-pass design — the actual number for a project still has to be checked against ASHRAE, SMACNA, CIBSE, local code, and the manufacturer's data.
02
Velocity ranges exist to balance two failure modes: too low wastes duct/pipe size and money, too high creates noise, erosion, or excessive pressure drop.
03
ACH and fresh air rates are occupancy- and use-driven — a kitchen or toilet needs far more air changes than an office for the same reason: odour and contaminant removal, not just comfort.
04
Plumbing follows the same logic as HVAC: minimum velocity keeps drains self-cleaning, maximum velocity avoids noise and water hammer.

Why these ranges exist, not just what they are

Every range above is a balance between two opposing costs. Push velocity too low and the duct or pipe has to be oversized to move the same flow — more material, more space, more cost, and for drainage specifically, not enough velocity to keep solids moving, which leads to blockages. Push it too high and the system gets noisy, erosion and water hammer risk climb, and pressure drop (and therefore fan or pump energy) rises sharply, since pressure loss scales with roughly the square of velocity. The "default" ranges in the tables above sit in the middle of that trade-off for typical applications — which is also exactly why an unusual application (very long runs, unusual noise sensitivity, corrosive fluid) can legitimately need a value outside them.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Treat every value here as a starting point for concept design, then verify against ASHRAE, SMACNA, CIBSE, local code, and manufacturer data for the actual project.
  • ✓Velocity ranges balance sizing cost against noise, erosion, and pressure drop — not arbitrary numbers.
  • ✓ACH and fresh air requirements scale with contaminant load and occupancy, which is why kitchens and toilets sit far above offices.
  • ✓Plumbing drainage slope decreases as pipe diameter increases, to keep velocity — not just flow — adequate for self-cleaning.
