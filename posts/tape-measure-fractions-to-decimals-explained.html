Fractions to Decimals Explained

A tape measure speaks in sixteenths, but drawings, spreadsheets and CNC software want decimals. Moving between them is just division, until rounding, repeating decimals and millimetres get involved.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Measurement
01
A fraction is a division: divide the numerator by the denominator. 5/16" is 5 ÷ 16 = 0.3125".
02
One inch is exactly 25.4 mm, so any inch fraction converts to millimetres by multiplying its decimal by 25.4.
03
Rounding a decimal to the nearest 1/16" can be off by up to 1/32" (0.79 mm). Use 1/32" or 1/64" when that is too coarse.
04
Tape fractions (denominators that are powers of 2) always end cleanly. Feet and thirds do not, so convert once and round last.

Fraction to decimal

Decimal inches  =  Whole inches  +  Numerator  /  Denominator
A mixed number like 7 3/8" is 7 + 3 ÷ 8 = 7.375".
Decimal feet  =  Feet  +  Inches  /  12
Convert the fraction to decimal inches first, then divide the inches by 12.

Worked example: 12 ft 7 3/8 in. The inches are 7 + 0.375 = 7.375, so decimal feet = 12 + 7.375 / 12 = 12.6146 ft. In inches that is 12 × 12 + 7.375 = 151.375 in, and in millimetres 151.375 × 25.4 = 3844.925 mm.

Each step on a standard tape is 1/16" = 0.0625" = 1.5875 mm. Reduce the fraction before looking it up: 4/16" is 1/4" and 6/16" is 3/8".

Decimal to nearest fraction

Numerator  =  round( Decimal  ×  Denominator )
Choose the denominator (16, 32 or 64), multiply, round to a whole number, put it over the denominator and reduce.

The most the nearest-fraction answer can be off is half a step: 1/32" (0.03125", 0.79 mm) at sixteenths, 1/64" (0.0156") at thirty-seconds, and 1/128" (0.0078") at sixty-fourths.

Decimal 0.400" has no exact tape fraction. The closest sixteenth, 3/8", is 0.025" (0.64 mm) short, while 13/32" is within 0.0063". Pick the step that suits the tolerance of the job, not the one that is easiest to read.

Why some conversions never end

A fraction in lowest terms gives a terminating decimal only if its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5. Every tape denominator (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64) is a power of 2, so tape fractions always terminate: 1/64 is exactly 0.015625. Thirds and twelfths do not, because they contain a factor of 3.

Terminates3/8 = 0.375 · 5/16 = 0.3125 · 1/5 = 0.2 · 7/20 = 0.35
Repeats1/3 = 0.333… · 7/12 = 0.58333… · 1/7 = 0.142857142857…

That is why decimal feet can look messy: dividing inches by 12 introduces a factor of 3. Keep the dimension in inches, or as an exact fraction, until the final step, and round once.

Rounding, tolerance and stacking

Rounding every dimension to the nearest 1/16" introduces up to 1/32" of error per dimension. The errors can add: ten dimensions, each rounded, could be out by as much as 10 × 1/32" = 0.3125" over the total run in the worst case. That is fine for framing and takeoffs, but not for machined or fabricated parts, where a drawing tolerance (for example ±0.005") is far tighter than a sixteenth. Work in decimals, and round only the final reported figure.

Mistakes that catch people out

MistakeWhat goes wrong
Reading 5/16" as 5.16 or 0.516The fraction is 5 divided by 16, which is 0.3125
Confusing 3/8" and 3/16"3/8" is 0.375" and 3/16" is 0.1875", half as much
Rounding decimal feet earlyA value like 0.6146 ft re-converts to a slightly different number of inches
Adding rounded sixteenthsErrors accumulate across a run; add the decimals instead
Using 1/16" resolution for machiningIt is much coarser than usual machining tolerances
Mixing 25.4 with a rounded 25Using 25 mm per inch understates millimetres by about 1.6%

Key takeaways

  • ✓Fraction to decimal is division: 7 3/8" = 7.375", and feet = inches ÷ 12.
  • ✓1" = 25.4 mm exactly, so 7/16" = 0.4375" = 11.1125 mm.
  • ✓Nearest fraction = round(decimal × denominator) over the denominator, with an error of up to half a step.
  • ✓Tape fractions terminate; thirds and twelfths repeat, so round once at the end.
  • ✓Choose the resolution (1/16, 1/32 or 1/64) from the tolerance of the work.
