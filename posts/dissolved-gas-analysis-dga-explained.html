Dissolved Gas Analysis (DGA) Explained

Power transformer oil is a running record of everything stressing the insulation. DGA reads the dissolved gases in that oil — the fingerprint, the test workflow, and why the pattern matters far more than any single number.

The transformer's blood test

Mineral oil inside a power transformer does two jobs at once — it insulates and it cools. Under normal service it slowly breaks down, and under abnormal electrical or thermal stress it breaks down faster, releasing characteristic gases that dissolve into the oil itself. Dissolved Gas Analysis samples that oil and measures which gases are present and in what concentration. The pattern — not any one gas alone — can reveal an incipient fault (electrical, thermal, or cellulose-related) long before it progresses to a failure that protection relays would have to catch.

Field checklist & reference framework

Sample quality = data quality
  • ✓Flush the sampling point; exclude every air bubble.
  • ✓Use a clean, gas-tight container; seal immediately.
  • ✓Record load, oil temperature, date/time and location.
  • ✓Identify main tank vs. LTC; follow site safety procedures.
Reference standards
  • IEC 60475:2022 — insulating-liquid sampling
  • IEC 60567:2023 — gas extraction and analysis
  • IEC 60599:2022 — DGA interpretation guidance
  • IEEE C57.104-2019 — mineral-oil transformers
Scope: mineral-oil transformers. Use fluid-specific guidance for ester or silicone liquids — gas solubility and interpretation thresholds differ from mineral oil.

Key takeaways

  • ✓DGA measures gases dissolved in insulating oil, released by electrical, thermal and cellulose-related stress inside the transformer.
  • ✓Nine key gases each lean toward a stress type, but the associations are indicative — a full diagnosis needs ratios and context, not one gas read alone.
  • ✓Trend, generation rate, pattern and operating context are what turn a lab result into a usable diagnosis — there's no single pass/fail ppm value.
  • ✓A gas-tight, air-free sample with accurate load and temperature records is the foundation every interpretation method depends on.
  • ✓Follow IEC 60475/60567/60599 or IEEE C57.104 as applicable, and confirm findings against other tests before acting on a DGA result.
