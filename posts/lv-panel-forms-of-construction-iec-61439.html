IEC 61439 Forms of Construction: Form 1 to Form 4 Explained

The Form number on an LV switchboard datasheet isn't a badge of quality — it's a specific statement about which internal barriers separate busbars, functional units and terminals from each other.

Engineerz CorneRReference · IEC 61439-1/2
01
Forms of construction describe internal separation inside an assembly — between busbars, functional units, and terminals — not the assembly's overall quality or rating.
02
Four base forms exist, Form 1 through Form 4, with separation increasing at each step and Forms 2–4 splitting further into lettered and typed sub-variants.
03
A higher Form generally means better operator safety, easier fault isolation, and better continuity of supply to unaffected sections — at the cost of a larger, more expensive enclosure.

What a "Form of Construction" actually is

IEC 61439 defines Forms of construction to describe how the inside of a low-voltage switchboard is physically subdivided — specifically, whether the busbars, the functional units (breakers, contactors, and the devices they control), and their outgoing terminals sit in shared space or behind their own dedicated barriers. The point isn't decoration or convention: separation limits how far a fault or an arc inside one compartment can spread, and it decides how much of the board has to be de-energized before someone can safely work on one circuit.

The Form number itself says nothing about a switchboard's current rating, short-circuit withstand, or ingress protection — those are separate ratings entirely. It answers one specific question: if something goes wrong in one part of this board, or someone needs to work on one part of it live, how much of the rest of the board stays isolated and protected?

The four forms, step by step

F1Basic
Form 1 — No internal separationBusbars, functional units and terminals all share the same compartment. The simplest and least expensive construction, and the least safe to work on live — touching any part exposes the whole assembly.
F2Better
Form 2 — Busbars separated from functional units2a: terminals still share space with the busbars. 2b: terminals are separated from the busbars too, either by insulated covering (Type 1) or by a solid metal/plastic partition (Type 2).
F3Good
Form 3 — Functional units separated from each otherEverything Form 2 provides, plus each functional unit is now isolated from its neighbours. 3a: terminals aren't separated from the busbars. 3b: they are, again by insulated covering (Type 1) or a partition (Type 2).
F4Best
Form 4 — Terminals separated from functional units too4a: a unit's terminals share its own compartment. 4b: terminals get their own separate compartment entirely. Seven further Types (1–7) refine exactly how busbars are separated, how cables gland in, and how terminals are insulated.

Why the Form number is a genuine trade-off

Each step up the Form ladder buys real operational benefits: an electrician can safely work on one outgoing circuit's terminals without exposing the busbars behind it, a fault inside one functional unit is far less likely to cascade into its neighbours, and a section can often be isolated and swapped without shutting down the rest of the board. None of that is free — every added barrier means more enclosure volume, more internal partitioning, and a higher unit cost, which is exactly why Form 1 boards still exist for low-risk, low-consequence applications rather than Form 4 simply replacing everything.

Lower Form (1–2)Cheaper and more compact, but a fault or live-working error in one part of the board can expose or affect the whole assembly.
Higher Form (3–4)Larger and costlier, but isolates faults, allows safer live work on individual circuits, and keeps unaffected sections running.

Specifying the right form

The Form isn't something to leave to a panel builder's standard offering. It's specified against the actual installation — a data centre or hospital feeding critical loads typically justifies Form 4b for maximum continuity and safe live maintenance, while a small distribution board serving a low-risk area may reasonably stay at Form 2. IEC 61439-2's annex sets out the seven recognised forms and types precisely so a specification can call out an exact configuration, rather than a vague "fully segregated" that different manufacturers might interpret differently.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Form of construction describes internal separation — busbars, functional units, terminals — not overall board quality or rating.
  • ✓Four base forms exist; Forms 2, 3 and 4 each split into lettered sub-variants, and Form 4 further splits into seven types.
  • ✓Higher forms trade cost and size for better safety, fault isolation, and continuity of supply.
  • ✓Specify the Form explicitly against IEC 61439 for the installation's actual risk and maintenance needs — don't leave it to a default.
