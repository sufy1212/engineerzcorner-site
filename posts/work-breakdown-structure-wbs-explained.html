Work Breakdown Structure (WBS): Scope, Decomposed Into Work You Can Assign

A schedule, a budget, and a RACI matrix are all downstream of one thing: whether the scope was broken down into pieces small enough to actually assign, estimate, and track.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Scope & Scheduling
01
A WBS breaks total project scope down into a hierarchy, ending in work packages small enough to estimate, assign, and track.
02
It's organized around deliverables — things produced — not around activities or the org chart.
03
The 100% rule: every level must fully account for the level above it, with nothing left out and nothing double-counted.
04
Everything else — the schedule, the budget, the RACI, the risk register — is built against the WBS, not the other way round.

What it actually is

A WBS is a hierarchical decomposition of the total scope of a project into smaller, more manageable pieces. It answers one question at every level: "what has to be produced to deliver the level above this?" Decomposition stops at the work package level — a piece of work small enough that one person or one team can own it, estimate its cost and duration, and report on whether it's done.

Deliverables, not activities

A WBS is a noun structure, not a verb list The most common mistake is building a WBS out of activities — "design," "procure," "install" — instead of deliverables. A deliverable-based WBS asks what gets produced ("Foundation," "Switchgear Room," "Roof Structure"); a schedule then attaches the verbs and sequencing to each of those deliverables afterward. Organizing by activity instead collapses the WBS and the schedule into the same document, and loses the one thing a WBS is supposed to guarantee — a complete, non-overlapping map of everything that has to exist by the end of the project.

The 100% rule

RuleWhat it prevents
The sum of child elements equals 100% of the parentScope gaps — work that exists but isn't captured anywhere
No child element belongs to more than one parentDouble-counted cost or duration when budgets and schedules roll up
Decomposition stops at a usable work package, not an arbitrary depthEither too coarse to assign, or so fine it becomes a task list, not a scope map

The 100% rule is what makes a WBS trustworthy as a base for cost and schedule roll-ups. If a project manager can point at the top box and say every dollar and every day traces down to a work package under it, the WBS is doing its job. If some scope exists only in someone's head or in an email thread, it isn't in the WBS yet — and it isn't in the budget or the schedule either.

Worked example

A small warehouse fit-out, decomposed three levels deep:

Decomposition

1.0 Warehouse Fit-OutProject
1.1 Electrical SystemsDeliverable
1.1.1 LV Distribution PanelWork package
1.1.2 Lighting InstallationWork package
1.2 Racking & StorageDeliverable
1.2.1 Rack AssemblyWork package

Each work package under 1.1 and 1.2 is what gets a cost estimate, a duration, and an owner. The WBS code (1.1.1, 1.1.2 …) then becomes the common key that the schedule, the cost breakdown, and the RACI matrix all reference — so a change to scope in one of those documents can be traced back to exactly which piece of the WBS it affects.

What sits downstream of it

Downstream toolWhat it takes from the WBS
Schedule / CPM networkWork packages become the activities that get sequenced and durations assigned
Cost estimate & budgetCost is estimated per work package, then rolled up through the hierarchy
Earned Value ManagementPlanned Value and Earned Value are both measured against WBS work packages
RACI matrixEach work package gets an assigned owner and accountable party

Key takeaways

  • ✓Structure the WBS around deliverables — nouns, not verbs — and let the schedule attach the sequencing afterward.
  • ✓Apply the 100% rule at every level: children must fully cover the parent, with nothing missing and nothing double-counted.
  • ✓Stop decomposing at a work package one person or team can own, estimate, and report against.
  • ✓Build the schedule, budget, EVM baseline, and RACI matrix against the WBS codes — not as separate documents that happen to agree.
