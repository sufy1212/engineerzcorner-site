LV Panel Types Explained: Switchgear, MCC, PFC, VFD, Feeder Pillar & More

Every job site talks about "the LV panel" as if there's only one. In practice a low-voltage system is a chain of different panel types, each doing one job — from the main incomer down to the final circuit. Here's what exists in the LV range, what each one does, and where it sits in that chain.

What counts as an "LV panel"?

Any low-voltage switchgear and controlgear assembly — a factory-built enclosure holding busbars, breakers, contactors and other devices that switch, protect, control or distribute electrical energy — is governed internationally by IEC 61439. "Low voltage" here means the assembly's rated voltage doesn't exceed 1000 V AC or 1500 V DC. Above that, you're into medium-voltage switchgear (IEC 62271), a different standard entirely.

IEC 61439 isn't one document; it's a series, and different panel types fall under different parts of it. Part 1 sets the general rules every assembly follows. Part 2 covers general power switchgear and controlgear assemblies (PSC) — the broad category most industrial and commercial panels fall into. Part 3 covers distribution boards meant to be operated by ordinary, untrained people (DBO) — your typical domestic or light-commercial final DB. Part 5 covers assemblies for power distribution in public networks (PENDA) — utility-side equipment like feeder pillars. Part 6 covers busbar trunking systems (busways).

Main power distribution panels

The panels that take bulk incoming power and split it into manageable feeders.

MDB / MSB
Main Distribution Board / Main Switchboard
The primary distribution hub, taking supply straight from the transformer or generator and splitting it into sub-mains and major feeders. Houses the main incomer breaker, bus-section and outgoing ACBs/MCCBs — rated up to 6300 A on large installations.
PCC
Power Control Center
Sits between the MDB and the load-specific panels on larger industrial sites — a high-capacity hub (typically 2000–6300 A) that receives from the MDB and feeds MCCs, APFC panels and other downstream boards.
SDB / DB
Sub-Distribution Board / Distribution Board
The final stage — splits a feeder down into individual final circuits: lighting, sockets, small power. In domestic and light-commercial work these are IEC 61439-3 DBOs, built for safe operation by untrained people (≤300 V to earth, ≤125 A per outgoing circuit).

Motor & drive panels

Panels that don't just distribute power to motors — they actively start, protect and control them.

MCC
Motor Control Center
A dedicated panel housing multiple motor starters — contactors, overload relays, MCCBs and (increasingly) soft starters or VFDs — each in its own standardized fixed or withdrawable functional unit, so a single motor can be isolated and pulled for maintenance without shutting the rest down.
VFD Panel
Variable Frequency Drive Panel
Houses one or more variable frequency drives that vary a motor's speed by adjusting output frequency — used for pumps, fans and conveyors where speed control saves energy and gives a soft, controlled start. Can be a standalone panel or built in as drive feeders inside an MCC.

Power quality panel

The panel that fixes the site's power factor rather than distributing or controlling load.

APFC / PFC Panel
Automatic Power Factor Correction Panel
Houses a bank of capacitors switched in and out by a controller (via contactors or thyristors) to keep the site's power factor near unity, cutting the reactive-power penalty on the electricity bill. Sites with heavy VFD or non-linear loads often add detuned reactors to avoid harmonic resonance.

Outdoor & utility-side distribution

Panels built for outdoor exposure or bulk current distribution rather than indoor switchboards.

Feeder Pillar
LV Feeder Pillar
A weatherproof outdoor or underground-fed cabinet that taps off a utility LV distribution network to serve a cluster of consumers, or a run of street lighting. Typically IP54/IP65 rated, 160–4000 A, and tested as a public electricity network distribution assembly.
Busbar Trunking
Busway / Busbar Trunking System (BTS)
A prefabricated, modular run of busbars in a metal enclosure, used instead of cable for high-current vertical risers or long horizontal runs. Tap-off units plug in anywhere along the run to feed a panel below without a separate cable pull.

Standby & control panels

Panels that manage where the power comes from, or that carry logic instead of load current.

ATS / AMF Panel
Automatic Transfer Switch / Auto Mains Failure Panel
Automatically switches the load between two sources — typically utility mains and a standby generator — on loss of the primary supply, and switches back once it's restored. On multi-generator sites this pairs with a synchronizing panel handling start/stop sequencing, synchronization and load sharing.
PLC / Automation Panel
Control Panel
Not a power-distribution panel at all — houses the PLC, I/O modules, relays, HMI and communication gear that run process or machine control logic. The enclosure itself still follows IEC 61439-2; the PLC hardware and programming follow IEC 61131.

How they connect

1
MDB / MSB — Main IncomerPower enters from the transformer or generator, protected by the main incomer breaker.
2
PCC — Power Control CenterOn larger sites, sits downstream of the MDB, splitting bulk power toward MCCs, APFC panels and other sub-boards.
3
MCC / APFC / SDB — Load-specific panelsMotor loads route through an MCC (with VFD feeders where speed control is needed); an APFC panel taps in parallel across the bus to correct the site's power factor; everything else routes through an SDB.
4
Final DBThe last stop before the load — splits a feeder into individual final circuits: lighting, sockets, small power.
5
Feeder pillar / busbar trunkingWhere the run is outdoors or high-current, a feeder pillar or busbar trunking system replaces buried cable between buildings or up through a structure.
6
ATS / AMF panelWhere standby power exists, this sits ahead of the MDB, choosing which source feeds the whole downstream chain.

Key takeaways

  • ✓An LV panel is any switchgear/controlgear assembly rated up to 1000 V AC / 1500 V DC, governed by the IEC 61439 series.
  • ✓Distribution panels (MDB, PCC, SDB/DB) just split power into smaller feeders; control panels (MCC, VFD, APFC) actively manage what's downstream.
  • ✓Feeder pillars and busbar trunking exist because not every LV run makes sense as buried cable — one's for outdoor utility tap-offs, the other for high-current risers.
  • ✓ATS/AMF panels decide the source; PLC/automation panels carry logic, not load current — neither is a distribution panel in the usual sense.
  • ✓Almost every panel type has its own IEC 61439 part: Part 2 for general power assemblies, Part 3 for ordinary-person DBs, Part 5 for public networks, Part 6 for busbar trunking.
