Harmonics in Power Systems: Sources, Effects and How to Mitigate Them

Every VFD, UPS and switch-mode supply on a system quietly injects distortion into the waveform — harmless in isolation, but cumulative, measurable, and eventually expensive to ignore.

Where harmonics actually come from

An ideal AC supply is one clean sine wave at a single frequency. Non-linear loads — ones that don't draw current in smooth proportion to the applied voltage — distort that waveform, and the distorted result can be mathematically decomposed into the fundamental frequency plus a series of harmonics riding on top of it, each at an integer multiple of the fundamental. Nearly every modern piece of electronic or power-converter equipment falls into this category:

Variable frequency drives (VFDs)
UPS systems and inverters
Switch-mode power supplies (SMPS)
LED lighting and LED drivers
Arc furnaces and welding machines
Computers, PLCs, printers and similar office/control loads

Quantifying the distortion: THD

Total harmonic distortion (THD) expresses how much of a waveform's energy sits in the harmonics rather than the fundamental, as a single percentage:

THD (%)  =  √(V2² + V3² + V4² + … + Vn²) ÷ V1  ×  100
V1 = fundamental voltage (RMS) · V2, V3, V4 … Vn = harmonic voltages (RMS)

Worked example: a fundamental voltage (V1) of 400V with a total harmonic voltage (RMS) of 20V gives THD = (20 ÷ 400) × 100 = 5%. The same calculation applies to current THD, which is generally the more revealing figure for equipment stress since it's current, not voltage, that drives I²R heating in cables and windings.

Which harmonic order, and from what

OrderTypical sourceNotable behaviour
3rd (triplen)Single-phase non-linear loads (SMPS, LED drivers)Adds arithmetically in the neutral conductor rather than cancelling
5th6-pulse converters, VFDsUsually the largest single harmonic in a VFD-heavy system
7th6-pulse converters, arcing devicesSecond-largest contributor alongside the 5th in most drive systems
11th, 13thHigh-frequency switching devicesSmaller magnitude but harder to filter passively

IEEE 519 voltage THD limits

IEEE 519 sets maximum voltage THD at the point of common coupling (PCC) — the point where a customer's system connects to the utility, or where multiple loads share a common supply. The allowed limit isn't one flat number; it tightens as system voltage increases, since higher-voltage systems generally serve larger, more sensitive, and more interconnected loads:

System voltageVoltage THD limit
< 1 kV8%
1 kV – 69 kV5%
69 kV – 161 kV2.5%
161 kV and above1.5%

What unmanaged harmonics actually cost you

Overheating of cables, transformers and motors from added I²R and eddy-current losses
Neutral conductor overload, driven specifically by triplen harmonics (3rd, 9th, 15th…)
Increased transformer losses and reduced effective capacity
Capacitor bank failure and reduced service life
Nuisance circuit breaker tripping and equipment malfunction
Poor power factor and higher energy costs

Solutions to reduce harmonics

Passive harmonic filter (PHF)A tuned LC circuit designed to absorb specific harmonic orders — simple, robust, and effective where the dominant orders are known and stable.
Active harmonic filter (AHF)Detects distortion in real time and injects an opposing harmonic current to cancel it — more flexible across a changing load mix, at higher cost.
K-rated transformerDesigned with the extra eddy-current and stray-loss capacity needed to carry harmonic-rich current without overheating at its nameplate rating.
Line reactor / DC link reactorAdds impedance ahead of a VFD or converter, smoothing the current waveform and reducing the harmonics it injects back onto the supply.

None of these substitute for good system design in the first place — adequate cable and transformer sizing with harmonics accounted for, proper neutral sizing where triplen harmonics are expected, and regular monitoring, all reduce how much correction equipment ends up carrying the load.

Measuring and monitoring

A power quality analyzer connected at the MDB or PCC measures voltage, current, THD%, the full harmonic spectrum, power factor, kW/kVA/kVAR, flicker and phase unbalance together — giving a much fuller picture than any single reading alone. Because harmonic-producing loads change as equipment is added, regular monitoring rather than a one-off measurement is what actually catches a system drifting toward its THD limit before it becomes a visible problem.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Harmonics are integer-multiple sine waves added by non-linear loads — VFDs, UPS, SMPS, LED drivers and arc furnaces chief among them.
  • ✓IEEE 519's voltage THD limit at the PCC tightens as system voltage rises, from 8% below 1kV to 1.5% above 161kV.
  • ✓Unmanaged harmonics show up as heat, neutral overload, capacitor failure, nuisance tripping and higher energy cost — not just a number on an analyzer.
  • ✓Match the mitigation — passive filter, active filter, K-rated transformer, or reactor — to which harmonic orders actually dominate your spectrum.
