IS, IEC & IEEE Codes: The Standards Behind Electrical Engineering

Three separate standards bodies, three separate letter prefixes, and one shared purpose: making sure an electrical installation is safe, reliable, and built the same defensible way regardless of who designed it.

Three bodies, one underlying job

Every one of these codes exists to answer the same underlying question for a specific piece of equipment or practice: what does "done correctly and safely" actually mean, in enough detail that two different engineers reach the same answer independently. IS codes are India's national standards, issued by the Bureau of Indian Standards. IEC codes are the international reference point that a great deal of national practice — including much of IS — is aligned with. IEEE standards fill in more of the applied power-system-engineering side: grounding studies, arc flash, harmonics, power quality, protection coordination.

None of these bodies compete for the same job — they overlap, cross-reference each other, and in practice a single project usually draws from all three depending on what's actually being designed.

For solar EPC projects: the codes that come up constantly

Solar power projects sit right at the intersection of all three families, which is exactly why the same handful of codes keep reappearing across design reviews, inspection checklists, and commissioning documentation.

Frequently used on solar EPC work

  • ✓IEC 61215 — PV module qualification
  • ✓IEC 61730 — PV module safety
  • ✓IEC 62446 — PV system documentation & commissioning tests
  • ✓IEC 62548 — PV array design
  • ✓IEC 60364-7-712 — Solar PV electrical installation
  • ✓IS 3043 — Earthing
  • ✓IEC 62305 — Lightning protection
  • ✓IEEE 80 — Substation grounding design
  • ✓IEC 60076 — Transformers
  • ✓IEC 62271 — HV switchgear
Codes referenced together on the same project don't always agree on every specific value — where an IS clause and an IEC clause both apply, the contract, the local regulator, or the more conservative of the two typically governs. Confirm which standard is contractually binding before treating any of these as a substitute for the actual project specification.

Key takeaways

  • ✓IS, IEC and IEEE aren't competitors — they're different scopes that frequently apply to the same project at once.
  • ✓IEC is the international reference point much of national practice, including IS, is aligned with.
  • ✓IEEE fills in the applied power-system side — grounding studies, arc flash, harmonics, protection.
  • ✓Solar EPC work routinely draws from all three families for a single installation, not just one.
