Types of HVAC Fans

Different fans, different applications, same purpose — better airflow. Every HVAC system moves air with a fan, but which fan depends on how much pressure the ductwork needs, how much space is available, and how the air has to travel once it leaves the blade.

HVAC fans move air through the system — but the mechanism differs by fan type, and that difference is what determines the pressure and airflow it can deliver.

Matching fan type to the job comes down to four things: airflow requirement, pressure needs, available space, and how the ductwork is laid out.

HVAC fans exist to move air through the system — supply, return, exhaust, or ventilation. Choosing the right fan type isn't a minor spec detail: it directly shapes comfort, energy efficiency, and how well the whole system performs. Each fan type below moves air by a different mechanism, and that mechanism is what decides where it belongs.

01
Axial Fan

Air flows parallel to the fan shaft, straight through the blades. This gives high airflow at low to medium pressure — the design is simple, so it's cheap to build and install.

Typical applications
  • Cooling towers
  • Ventilation systems
  • Parking areas
  • Industrial exhaust
Key advantages
  • High airflow capacity
  • Energy efficient
  • Simple design
  • Low installation cost
02
Centrifugal Fan

Air enters the fan axially and exits perpendicular to the fan shaft. That 90° redirection is what generates medium to high pressure, making it suitable for ducted systems that need to overcome real resistance.

Typical applications
  • AHU systems
  • Ducted HVAC systems
  • Commercial buildings
  • Hospitals & clean rooms
Key advantages
  • High pressure capability
  • Suitable for long duct runs
  • Stable performance
  • Widely used in AHUs
03
Inline Fan

Air flows in a straight line through the fan, in the same duct-mounted housing it lives in. The compact design is built specifically to drop into a duct run without needing a separate plenum.

Typical applications
  • Duct ventilation
  • Bathroom & kitchen exhaust
  • Small spaces
  • Boosting airflow
Key advantages
  • Compact & lightweight
  • Easy installation
  • Low noise
  • Cost effective
04
Mixed-Flow Fan

Air enters axially and exits at an angle — a hybrid between axial and centrifugal geometry. That gives it medium pressure and medium airflow, splitting the difference between the two.

Typical applications
  • Tunnel ventilation
  • Industrial ventilation
  • Large spaces
  • Marine applications
Key advantages
  • Balanced performance
  • Space-saving design
  • Efficient for angled airflow needs
  • Versatile applications
05
Exhaust Fan

Draws air from the space and expels it outside — used to remove stale, hot, or contaminated air rather than to supply conditioned air into a room.

Typical applications
  • Toilets & bathrooms
  • Kitchens
  • Parking garages
  • Industrial spaces
Key advantages
  • Effective air removal
  • Improves indoor air quality
  • Prevents moisture and odors
  • Easy to maintain

Fan types at a glance

Actual airflow and pressure figures vary by blade design, motor size and manufacturer — always check the fan curve for the specific unit being selected.

Key takeaway

  • ✓Selecting the right fan type depends on airflow requirements, pressure needs, system design, and application — not just what's on hand.
  • ✓Axial fans move the most air for the least cost, but can't build the pressure a long, resistance-heavy duct run needs.
  • ✓Centrifugal fans are the default for ducted HVAC and AHUs precisely because the 90° turn gives them the pressure axial fans lack.
  • ✓Inline and exhaust fans solve a narrower problem — a compact duct-mounted boost, or point extraction of stale or contaminated air.
  • ✓The right choice up front means better performance, lower energy consumption, and fewer surprises over the system's service life.
