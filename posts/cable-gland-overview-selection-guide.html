Cable Glands: What They Do and How to Get the Selection Right

A cable gland looks like a small brass fitting, but it's doing four separate jobs at once at the exact point a cable is most vulnerable — where it stops being protected by conduit or tray and enters an enclosure.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Electrical / Cable Accessories
01
Cable holding & support — grips the cable so pulling force never reaches the internal terminals.
02
Dust & moisture protection — maintains the enclosure's IP rating at the entry point.
03
Electrical safety — provides armour earthing and keeps grounding continuity intact.
04
Cable sealing — protects insulation from the sharp edge of the panel cut-out itself.

What it actually is

A cable gland is the mechanical fitting used to secure and seal a cable exactly where it enters an equipment enclosure — a panel, junction box, inverter, transformer, motor, or switchgear. It's a small component doing several jobs simultaneously: gripping the cable so tension never transmits to the terminals it's wired to, sealing the entry point against dust and moisture, and — for armoured cable — carrying the armour's earth connection through to the enclosure.

Types of cable gland

Brass cable glandCommonly used for armoured and industrial cables. Good mechanical strength and corrosion resistance.
Double compression glandTwo sealing points — inner seal on the cable bedding, outer seal on the armour and sheath. Used for armoured cables.
Single compression glandOne sealing point, no armour clamping. Used where the cable is unarmoured and armour protection isn't needed.
EMC cable glandBuilt for electromagnetic shielding continuity — used where shielded cables need that shielding carried through to the enclosure.
Nylon cable glandLightweight and corrosion resistant. Common on control and instrumentation cables where mechanical demands are lower.

Main parts

  1. 1
    Gland bodyThe main threaded portion, fixed directly to the enclosure.
  2. 2
    Lock nutSecures the gland body in place inside the panel wall.
  3. 3
    Sealing ringProvides the moisture and dust seal against the cable outer sheath.
  4. 4
    Armour cone / clampGrips the cable's metallic armour, for armoured-cable installations.
  5. 5
    Compression nutTightens down to complete the seal around the cable.
  6. 6
    Earth tagProvides the connection point for the armour's earth continuity.

Selection criteria

  • 1Cable size and outer diameter
  • 2Required IP protection rating
  • 3Armoured vs. unarmoured cable
  • 4Material requirement — brass, stainless steel, or nylon
  • 5Cable voltage rating
  • 6Installation environment

Quality inspection points

  • ✓Correct gland size matched to the cable's outer diameter
  • ✓Gland and lock nut both properly tightened
  • ✓No damage to the cable sheath during installation
  • ✓Armour properly clamped and earthed
  • ✓Sealing ring properly compressed, not pinched or skewed
  • ✓Unused enclosure entries sealed with blanking plugs
  • ✓Enclosure's rated IP protection maintained after installation
  • ✓Cable bending radius respected near the gland entry

Installation walkthrough — double compression gland

1Prepare the cable end as required — strip sheath, armour, and bedding to the correct lengths.
2Slide the gland components onto the cable in the correct order before making up the entry.
3Insert the cable into the enclosure, feeding it through the gland body.
4Tighten the armour cone so it clamps the armour wires properly.
5Tighten the compression nut to seal against the cable bedding.
6Confirm overall tightness, check the seal, and verify earthing continuity through the earth tag.

Common materials

A correctly installed cable gland protects more than the cable — it's what keeps the enclosure's IP rating, the earthing system, and the termination itself all working together as intended.

Key takeaways

  • ✓A cable gland is doing four jobs at once: grip, seal, earth, and protect — not just "holding the cable in."
  • ✓Gland type has to match the cable — armoured cable needs a gland built to clamp and earth that armour.
  • ✓IP rating is only as good as its weakest entry point — every unused entry needs a blanking plug too.
  • ✓Correct tightening and bend radius matter as much as picking the right gland in the first place.
