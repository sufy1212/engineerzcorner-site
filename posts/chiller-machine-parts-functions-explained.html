Chiller Machine: Main Parts & Functions

The chiller is the heart of centralized air conditioning — every AHU, FCU and cooling coil downstream depends on the chilled water it produces. Here's what's inside one, component by component, and how the refrigeration cycle actually turns hot water into cold water.

Engineerz CorneRReference · Central Plant
01
A chiller doesn't create cold — it moves heat, from the chilled water loop to the condenser side, using the refrigeration cycle.
02
Four components do the core work — compressor, condenser, expansion valve, evaporator — everything else supports, protects or controls that loop.
03
The chilled water circuit and the refrigeration cycle are two separate loops that only exchange heat inside the evaporator and condenser.
04
Air-cooled vs water-cooled is a capacity and site-constraint decision, not a "better/worse" one.

A chiller is an HVAC machine that removes heat from water and supplies chilled water — typically leaving around 6–7°C and returning around 11–12°C — to AHUs, FCUs or process cooling loads. Everything on the machine exists to make that refrigeration cycle happen safely, efficiently, and with enough monitoring to shut itself down before something breaks.

Step 1
Compressor
Compresses low-pressure refrigerant vapour into a hot, high-pressure vapour — the only component doing actual work in the cycle.
Step 2
Condenser
Rejects that heat to air or condenser water, condensing the refrigerant back into a high-pressure liquid.
Step 3
Expansion Valve
Drops refrigerant pressure sharply, producing a cold low-pressure liquid/vapour mix ready to absorb heat.
Step 4
Evaporator
Absorbs heat from chilled water — the refrigerant boils off as low-pressure vapour and the cycle repeats.

Items 1–5 are the refrigeration cycle itself. Items 6–10 keep that cycle clean, dry and precisely metered. Items 11–13 are the water side — chilled water to the load, condenser water to atmosphere. And 14–18 are the nervous system: the panel won't let the machine run unless flow, pressure and current are all inside safe limits.

Basic refrigeration cycle

Every chiller — air-cooled or water-cooled, screw or centrifugal — runs the same four-step loop:

1
CompressorDraws in low-pressure refrigerant vapour from the evaporator and compresses it into a hot, high-pressure vapour.
2
CondenserRejects that heat to air or condenser water, condensing the refrigerant into a high-pressure liquid.
3
Expansion ValveDrops the refrigerant's pressure sharply, producing a cold low-pressure liquid/vapour mix.
4
EvaporatorAbsorbs heat from the chilled water — the refrigerant boils off as low-pressure vapour and the cycle repeats.

The evaporator is where the actual "chilling" happens — chilled water gives up its heat to the boiling refrigerant. The condenser is where that same heat, plus the heat added by the compressor, gets dumped to atmosphere. The compressor is the only component doing work; everything else just moves heat around.

Chilled water circuit

This is the loop most people actually interact with, and it's entirely separate from the refrigerant inside the machine — the two only meet at the evaporator's heat-exchanger surface. Typical design values: supply 6–7°C, return 11–12°C (values depend on design and manufacturer).

Chiller → Chilled Water Pump → AHU/FCU

The chilled water pump pushes cold water from the evaporator out to every AHU and FCU coil in the building.

AHU/FCU → Return Water → Chiller

Water picks up heat from room/supply air at each coil and returns warmer, ready to be re-chilled at the evaporator.

Air-cooled vs water-cooled chiller

Air-Cooled ChillerWater-Cooled Chiller
Uses fans and condenser coils to reject heatUses a water-cooled condenser
Rejects heat directly to outdoor airRequires condenser-water pumps and a cooling tower
No cooling tower or condenser-water pump neededMore energy efficient for larger capacities
Suitable for small to medium capacitiesSuitable for large commercial / industrial applications

The trade-off is simple: air-cooled skips the cooling tower and condenser-water pump entirely — less plant, less water treatment, less to maintain — but it's less efficient at scale, since it's rejecting heat straight to outdoor air rather than through a wet cooling tower. Water-cooled adds that extra loop and equipment, but wins on efficiency once the load gets large enough to justify it.

Key benefits

  • ✓High energy efficiency compared with unitary or split systems at building scale.
  • ✓Precise temperature & humidity control across large, mixed-use buildings.
  • ✓Quiet operation — the compressor and refrigeration cycle sit in a plant room, not in occupied space.
  • ✓Long service life with proper maintenance of the refrigeration and water sides.
  • ✓Suitable for large and critical facilities — hospitals, data centers, commercial buildings, hotels & resorts, industries.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Compressor, condenser, expansion valve and evaporator are the four components that actually run the refrigeration cycle.
  • ✓The chilled water circuit and the refrigerant circuit are separate loops, meeting only at the evaporator.
  • ✓Safety devices, flow switches and pressure sensors exist so the panel can refuse to start — or trip — before damage occurs.
  • ✓Air-cooled suits smaller loads and simpler plant; water-cooled suits larger loads where the extra efficiency justifies the cooling tower.
