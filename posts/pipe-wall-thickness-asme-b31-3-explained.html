Pipe Wall Thickness (ASME B31.3)

The pressure formula gives you the wall the pipe needs to hold the pressure. The pipe you order has to be thicker, because the mill is allowed to deliver a thinner wall than the one on the label and corrosion will remove some more.

Engineerz CorneRReference · ASME B31.3
01
Pressure design thickness t = PD / 2(SEW + PY) is only the wall needed to contain internal pressure. It is the starting point, not the thickness you order.
02
Add the corrosion, erosion and mechanical allowance c to get the minimum required thickness tm = t + c.
03
For new seamless pipe, divide tm by 0.875 to cover the 12.5% mill under-tolerance, then pick the next standard schedule at or above that figure.
04
The mill tolerance is a purchasing allowance. It is not applied when evaluating pipe already in service.

The pressure design equation

ASME B31.3 gives the pressure design thickness of straight pipe under internal pressure (paragraph 304.1.2) as:

t = P × D / [ 2 ( S × E × W + P × Y ) ]

SymbolMeaningWhere it comes from
PInternal design gauge pressureProcess design basis, not the operating pressure
DOutside diameter of the pipeActual OD from ASME B36.10M / B36.19M (NPS 6 is 6.625 in, not 6 in)
SAllowable stress at the design temperatureB31.3 Table A-1, for the pipe material
EQuality factorB31.3 Table A-1A or A-1B, by pipe type and weld
WWeld joint strength reduction factor1.0 unless the temperature range calls for a lower value
YCoefficientB31.3 Table 304.1.1, by material and temperature

Use consistent units: pressure and stress in psi with diameters in inches, or in MPa with millimetres. For ferritic steels at or below 900 °F (482 °C), Y = 0.4. The equation applies to thin-wall pipe, where t is less than D/6; thicker wall pipe needs a different approach in the code.

From pressure thickness to the pipe you order

1Calculate the pressure design thickness t from the equation above.
2Add the allowance c (corrosion, erosion, and thread or groove depth where there is one): tm = t + c.
3Cover the mill tolerance. For seamless pipe with a 12.5% under-tolerance, nominal thickness must be at least tm / 0.875.
4Choose the next standard wall thickness at or above that value from ASME B36.10M (carbon and alloy steel) or B36.19M (stainless).
5Check: the selected wall less 12.5% must still be at least tm.

Worked example: NPS 6 carbon steel

Design pressure 1000 psig, NPS 6 (D = 6.625 in), carbon steel with S = 15,000 psi at the design temperature, seamless so E = 1, W = 1, Y = 0.4, allowance c = 0.05 in.

StepWorkingResult
Pressure design thickness t1000 × 6.625 / [2 (15,000 + 1000 × 0.4)] = 6625 / 30,8000.215 in
Minimum required thickness tm0.215 + 0.0500.265 in
Nominal after mill tolerance0.265 / 0.8750.303 in
Schedule 40 checkWall 0.280 in, less 12.5% = 0.245 in, against tm of 0.265 inFails
Schedule 80 checkWall 0.432 in, less 12.5% = 0.378 in, against tm of 0.265 inPasses

The pressure thickness of 0.215 in looks as if Schedule 40 would be plenty. It is not: allowance and mill tolerance together take the requirement above the 0.280 in wall, so the line has to go to Schedule 80, unless a thickness between the two is specified and available. The thin-wall condition holds because D/6 = 1.10 in, far above t.

Sensitivity on the same pipe: a 0.125 in allowance instead of 0.05 raises tm to 0.340 in and the nominal to 0.389 in; a lower allowable stress of 12,000 psi (a hotter design temperature) gives t = 0.267 in and a nominal of 0.362 in. Both still land on Schedule 80, but they eat into the margin that the 0.378 in minimum wall gives.

Three thicknesses that get confused

NameMeaningUsed for
tPressure design thicknessContaining the pressure; the base for everything else
tmt plus allowancesMinimum thickness the pipe must actually have
Nominaltm divided by 0.875 for seamless pipe, then rounded up to a scheduleWhat goes on the purchase order and the piping class

When the mill tolerance does and does not apply

The 12.5% under-tolerance is a manufacturing allowance and is the usual figure for seamless carbon steel pipe such as ASTM A106 and A53. It belongs in new design and purchasing, where the delivered wall may be thinner than the nominal. For pipe already installed, the retirement thickness used in inspection under API 570 is based on the pressure design thickness plus any allowances, without the mill tolerance. Welded pipe and other product specifications can state a different tolerance, so take the figure from the pipe specification being bought.

Mistakes that catch people out

MistakeWhat goes wrong
Using nominal pipe size as DNPS 6 has a 6.625 in outside diameter; using 6 gives a thinner result
Reading S at room temperatureAllowable stress falls with temperature, so the wall must be sized at the design temperature
Mixing unitsMPa with inches, or bar without conversion, is out by an order of magnitude
Leaving out E or WWelded pipe and high temperature service can carry factors below 1.0
Skipping the mill toleranceThe delivered wall can be 12.5% under nominal, and the margin vanishes
Treating the result as the whole designThis equation covers internal pressure only; external pressure, weight, thermal loads and flexibility are separate checks

Key takeaways

  • ✓t = PD / 2(SEW + PY) is the pressure thickness; add allowance c for tm, then divide by 0.875 for new seamless pipe.
  • ✓Use the actual outside diameter, allowable stress at design temperature, and consistent units.
  • ✓Y is 0.4 for ferritic steel at or below 900 °F (482 °C); other cases come from Table 304.1.1.
  • ✓Pick the next standard schedule at or above the nominal thickness, then check wall less 12.5% against tm.
  • ✓Confirm all values against the edition of ASME B31.3 and the pipe specification for your project.
