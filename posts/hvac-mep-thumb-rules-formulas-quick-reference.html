HVAC MEP Thumb Rules & Formulas: Quick Reference for Engineers

Nine fast rules-of-thumb for cooling load, airflow, fan and pump power, chilled water flow, chiller capacity, cooling tower sizing, and pipe selection — useful for estimating, not for final design.

Every rule here reduces to one core relationship: heat, flow, and ΔT (or ΔP) are always locked together — change one and the other two respond.

Use these for early-stage sizing, quick sanity checks, and BOQ estimates — then verify with detailed calculations before issuing for construction.

1. Cooling load (preliminary estimate)

Before any detailed heat-gain calculation exists, a floor-area-based factor gives a rough tonnage for early budgeting. Residential space typically runs 20–25 BTU/hr·ft² (215–270 W/m²); office and commercial space runs higher, around 25–35 BTU/hr·ft² (270–375 W/m²), reflecting denser occupancy, higher equipment load, and more glazing.

Cooling Load (BTU/hr)  =  Floor Area (ft²)  ×  Heat Load Factor
Final sizing should always come from a detailed load calculation accounting for occupancy, orientation, glazing, lighting, ventilation, and equipment loads — this factor is for early budgeting only.

2. Airflow (CFM) from tonnage

The most common comfort-cooling rule of thumb ties supply airflow directly to refrigeration tonnage.

1 TR  =  400 CFM
Typical supply air range: 350–450 CFM/TR. AHU coil face velocity typically runs 400–500 FPM — go much higher and moisture starts carrying off the coil into the duct.

3. Fan horsepower

Fan power depends on how much air it's moving, against how much resistance, at what efficiency.

HP  =  (CFM × Static Pressure)  /  (6356 × Fan Efficiency)
CFM = airflow · Static Pressure = in. w.g. · Fan Efficiency is typically 50–70% for standard centrifugal fans.

4. Chilled water flow rate

Flow rate for a chilled water coil comes straight from the heat balance: the coil has to remove a certain amount of heat, and water can only carry so much heat per gallon per degree of temperature rise.

GPM  =  BTU/hr  /  (500 × ΔT)   or   GPM  =  (24 × TR)  /  ΔT
Worked example: a 2 TR cooling load with a 10°F ΔT gives a flow rate of 4.8 GPM.

5. Chiller capacity

Converting a measured cooling load back into tons uses the same 12,000 BTU/hr-per-ton conversion in reverse.

TR  =  Total Cooling Load (BTU/hr)  /  12,000
Worked example: a 60,000 BTU/hr load is exactly 5 TR.

6. Cooling tower sizing

For water-cooled chillers, the cooling tower has to reject not just the building's cooling load but also the heat added by the compressor itself.

Heat Rejection  ≈  1.25 × Chiller Capacity (TR)
Condenser water flow: GPM = (24 × Heat Rejection TR) / ΔT — typical condenser water ΔT runs 9–10°F.

7. Pipe sizing (GPM-based)

Pipe size can't be picked from flow rate alone. A given GPM can run through a small pipe at high velocity or a larger pipe at low velocity — the choice depends on several other factors working together:

  • ✓Water velocity — recommended range is typically 4–8 ft/s
  • ✓Friction loss along the run
  • ✓Allowable pressure drop
  • ✓Pipe material (roughness affects friction)
  • ✓Equivalent length, including fittings and valves
Always design for efficiency and minimal pressure drop — oversizing wastes material and cost, undersizing wastes pump energy for the life of the system.

8 & 9. Pump power

Pump power can be expressed in horsepower or kilowatts — both formulas describe the same relationship between flow, head, and efficiency.

HP  =  (Flow (GPM) × Head (ft))  /  (3960 × Pump Efficiency)
Flow = GPM · Head = Total Dynamic Head (ft) · Pump Efficiency typically 70–90%.
Power (kW)  =  (Q (GPM) × H (ft) × 9.81)  /  (η × 367 × 1000)
Q = Flow (GPM) · H = Head (ft) · η = Pump Efficiency (typically 70–90%).

Key takeaways

  • ✓These thumb rules are for preliminary design, estimation, and quick project checks — not a replacement for detailed engineering calculations.
  • ✓Nearly every formula here reduces to the same shape: capacity is flow multiplied by a temperature or pressure difference, divided by an efficiency term.
  • ✓1 TR = 12,000 BTU/hr = 400 CFM is the conversion most of the others are built on — worth memorizing on its own.
  • ✓Pipe and pump sizing both depend on more than one number at a time — flow rate alone never fully determines the answer.
