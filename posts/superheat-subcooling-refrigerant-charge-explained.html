Superheat & Subcooling Explained

Gauge pressure alone can't tell you whether a system is low on charge. Superheat and subcooling — how far the refrigerant sits from its saturation temperature — can.

Engineerz CorneRReference · HVAC / Refrigeration
01
Superheat is measured on the low side: suction line temperature minus the saturation temperature at suction pressure.
02
Subcooling is measured on the high side: saturation temperature at liquid pressure minus liquid line temperature.
03
Which one you charge to depends on the metering device — subcooling for TXV systems, superheat for fixed-orifice systems.

One reading is ambiguous; the pair, together with the pressures, points to a specific fault.

Both are distances from saturation

For a pure refrigerant, the temperature at which it boils or condenses is fixed by its pressure. Read the pressure on a gauge, look up the matching saturation temperature on a pressure–temperature chart for that refrigerant, and you know where the refrigerant sits on the boundary between liquid and vapor. Superheat is how far above that boundary the vapor has been heated after the evaporator finishes boiling it. Subcooling is how far below the boundary the liquid has been cooled after the condenser finishes condensing it.

Superheat  =  Tsuction line − Tsat(Psuction)
Suction line temperature is measured near the evaporator outlet or compressor inlet, with the sensor insulated so ambient air doesn't skew it.
Subcooling  =  Tsat(Pliquid) − Tliquid line
Liquid line temperature is measured near the condenser outlet. For blends with temperature glide, use the dew point for superheat and the bubble point for subcooling.

Why each one matters

Superheat protects the compressorIt confirms only vapor is returning. Too little superheat risks liquid reaching the compressor; too much means the evaporator is starved and the compressor runs hot.
Subcooling protects the metering deviceIt confirms solid liquid is reaching the expansion device. Too little and flash gas forms in the liquid line, cutting capacity.

Which one you charge to

Metering deviceTypically charged byWhy
TXV / EEVSubcoolingThe valve modulates to hold evaporator superheat, so superheat barely changes with charge — subcooling is the sensitive indicator
Fixed orifice / pistonSuperheatThe orifice can't adjust, so superheat varies with charge and load and is the sensitive indicator

Target values are set by the equipment manufacturer — often a subcooling target on the nameplate or a superheat table keyed to indoor wet-bulb and outdoor dry-bulb temperature. Use those rather than a generic number.

Reading the pair together

ConditionSuperheatSubcoolingSuction pressure
Low chargeHighLowLow
OverchargeNormal to lowHighNormal to high (head pressure high)
Liquid line restrictionHighHighLow
Low evaporator airflowLowNormalLow (coil may ice)

These are general trends, not a diagnosis by themselves. Confirm airflow, filter and coil condition, and measurement accuracy before adding or removing refrigerant — a bad sensor placement or a dirty coil produces the same numbers as a charge problem.

Key takeaways

  • ✓Superheat = suction line temp − saturation temp at suction pressure; subcooling = saturation temp at liquid pressure − liquid line temp.
  • ✓Charge to subcooling on TXV systems and to superheat on fixed-orifice systems, using the manufacturer's targets.
  • ✓High superheat with low subcooling points to low charge; high subcooling with high head pressure points to overcharge.
  • ✓High superheat with high subcooling points to a restriction, not a charge shortage.
  • ✓Rule out airflow and measurement problems before changing the charge.
