About This Toolkit

What's here, how to use it, and where its limits are.
A free set of reference calculators across nine engineering disciplines, built for quick sanity checks and first-pass sizing — not a substitute for a stamped calculation package.

What's here

Electrical (short circuit, voltage drop, selectivity/TCC, cable sizing, panel estimate, transformer/genset sizing, busbar/enclosure sizing), Mechanical (pipe flow, pump power, duct sizing, heat load, belt drive), HVAC (duct sizing, cooling load, refrigeration/chiller capacity), Civil (beam bending, column capacity, footing sizing, retaining wall stability), Automation (signal scaling, PID tuning, control valve sizing, level transmitter ranging, VFD speed scaling, plus a symbol library, engineering guides and comparisons), Process (relief valve sizing, vessel/tank volume & level, line sizing, cavitation/flashing check, purge gas sizing, plus a symbol library and interview questions), Solar (array/battery/inverter sizing, plus a symbol library, engineering guides and comparisons), Project Management (RACI matrix, risk register, action/task tracker, meeting minutes, earned value management, team directory, punch list, permit-to-work/LOTO, plus commercial terms, Incoterms and PMBOK/PMP reference pages), and general-purpose tools (unit converter, site term translator, date/business-day calculator, markup/margin, fraction/decimal, number base converter). Most disciplines also have a learning-notes reference page covering the underlying theory.

Alongside the engineering disciplines, a separate Study Abroad section covers the practical side of studying overseas — IELTS/PTE/TOEFL score conversion, cost of living, education loan EMI, a simplified visa points estimate, university fee comparison, currency conversion, an SOP word checker, and an application timeline planner. It isn't engineering content and isn't held to the same code-reference standard as the calculators above — see the Terms & Disclaimer for its specific caveats.

There's also an Interview Prep section with discipline-specific and general/behavioral questions and model-answer talking points, a growing Blog of short technical articles, and a set of free browser games under Technical Games — Quickfire (calculation speed drills), the Daily Engineering Quiz, Symbol Flashcards, Symbol Match, plus visual troubleshooting games (Fault Finder, Defect Finder, Machine Fixer, HVAC Doctor, Signal Chain, Panel Doctor, Load Stack, Engineering Escape Room). All games are free with no signup required.

How to use it

Every calculator is self-contained on its own page — fill in the inputs, read the result. Most tools also show the underlying formula or a short worked note so the result isn't a black box. None of the figures here are a substitute for your own engineering judgement, applicable codes and standards, or a qualified reviewer's sign-off — treat every output as a first-pass sanity check, not a final answer.

The notes pages (one per discipline) exist for the moments a calculator alone doesn't answer the "why" — they walk through the underlying theory, typical ranges, and common mistakes, so you can sanity-check a result against reasoning, not just a number.

Who this is for

This started as a personal reference — the kind of quick-check tool an engineer reaches for during a site visit, a design review, or while prepping for an interview, when pulling out a full calculation package or a thick standard isn't practical. It's built for working engineers, students, site supervisors, and anyone studying for an interview or exam who wants a fast, no-signup way to check a number or refresh a concept.

It isn't built for, and shouldn't be used for, final design sign-off, stamped calculation packages, or any decision where getting it wrong has safety or financial consequences — that work belongs with a qualified, licensed engineer working from project-specific data.

How content gets added and updated

New calculators, notes, and posts are added and refined over time as gaps get noticed — a formula that needed a sanity-check tool, a concept that kept coming up, a topic worth a proper write-up. There's no fixed publishing schedule. The Blog and the homepage "Recent" section reflect what's shipped lately, and the RSS feed or the newsletter signup (see the Privacy Policy) are the two ways to follow new additions without having to check back manually.

Corrections matter more than volume here — if a formula, default value, or explanation looks off, that's exactly the kind of feedback this site wants to hear (see Contact below). Reference material is only useful if it's accurate.

Free, ad-supported, no account required

Every calculator, note, guide, and game on this site is free to use, with no account, signup, or paywall. The site is supported by advertising and covers its own hosting costs that way — see the Privacy Policy for how ads and analytics work here. The optional newsletter signup is exactly that: optional, and never required to use anything on the site.

Accuracy and limitations

These tools are built and checked carefully, but they're still simplified, general-purpose references — they use standard formulas and typical default coefficients, not project-specific data, manufacturer datasheets, or your local code amendments. See the Terms & Disclaimer for the full engineering disclaimer before relying on any figure from this site in a real design, purchase, installation, or safety decision.
