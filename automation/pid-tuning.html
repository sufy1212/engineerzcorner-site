PID Tuning (Reaction Curve)

Ziegler-Nichols open-loop method, from a FOPDT model
Automation Index AT.01 Signal Scaling AT.02 PID Tuning AT.03 Valve Sizing AT.04 Level Ranging AT.05 VFD Scaling AT.06 Notes
Starting-point gains from an open-loop step test: put the loop in manual, make a small step change in controller output, and read the process gain, time constant and dead time off the resulting reaction curve. Treat the results as a first pass to fine-tune from, not a final setting.

How this is calculated
P: Kp = τ/(K·L)  ·  PI: Kp = 0.9τ/(K·L), Ti = L/0.3  ·  PID: Kp = 1.2τ/(K·L), Ti = 2L, Td = 0.5L
Ziegler-Nichols open-loop (reaction-curve) tuning — tends to give an aggressive, somewhat oscillatory response. Treat these as a starting point and back off Kp (and/or open up Ti) if the loop overshoots or hunts in practice. Not suitable for processes without genuine dead time, or for pure integrating processes (e.g. most level loops).
First-pass tuning from an open-loop model — always verify with a bump test on the real loop before leaving it in auto.
