Full Automation Symbol Reference

Every symbol above, with its full meaning and typical usage in one scannable table — useful for a quick page search or side-by-side comparison instead of clicking through each tile individually.

SymbolCategoryMeaningWhere it's used
Field-Mounted InstrumentInstrument BubblesA bare circle — a discrete instrument physically installed in the field, not on a panel or in a control system.P&ID instrument bubbles at the actual sensor/transmitter location, tagged with its loop letters (e.g. PT-101).
Panel-Mounted InstrumentInstrument BubblesA circle split by a horizontal line — an instrument mounted on a local or main control panel, accessible to an operator.P&IDs to distinguish panel-front indicators/controllers from field-mounted devices at the same loop.
PLC/DCS FunctionInstrument BubblesA circle inscribed in a square — a software function block executed inside a PLC or DCS, with no dedicated physical hardware of its own.P&IDs and control narratives representing control logic (PID, alarms, interlocks) that lives in the controller's code.
Shared Display/ControlInstrument BubblesA hexagon-like flattened bubble — an instrument function shared across a common operator interface, such as one HMI screen serving several loops.P&IDs where several loops are viewed and adjusted from the same shared operator display rather than separate dedicated hardware.
Instrument Tag LettersInstrument BubblesAn instrument bubble carrying letter codes (first letter = measured variable, following letters = function) — e.g. PT = Pressure Transmitter, TIC = Temperature Indicating Controller.Every instrument bubble on a P&ID, to identify what it measures and what it does at a glance.
Pressure TransmitterField DevicesAn instrument bubble tagged PT with a process connection tail — converts process pressure into a transmitted signal (commonly 4-20mA).P&IDs and loop diagrams at every point process pressure is measured and transmitted to a controller or display.
Temperature TransmitterField DevicesAn instrument bubble tagged TT — converts a temperature sensor's signal (RTD, thermocouple) into a transmitted process signal.P&IDs at every temperature measurement point feeding a controller, recorder, or alarm.
Level TransmitterField DevicesAn instrument bubble tagged LT — measures and transmits liquid or solids level in a vessel, by DP, ultrasonic, radar, or another method.P&IDs at tank and vessel level measurement points, typically feeding a level controller or high/low alarm.
Flow TransmitterField DevicesAn instrument bubble tagged FT — converts a flow element's output into a transmitted flow rate signal.P&IDs immediately downstream of the flow element it's paired with, on the process line being measured.
Flow Element (Orifice Plate)Field DevicesA restriction plate shown in the flow line — creates a measurable pressure drop proportional to flow rate for a DP-based flow transmitter to read.P&IDs immediately upstream of a differential-pressure flow transmitter, on the line the flow is being measured.
ThermowellField DevicesA protective sleeve shown penetrating a pipe or vessel wall — lets a temperature sensor be inserted, removed, or replaced without breaking process containment.Piping and vessel drawings at every temperature measurement point, especially where the sensor may need servicing without a shutdown.
Control Valve (Globe)Final Control ElementsA bowtie shape — the general symbol for an in-line valve; combined with an actuator symbol above it, represents an automated control valve.P&IDs on any process line where flow is throttled automatically rather than by a hand valve.
Control Valve with ActuatorFinal Control ElementsA bowtie valve body topped with a diaphragm or actuator box — shows the valve is automatically positioned by a pneumatic, electric, or hydraulic actuator, not hand-operated.P&IDs to distinguish automated control valves from manual isolation valves in the same line.
Ball ValveFinal Control ElementsA circle in the flow line representing the ball's bore — a quarter-turn valve using a rotating bored ball to start, stop, or throttle flow.P&IDs and isolation valve schedules wherever a quick-acting shutoff or moderate throttling valve is specified.
Butterfly ValveFinal Control ElementsA circle (the body) with a diagonal line (the disc) — a quarter-turn valve using a rotating disc on a shaft to control flow.Large-diameter lines and HVAC/utility water systems where a lightweight, compact throttling valve is preferred.
Check ValveFinal Control ElementsA triangle pointing in the permitted flow direction inside the line — allows flow one way only, preventing backflow.Piping drawings immediately downstream of pumps and anywhere reverse flow must be prevented.
Solenoid ValveFinal Control ElementsA valve body with a coil symbol above it — an electrically actuated on/off valve, typically for pneumatic or hydraulic pilot service.P&IDs wherever a fast-acting electric on/off valve is used, often to pilot a larger actuator or for ESD service.
Diaphragm ActuatorFinal Control ElementsA dome shape mounted above a valve body — a pneumatic actuator that positions the valve stem in proportion to a control air signal.P&ID valve symbols to show the valve is pneumatically (not electrically or manually) actuated.
VFD / Motor DriveFinal Control ElementsA box enclosing a stylized frequency waveform — represents a variable frequency drive controlling motor speed.Electrical and P&ID drawings at every motor whose speed is modulated rather than run at fixed line frequency.
Pneumatic Signal LineSignal LinesA line with evenly spaced short hash marks — carries a pneumatic (compressed air) control signal, historically 3-15 psi.P&IDs for legacy or field-air-actuated instrumentation loops, distinguishing pneumatic from electric signal runs.
Electric Signal LineSignal LinesA long-dashed line — carries an electric analog or discrete signal, such as a 4-20mA current loop or a 24VDC discrete input.P&IDs and loop diagrams for the large majority of modern transmitter-to-controller and controller-to-actuator wiring.
Capillary/Hydraulic LineSignal LinesA finely dotted line — represents a filled capillary tube or hydraulic signal line, transmitting pressure through a liquid-filled system.Remote-seal pressure and level instruments, and hydraulically actuated final control elements.
Software/Data LinkSignal LinesA line with an alternating dash-dot pattern — represents a digital data link (fieldbus, network) rather than a discrete point-to-point signal.P&IDs and network architecture drawings showing which instruments communicate over a shared digital network.
Undefined SignalSignal LinesA generic broken line used before a specific signal type (pneumatic, electric, digital) has been finalized in early design.Preliminary or conceptual P&IDs, later replaced with the specific signal-type line once the design is confirmed.
Signal Direction ArrowSignal LinesAn arrowhead on a signal line — clarifies which direction a signal or process value actually flows through the loop.Loop diagrams and control narratives wherever signal direction isn't otherwise obvious from the drawing layout.
PID Controller BlockControllers & LogicAn instrument bubble tagged with the controller's algorithm — continuously adjusts a final control element based on proportional, integral, and derivative action on the error signal.Control narratives and P&IDs at the controller function of a modulating control loop.
On/Off ControllerControllers & LogicAn instrument bubble with a simple step-response icon — switches a final control element fully on or off at a setpoint, rather than modulating.Simple two-position control loops such as basic heaters, pumps, or level switches, where fine modulation isn't required.
Relay CoilControllers & LogicA rectangle in a control circuit — an electromechanical or solid-state switch, energized by a small control signal to switch a larger load circuit.Electrical schematics and ladder logic diagrams wherever discrete on/off switching logic is implemented in hardware.
Alarm (High/Low)Controllers & LogicAn instrument bubble tagged with an alarm function (HA = High Alarm, LA = Low Alarm) — notifies an operator when a process value crosses a set limit.P&IDs and alarm schedules at every point a process variable is monitored for an abnormal condition.
Interlock Logic (AND)Controllers & LogicA D-shaped logic gate — represents an AND function requiring every input condition true before the output (often a trip or permissive) is issued.Cause-and-effect diagrams and logic drawings for permissive, interlock, and shutdown logic.
Local Control PanelPanels & NetworksA bordered box enclosing panel-mounted instrument bubbles — represents a physical enclosure grouping several field-area instruments and controls together.P&IDs and layout drawings showing which instruments are grouped in a shared local panel versus mounted individually in the field.
Instrument Rack/Junction BoxPanels & NetworksA box with terminal points marked — a field enclosure where multiple instrument cables land and are grouped before running back to the control room.Cable schedules and field wiring drawings at the point several field devices' wiring is consolidated into a home-run cable.
Fieldbus/Network SegmentPanels & NetworksA trunk line with multiple device drops shown along it — a shared digital communication segment (Profibus, Foundation Fieldbus, Modbus) carrying several devices on one cable run.Network architecture drawings showing which instruments share a common digital bus segment rather than individual home-run wiring.
HMI/Operator StationPanels & NetworksA monitor/screen icon — the operator's software interface for viewing process values and issuing commands to the control system.Control system architecture diagrams at every point an operator interacts with the process through a screen rather than a physical panel.
Safety Instrumented FunctionSafetyA shield outline around a logic or instrument symbol — marks a function forming part of a Safety Instrumented System (SIS), independent of the basic process control system.P&IDs and safety requirement specifications to clearly separate SIS-rated loops from ordinary process control loops.
Emergency Shutdown ValveSafetyA control valve symbol with a fail-safe indicator — a valve that drives to its safe position (typically fail-closed or fail-open) on loss of signal or power.P&IDs at isolation points that must automatically reach a safe state during an emergency shutdown event.
Relief/Safety Valve (PSV)SafetyA triangular spring-loaded valve symbol — opens automatically at a set pressure to protect equipment from overpressure, independent of the control system.P&IDs on vessels and lines wherever overpressure protection is required, typically piped to a flare or safe location.
