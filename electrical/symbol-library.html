Full Electrical Symbol Reference

Every symbol above, with its full meaning and typical usage in one scannable table — useful for a quick page search or side-by-side comparison instead of clicking through each tile individually.

SymbolCategoryMeaningWhere it's used
Circuit BreakerProtectionAutomatically interrupts current on overload or fault, and can be manually opened/closed for isolation.Drawn on single-line and schematic diagrams at every protected feeder, board incomer and outgoing way.
FuseProtectionA one-time sacrificial overcurrent device — melts and opens the circuit above its rated current, then must be replaced.Common on the primary side of small transformers, control circuit protection, and cost-sensitive branch circuits.
Earth / GroundProtectionRepresents a connection to earth ground, providing a fault current return path and touch-voltage protection.Shown at equipment frames, neutral-earth links, lightning protection down-conductors and earth electrodes.
TransformerPowerTwo magnetically coupled windings (shown as overlapping circles) that step voltage up or down between two circuits.Used on single-line diagrams at every voltage transformation point, from utility transformers down to control-power transformers.
MotorLoadsA circle enclosing the letter M — the standard symbol for any electric motor load on a single-line diagram.Placed at the end of a feeder to represent a pump, fan, compressor or other motor-driven load.
GeneratorPowerA circle enclosing the letter G — represents any rotating electrical generation source.Used for standby/genset units, alternators and any other rotating source feeding the system.
CapacitorComponentsTwo parallel plates (drawn as parallel lines) that store energy in an electric field and supply reactive power.Shown in power factor correction banks and as a component in filter and snubber circuits.
Push Button (N.O.)ControlA normally-open contact, drawn open with a diagonal actuator bar — closes only while the button is pressed.Start buttons on motor control stations and momentary command inputs on control panels.
Push Button (N.C.)ControlA normally-closed contact, drawn as a continuous line crossed by the actuator — opens while the button is pressed.Stop buttons and emergency-stop circuits, where the safe state is the closed (current-flowing) state.
Lamp / IndicatorControlA circle with an internal cross — the standard symbol for an indicating lamp or pilot light.Panel-mounted run/trip/fault indication, and status lamps on motor control centers.
Disconnect / IsolatorProtectionA manually operated open-blade switch with no fault-breaking capacity — for isolation only, not switching under load.Upstream of equipment needing a visible, lockable isolation point for maintenance.
BatteryPowerAlternating long and short parallel lines — each pair represents one cell of a DC storage battery.DC control power supplies, UPS battery banks and standby/backup power schematics.
MCCBProtectionA molded-case circuit breaker — drawn like a breaker but sized larger, for higher current ratings than a miniature breaker.Main incomers and large feeder protection on distribution boards and switchgear.
Overload RelayProtectionA thermal or electronic sensing device — trips a contactor on sustained overcurrent to protect a motor winding.Wired in series with a motor's line current, paired with a contactor in a direct-on-line starter.
Surge ArresterProtectionA voltage-limiting device shunting transient overvoltage to earth — protects downstream equipment from lightning and switching surges.Installed at incoming service points and ahead of sensitive electronic loads.
RCD / ELCBProtectionA residual-current device — trips on earth-leakage current imbalance, protecting against electric shock rather than overcurrent.Socket outlet circuits, bathrooms and any circuit needing shock protection beyond overcurrent devices.
Lightning RodProtectionAn elevated conductor with a down-conductor path to earth — intercepts a lightning strike before it reaches the structure.Roof-level air terminals on lightning protection system layouts.
Bus DuctPowerAn enclosed metal duct carrying busbars — a rigid, high-current alternative to cable for short power runs.Transformer-to-switchboard connections and vertical riser power distribution in multi-storey buildings.
UPSPowerAn uninterruptible power supply — an enclosure symbol with a lightning bolt, providing ride-through backup power on mains failure.Critical loads such as control systems, servers and life-safety equipment needing zero-break backup.
Static Transfer SwitchPowerA solid-state switch selecting between two live sources — transfers load between them within milliseconds, no break in supply.Dual-fed critical loads needing faster switchover than a mechanical automatic transfer switch can provide.
Reactor / InductorPowerA coiled-line symbol — a series inductor limiting fault current or smoothing harmonics.Current-limiting reactors ahead of switchgear, and harmonic filter reactors in drive systems.
RectifierPowerA box enclosing an AC-to-DC waveform symbol — converts alternating current to direct current.Battery charger front ends, DC drive input stages and rectifier sections of UPS systems.
Heater LoadLoadsA rectangle with internal zig-zag or resistive element marks — a fixed resistive heating load.Space heaters, immersion heaters and process heating elements on load schedules.
LuminaireLoadsA circle with a downward tail — a lighting fixture or luminaire outlet.Reflected ceiling plans and lighting layout drawings at every fitting location.
Socket OutletLoadsA circle with two internal pin marks — a general power socket outlet.Power layout drawings at every wall or floor socket location.
ContactorControlAn electrically operated switch — repeatedly makes/breaks a power circuit under control-signal command.Motor starters, lighting contactors and any circuit switched remotely rather than by a local manual switch.
Timer RelayControlA clock-face symbol — a relay whose contacts operate after a preset time delay rather than instantly.Star-delta starter transition timing, delayed-start sequencing and off-delay lighting control.
Limit SwitchControlA mechanically actuated switch with a roller/lever arm — changes state when a moving part reaches a physical limit.End-of-travel detection on doors, gates, conveyors and linear actuators.
Selector SwitchControlA circle with an angled pointer — a multi-position manual switch for mode or hand/off/auto selection.Control panel front faces for local/remote and manual/auto mode selection.
Emergency StopControlA double-circle mushroom-head button symbol — a latching, normally-closed safety stop control.Machine control stations and panels wherever an immediate, fail-safe stop function is required.
Proximity SensorControlA sensor body with radiating detection arcs — a non-contact switch detecting the presence of a nearby object.Conveyor and machine control for position or presence detection without physical contact.
DiodeComponentsA triangle pointing into a bar — allows current to flow in one direction only.Rectifier circuits, freewheel/flyback protection across coils, and polarity protection.
ResistorComponentsA zig-zag line — the general symbol for a resistive circuit element.Schematic diagrams wherever a discrete resistor limits current or drops voltage.
Terminal BlockComponentsA row of numbered terminals — the physical connection point between field wiring and panel wiring.Wiring diagrams and panel schedules at every field-to-panel cable termination.
Junction BoxComponentsA square box divided into quadrants — a sealed enclosure for joining or branching cables.Cable routes wherever conductors are joined, tapped or need a maintenance access point.
Cable TrayComponentsParallel horizontal lines — an open supporting structure for routing multiple cables together.Cable routing drawings and riser sections showing the containment path between rooms and floors.
AmmeterMeasurementA circle enclosing the letter A — a current-measuring instrument.Panel meters and CT secondary circuits reading load current.
VoltmeterMeasurementA circle enclosing the letter V — a voltage-measuring instrument.Panel meters and busbar voltage monitoring points on switchboards.
Current TransformerMeasurementA ring core around the primary conductor — steps a large primary current down to a standard secondary for metering/protection.Around incomer and feeder conductors, feeding ammeters, protection relays and energy meters.
Energy MeterMeasurementAn enclosure labeled kWh — records cumulative energy consumption over time.Utility revenue metering points and sub-metering for tenant or departmental billing.
Distribution BoardPowerA rectangular enclosure with multiple outgoing ways — splits an incoming supply into several protected final circuits.Single-line diagrams at every panelboard, distribution board and final-circuit distribution point.
Automatic Transfer SwitchPowerTwo sources feeding a common switch symbol — automatically transfers a load between normal and standby supply.Standby generator systems, switching essential loads from utility to genset on mains failure.
