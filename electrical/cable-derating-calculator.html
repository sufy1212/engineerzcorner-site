How this is calculated
Ambient temperature correction: Ca = √[(θp − θa) / (θp − θa,ref)], θp = max conductor temp (70°C PVC / 90°C XLPE).
Combined factor: C = Ca × Cg × Cs × Cd × Cx
Effective ampacity: Iz = Iz0 × C — must be ≥ your design load current, Ib.
Only combines factors you supply — it doesn't look up Cg, Cs or Cd from a table itself, since those depend on installation method and standard. Cross-check against the base ampacity source you're using.

About Cable Derating

A cable's nameplate or table ampacity (Iz0) assumes a specific set of reference conditions — a standard ambient temperature, a single cable in free air, no other circuits nearby. Real installations rarely match that exactly, so each departure from the reference condition gets its own correction factor: hotter ambient reduces the rating, cables bunched together in a group reduce it further, and cables buried in soil pick up factors for thermal resistivity and depth. Multiply them all together and apply that combined factor to Iz0, and you get the effective ampacity the cable can actually carry in its real installation.

This calculator handles the temperature correction with the standard formula and lets you drop in whichever other factors apply from your cable manufacturer's data or IEC 60364-5-52, so you get one combined number and a clear pass/fail against your design load current — instead of multiplying four or five factors by hand and hoping none got missed.
