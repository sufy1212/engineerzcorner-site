Two checks per cable: (1) can it carry the load current continuously once you derate for temperature and grouping, and (2) can it survive the fault current for as long as the breaker takes to clear it. Base ampacity (Iz0) — the un-derated current rating for your cable's size, insulation and installation method — isn't calculated here; pull it from your cable manufacturer's datasheet or IEC 60364-5-52 Table B.52.4, since that's exact standardised data I'd rather not guess at. Everything downstream of that (temperature correction, grouping, thermal withstand) is proper formula-based math. Working out the combined grouping/burial/ambient factor (Cg here) from several separate correction factors? Use the Cable Derating Factor calculator and bring the result back to this field. How this is calculated
Ambient temperature correction: Ca = √[(θp − θa) / (θp − θa,ref)], θp = max conductor temp (70°C PVC / 90°C XLPE).
Corrected ampacity: Iz = Iz0 × Ca × Cg — must be ≥ your design load current, Ib.
Thermal short-circuit withstand (IEC 60364-5-54 / IEC 60949): S required = (I × √t) / k, where I = fault current (A), t = disconnection time (s), k = material/insulation constant.
k values used here: Cu-PVC 115, Cu-XLPE 143, Al-PVC 76, Al-XLPE 94 — these are the standard widely-published IEC constants; confirm against your cable's actual insulation class if it's unusual.
Doesn't apply soil thermal resistivity correction for buried cables, or the grouping-factor tables themselves (you enter Cg directly) — add those from your reference table if relevant to your installation.
