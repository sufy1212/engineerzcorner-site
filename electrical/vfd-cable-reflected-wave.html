VFD Cable Length / Reflected Wave Calculator

Estimate transmission-line voltage doubling at the motor terminals for a given drive, cable and run length
A PWM drive's fast output edges turn a long motor cable into a transmission line: when the cable run is longer than a "critical length" set by the drive's rise time and the cable's propagation velocity, the impedance mismatch at the motor reflects the pulse back and forth, and the motor can see a peak voltage approaching twice the drive's DC bus voltage — well above the sine-wave RMS rating and enough to stress winding insulation over time. This tool estimates that critical length and the resulting peak motor terminal voltage from your drive and cable inputs, as a first-pass screening check.

How this is calculated
L_crit = (v × t_r) / 2  ·  V_bus ≈ 1.41 × V_LL  ·  for L < L_crit: V_peak ≈ V_bus×(1 + L/L_crit)  ·  for L ≥ L_crit: V_peak ≈ 2 × V_bus
This models the classic transmission-line reflection case: the drive-end impedance is a near-short relative to the cable (large bus capacitance), and the motor-end impedance is high relative to the cable's surge impedance at PWM edge frequencies, so the reflected pulse adds to the incident one and voltage ramps toward double the DC bus voltage as cable length reaches the critical length. It is a first-pass screening estimate, not a substitute for an oscilloscope measurement or the drive manufacturer's own cable-length tables — real installations can see additional ringing overshoot beyond simple doubling from underdamped LC resonance, multiple motors on one feeder, or unterminated cable ends, and long-cable derating tables from drive vendors already build in their own safety margin. If the estimated peak approaches or exceeds the NEMA reference, or the cable run is close to or beyond the critical length, consider a motor-rated (dV/dt or sine-wave) output filter, an inverter-duty motor per NEMA MG1 Part 31 / IEC 60034-25, or shortening the run.
