PM Fundamentals

Core PM concepts used to check you understand the framework, not just the vocabulary.
QuestionAnswer
What are the main project management knowledge areas or process groups?Process groups run initiating, planning, executing, monitoring & controlling, and closing; alongside these, knowledge areas cover scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, communications, risk, procurement, stakeholders and integration.
What is the difference between a project and a program?A project is a temporary endeavor with a defined start/end delivering a specific output; a program is a group of related projects managed together to realize benefits not achievable by managing them individually.
What is the triple constraint (iron triangle) and how do the three elements interact?Scope, time and cost are interdependent — changing one without adjusting the others affects quality; e.g., compressing schedule usually raises cost or cuts scope, and this trade-off is what a PM actively manages.
What's the difference between a project charter and a project management plan?The charter is a short document authorizing the project and naming the PM with high-level objectives and authority; the management plan is the detailed, evolving document covering how scope, schedule, cost, risk, etc. will actually be managed and executed.
Explain the difference between a stakeholder register and a RACI matrix.A stakeholder register identifies who has an interest in the project and their influence/expectations; a RACI matrix assigns specific Responsible/Accountable/Consulted/Informed roles for individual tasks or deliverables — one is about people, the other about task ownership.
What is scope creep and how do you prevent it?Uncontrolled expansion of project scope without corresponding adjustments to time/cost/resources; prevented through a clear scope baseline, formal change control process, and disciplined stakeholder communication about what's in and out of scope.
What is the difference between a milestone and a deliverable?A deliverable is a tangible output produced during the project (a document, a completed system, a report); a milestone is a significant point in time marking progress (often tied to completion of one or more deliverables) with zero duration itself.
What's the difference between qualitative and quantitative risk analysis?Qualitative analysis ranks risks by relative probability and impact (often via a probability-impact matrix) to prioritize response effort; quantitative analysis assigns numerical values (e.g., expected monetary value, Monte Carlo simulation) to model overall project risk exposure.
What is the difference between a risk and an issue?A risk is an uncertain future event that may or may not occur; an issue is something that has already happened and now needs a response — risks are tracked in a risk register, issues in an issue/action log.
What does "baseline" mean in project management?An approved reference version of the scope, schedule, or cost plan against which actual performance is measured; changes to it go through formal change control rather than being edited freely.

Planning & Controls

Scheduling, cost control and reporting — the practical backbone of most PM interviews on engineering projects.
QuestionAnswer
What is the critical path and why does it matter?The longest sequence of dependent tasks through the schedule that determines the minimum project duration; any delay on the critical path delays the whole project, while tasks with float can slip without impact — which is why it drives where a PM focuses attention.
Explain earned value management (EVM) in simple terms.It compares planned value (budgeted cost of scheduled work), earned value (budgeted cost of work actually completed) and actual cost, letting you calculate schedule and cost variances/performance indexes (SV, CV, SPI, CPI) to objectively track whether a project is ahead/behind and under/over budget.
What is float (slack) in a schedule?The amount of time a task can be delayed without delaying a downstream milestone (free float) or the overall project finish (total float); critical path tasks have zero total float by definition.
What's the difference between a WBS and a schedule?The Work Breakdown Structure decomposes total scope into manageable deliverables/work packages (what needs to be done); the schedule sequences that same work in time with durations and dependencies (when it will be done).
How do you handle a change request on an active project?Log it formally, assess impact on scope/schedule/cost/risk, route it through the change control board or agreed approval authority, and only update baselines once it's formally approved — never absorb changes informally.
What is a RACI matrix used for and how do you build one?It clarifies exactly who is Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome), Consulted (gives input), and Informed (kept updated) for each task or deliverable — built by listing key activities against stakeholders and assigning exactly one Accountable per row to avoid ambiguity.
What's the difference between a risk register and a risk response plan?The risk register lists identified risks with their probability, impact and owner; the response plan defines the specific strategy for each (avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept) and the actions/triggers tied to it.
How do you track progress on a project with multiple contractors?Standardize a common reporting format/frequency across contractors, tie progress claims to verifiable milestones or measured quantities rather than self-reported percentages, and reconcile schedule/cost updates against the master program regularly.
What is the purpose of a punch list at project close-out?It records outstanding defects or incomplete items identified during inspection/handover so they can be tracked to closure before final acceptance, keeping completion criteria explicit and disputes to a minimum.
How do Incoterms affect a project's procurement and risk exposure?They define exactly where responsibility, cost and risk for goods in transit transfer from seller to buyer (e.g., FOB vs DDP), which directly affects who arranges insurance/freight and where liability sits if goods are damaged or delayed.

Site & Practical Scenarios

How you think on your feet during delivery — often matters more than textbook recall.
QuestionAnswer
A project is falling behind schedule — how do you respond?Identify whether the slippage is on the critical path, evaluate fast-tracking (overlapping tasks) or crashing (adding resources) options and their cost/risk trade-offs, communicate the impact transparently to stakeholders, and update the recovery plan with a revised forecast rather than hoping it self-corrects.
Two contractors disagree over responsibility for a delay — how do you handle it?Go back to the contract and the as-built program to establish facts objectively, review notices/correspondence for who flagged what and when, and mediate toward a documented resolution rather than assigning blame informally — this is also why contemporaneous records matter throughout the project.
How would you run a project kickoff meeting?Confirm scope, objectives and success criteria are understood the same way by everyone, introduce the team and RACI, walk through the high-level schedule and key milestones, and align on communication/reporting cadence before work starts.
How do you manage a stakeholder who keeps requesting scope additions informally?Acknowledge the request without committing, route it through the formal change control process so impact is assessed and approved before work starts, and reinforce (without confrontation) why informal changes put schedule/cost/quality at risk.
What would you do if a critical permit or approval is delayed?Assess knock-on effects on the critical path immediately, look for sequencing work that doesn't depend on the permit to keep progress moving, escalate through the right channel with a clear ask, and update stakeholders on the revised timeline rather than waiting silently.
How do you run an effective progress/status meeting?Focus on exceptions and decisions needed rather than reading through a full status report line by line, track open actions from the previous meeting to closure, and keep it time-boxed with clear owners and dates coming out of every discussion point.
How would you handle a safety incident on site as the project lead?Ensure immediate safety/first aid response takes priority over everything else, secure the scene and preserve evidence, follow the incident reporting and investigation procedure, and communicate transparently with stakeholders while the root cause investigation runs.
How do you keep a multi-discipline project's interfaces from falling through the cracks?Maintain an explicit interface register naming what's needed from whom and by when across disciplines, review it at a regular cadence (not just when problems appear), and assign clear ownership for each interface point rather than leaving it as everyone's shared responsibility.
How would you close out a project and capture lessons learned?Verify all deliverables/punch items are accepted, reconcile final costs against budget, formally hand over documentation/warranties, and run a lessons-learned session while it's fresh — capturing what should change next time, not just what went well.
