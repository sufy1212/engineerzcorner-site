Process Fundamentals

P&IDs, fluid behavior and the core process concepts that come up constantly.
QuestionAnswer
What's the difference between a PFD and a P&ID?A Process Flow Diagram (PFD) shows the major equipment, process streams and material balance at a high level; a Piping & Instrumentation Diagram (P&ID) is the detailed engineering document showing every line, valve, instrument and control loop needed to build and operate the system.
What does an instrument bubble on a P&ID tell you?The letters inside identify the measured variable and function (e.g., FT = flow transmitter, PIC = pressure indicating controller), and the bubble's border style (solid, dashed, or with a line through it) indicates whether it's field-mounted, panel-mounted, or part of a DCS/PLC system.
What is the purpose of the legend sheet in a P&ID package?It defines every symbol, line type, abbreviation and instrument tag convention used across the drawing set, so any engineer reading the P&IDs interprets them consistently rather than guessing at site- or company-specific notation.
Explain the difference between cavitation and flashing in a control valve.Cavitation occurs when local pressure drops below vapor pressure inside the valve and then recovers above it downstream, so vapor bubbles form and violently collapse (causing noise and damage); flashing occurs when the downstream pressure stays below vapor pressure, so the vapor bubbles that form never collapse — flashing is less damaging to the valve itself but still affects capacity and can cause erosion.
What is NPSH and why does it matter for pump selection?Net Positive Suction Head is the margin of pressure above the fluid's vapor pressure available at the pump suction; if NPSH available falls below NPSH required by the pump, the fluid vaporizes at the impeller eye and collapses downstream, causing cavitation, noise, and accelerated wear.
What's the difference between erosional velocity and economic velocity in line sizing?Erosional velocity is the upper limit above which the fluid (especially if it carries solids or is two-phase) starts to erode pipe walls and fittings — a hard constraint; economic velocity is the range that balances pipe/material cost against pumping/pressure-drop cost — a design optimization within the erosional limit.
Why is a control valve typically sized so it's not fully open at normal operating flow?Leaving margin (commonly running 60-80% open at normal flow) preserves the ability to increase flow when needed and keeps the valve working in its more linear, controllable range — a valve running near 100% open loses the ability to compensate for upset conditions.
What is the difference between a relief valve and a safety valve, and where does a PSV fit in?Historically a "relief valve" opens proportionally to overpressure (typical for liquid service) while a "safety valve" pops fully open at setpoint (typical for compressible/gas service); a PSV (Pressure Safety Valve) is the general term used for the spring-loaded device that protects equipment from overpressure, whichever behavior applies to the service.
What's the difference between a conventional, bellows, and pilot-operated PSV?A conventional PSV's set pressure is affected by backpressure since the spring chamber is vented to the outlet side; a bellows PSV isolates the spring chamber from backpressure using a bellows, keeping the set pressure accurate even with variable backpressure; a pilot-operated PSV uses a small pilot valve to control a larger main valve, giving tighter set-pressure accuracy and better performance at high operating-to-set-pressure ratios.
What is turndown ratio for a control valve, and why does it matter?It's the ratio between the maximum flow the valve can pass and the minimum flow it can still control accurately; a valve with poor turndown loses controllability at low flow, so selecting a valve (and trim) with adequate turndown for the full expected operating range is part of proper sizing.
What's the difference between a globe valve and a ball valve, and when would you choose each for control?A globe valve throttles well across its full stroke and is the default choice for continuous modulating control; a ball valve (especially segmented/V-notch ball) is more common for on/off isolation or where high capacity and lower pressure drop matter, though characterized ball valves are also used for control in some services.
What does "line sizing" actually balance, and what are the typical constraints?It balances pressure drop, velocity limits (erosional and, for two-phase or slurry lines, minimum velocity to avoid settling), noise, and capital cost of larger pipe against pumping/compression energy cost — sized to keep velocity within the allowable range for the fluid and service.
What is dead-leg in a piping system and why is it a concern?A dead-leg is a branch or section of pipe with little or no flow (e.g., an unused tie-in or an infrequently used bypass); it's a concern because stagnant fluid there can lead to corrosion, contamination, freezing, or — in hazardous service — an unmonitored pocket of trapped material.
What's the purpose of a knockout drum or separator upstream of a compressor or flare?It removes entrained liquid droplets from a gas stream before the gas reaches equipment (like a compressor) that could be damaged by liquid carryover, or before it reaches a flare where liquid could cause burning issues or environmental non-compliance.
What's the difference between batch and continuous process operation?Continuous processes run steady-state with material flowing through the system constantly, well suited to high, consistent-volume production; batch processes process a fixed quantity of material through a sequence of steps before starting the next batch, better suited to lower volumes, multiple products on shared equipment, or processes needing discrete quality control per batch.
Why do intrinsically safe and explosion-proof equipment ratings exist for process instrumentation?Process areas with flammable gases, vapors, or dust require equipment that either can't generate enough energy to ignite the atmosphere (intrinsically safe) or that safely contains any internal ignition within a rugged enclosure (explosion-proof/flameproof) — selecting the right protection type is based on the area's hazardous classification (zone/division and gas group).

Equipment & Sizing

Heat exchangers, vessels, pumps and the equipment-selection questions.
QuestionAnswer
What is LMTD and why is it used for heat exchanger sizing?Log Mean Temperature Difference is the effective average temperature driving force across a heat exchanger, calculated logarithmically rather than arithmetically because the temperature difference between hot and cold streams changes non-linearly along the exchanger length — it's combined with the overall heat transfer coefficient and required duty to size the heat transfer area.
What's the difference between counter-current and co-current (parallel) flow in a heat exchanger?Counter-current flow runs the two fluids in opposite directions, giving a higher effective LMTD and allowing the cold stream to be heated closer to the hot stream's inlet temperature; co-current flow runs them in the same direction, which is simpler in some geometries but always gives a lower LMTD for the same terminal temperatures, so counter-current is generally preferred for efficiency.
What's the difference between a shell-and-tube and a plate heat exchanger?Shell-and-tube exchangers handle higher pressures/temperatures and are easier to clean mechanically on the tube side, making them common for high-duty or fouling services; plate exchangers give a much higher heat transfer area per unit volume and are more compact and efficient for a given duty, but are typically limited to lower pressure/temperature ratings.
How do you size a relief valve (PSV) at a basic level?Identify the worst-case overpressure scenario (fire, blocked outlet, control valve failure, etc.), calculate the required relief rate for that scenario, then select the orifice size using the applicable sizing standard (e.g., API 520/521) so the valve can pass that flow at the allowable accumulated pressure.
What is dilution purge gas sizing meant to achieve?It determines the purge gas flow rate needed to keep a vessel, duct, or enclosure's atmosphere diluted below a target concentration (often a fraction of the lower flammable limit) by continuously displacing the contaminant or flammable gas, rather than relying on a fixed volume exchange.
How do you determine pipe wall thickness per ASME B31.3, at a basic level?Calculate the pressure-design thickness from design pressure, pipe outside diameter, allowable stress for the material/temperature, and a weld joint efficiency factor, then add a corrosion allowance and round up to the nearest available (and typically next-standard) wall schedule.
What's the difference between a Coriolis, magnetic, and DP orifice flow meter, from a selection standpoint?A Coriolis meter gives direct, highly accurate mass flow with no dependence on fluid properties but costs more and has line-size/pressure-drop limits; a magmeter is low-maintenance and accurate but only works on conductive liquids; a DP orifice meter is simple and inexpensive but has more pressure loss, a limited turndown, and requires straight-run piping for accuracy.
What determines whether a vessel needs a vortex breaker at its outlet nozzle?A vortex breaker prevents a swirling vortex from forming as liquid level drops near the outlet, which could otherwise pull vapor or gas down into the outlet line — needed whenever the outlet is submerged and low-level operation is expected, especially on pump suction nozzles.
What's the difference between a rupture disc and a spring-loaded PSV, and when is a rupture disc preferred?A rupture disc bursts at a set pressure and stays open (no reclosing), giving a fast, full-bore response with no moving parts to stick, but requires replacement after each activation and doesn't reseat; it's preferred for very fast pressure spikes, corrosive/fouling service where a PSV's moving parts would be unreliable, or as a backup ahead of a PSV to protect it from the process.
What's the purpose of a spectacle blind (spade) in a piping system?It's a two-sided plate — one solid, one open (a ring) — bolted into a flanged joint so a line can be positively, visibly isolated (blind side in) or reconnected (ring side in) without relying on a valve alone, which is important where a guaranteed physical block is required, such as before maintenance.
How do you decide between a globe control valve and a butterfly valve for a modulating service?A globe valve gives better control precision, tighter shutoff, and handles higher pressure drops, making it the default for demanding control duty; a butterfly valve is lower cost, lighter, and works well for large-diameter, lower-pressure-drop services where precise control isn't as critical.
What is the significance of the vapor pressure margin in vessel and pump system design?Keeping operating pressure comfortably above the fluid's vapor pressure at operating temperature prevents unwanted flashing/cavitation in vessels, pump suction lines, and control valves — the available margin directly drives NPSH available and flashing risk calculations.
What's the difference between gauge, absolute, and differential pressure measurement, and when would a process use each?Gauge pressure (relative to atmosphere) is used for most general process monitoring; absolute pressure (relative to a vacuum) is needed for vacuum service or where atmospheric variation would introduce error; differential pressure measures the difference between two points and is used for flow measurement (via orifice/DP element), level measurement (via head), and filter/strainer fouling monitoring.
What's the basic difference between API 650 and API 620 tanks?API 650 covers low-pressure, atmospheric (or near-atmospheric) welded storage tanks, the more common case for bulk liquid storage; API 620 covers tanks designed for higher internal pressures than API 650 allows, using a different design approach for the added pressure rating.
Why might a process specify a venturi meter instead of a simple orifice plate for flow measurement?A venturi has a gradual contraction and expansion rather than a sharp-edged restriction, giving significantly lower permanent pressure loss for the same differential signal — worth the extra cost where pumping/compression energy savings or a limited available pressure drop matter.

Site & Practical Scenarios

Commissioning, troubleshooting and P&ID-reading on live systems.
QuestionAnswer
A control valve is making unusually loud noise and the downstream piping shows pitting damage — what's likely happening?Cavitation is the likely cause — pressure recovering above vapor pressure downstream of the vena contracta causes vapor bubbles to collapse violently against the valve trim and downstream pipe wall; the fix usually involves anti-cavitation trim, relocating the pressure drop across multiple stages, or changing the operating point to reduce the pressure ratio across the valve.
A centrifugal pump is cavitating intermittently — what would you check first?Check NPSH available against NPSH required at the actual operating flow (not just design flow), since cavitation often shows up only at higher-than-design flow rates where NPSH required rises and NPSH available may drop — also check for a partially closed suction valve, a clogged strainer, or suction line losses higher than assumed.
How would you read a P&ID to trace a control loop from field to final control element?Start at the instrument bubble on the process line (e.g., a transmitter), follow its tag through the legend to confirm signal type, trace the signal line to the controller (panel or DCS symbol), then follow the controller's output signal line to the final control element (valve or damper) and confirm its fail-safe position and any associated interlocks shown on the same drawing.
A relief valve keeps chattering (rapidly opening and closing) in service — what usually causes this and how would you address it?Chattering is often caused by excessive inlet pressure drop (undersized inlet piping) or the valve being oversized for the actual relieving scenario, both of which cause the valve to open, quickly relieve the small pressure buildup, and reseat repeatedly; the fix is checking inlet line sizing against the 3% pressure drop guideline and reviewing whether the valve is correctly sized for the actual case.
How would you troubleshoot a heat exchanger that's no longer meeting its design duty?Check for fouling (reduced U-value) by comparing actual approach temperatures against design, verify actual flow rates and inlet temperatures match design assumptions, and inspect for issues like bypass flow, trapped air/vapor blocking a section of tubes, or a partially plugged bundle.
A vessel's level transmitter and local gauge glass disagree — how do you investigate?Check the transmitter's zero/span calibration against actual reference points, confirm the transmitter and gauge glass taps are at the same elevation, check for a blocked impulse line or trapped gas/vapor pocket in a DP-type transmitter's wet leg, and verify neither device is reading a foam or interface layer differently than the other.
What's your process for a safe line-break on a process pipeline?Isolate the line (double block and bleed or a spectacle blind where required), depressurize and drain/purge to remove hazardous material, verify zero energy with a gas test or pressure check as applicable, apply lockout/tagout on the isolation valves, and follow the site's permit-to-work process before breaking the joint.
A control valve won't hold the commanded position under process upset conditions — what would you check?Verify actuator air/hydraulic supply pressure is adequate under upset flow demand, check the positioner's tuning and calibration against actual stroke, inspect for excessive friction/stiction in the packing or trim, and confirm the valve isn't simply undersized (running near 100% open) for the upset flow condition.
How would you approach commissioning a new process line before introducing hazardous fluid?Complete mechanical punch-list closure and hydrotest/pressure test, flush and clean the line to remove construction debris, purge with inert gas to remove air/oxygen where required, verify instrument loop checks and interlocks function correctly, and follow a documented pre-commissioning checklist with sign-off before any hazardous material is introduced.
What would you check if a flow measurement seems to read consistently low compared to the actual delivered volume?Check for a partially blocked upstream strainer or a straight-run requirement not being met (causing distorted flow profile at the meter), confirm the meter's calibration and configured pipe/fluid parameters are correct, and check for entrained gas or two-phase flow if the meter technology assumes a single-phase liquid.
A gas detector in a process area keeps giving nuisance alarms — how would you approach it, without just silencing the alarm?Verify the sensor is calibrated and within its service life, check for a legitimate low-level source (a minor leak, purge vent, or nearby activity) before assuming it's a false alarm, and review whether the alarm setpoint and sensor location are appropriate for the actual hazard — disabling or ignoring the alarm without investigating defeats the purpose of the detector.
How would you verify a pressure safety valve is actually set correctly before returning it to service after maintenance?Bench-test the valve on a test stand to confirm it opens (pops) at its stamped set pressure and reseats properly, check that the nameplate/tag data matches the required set pressure for that service, and ensure test documentation is completed before reinstalling — visual inspection alone doesn't confirm set pressure.
What would you check if two redundant level transmitters on the same vessel disagree beyond an acceptable tolerance?Cross-check both against a local level gauge or independent reference, check for a difference in mounting elevation or impulse line condition (trapped gas, plugged line) that could explain a real offset, and review the voting/selection logic to confirm it isn't silently picking a faulty reading.
A batch process is showing inconsistent product quality between batches — where would you start investigating?Check whether raw material specs and charge quantities were consistent between batches, review whether process parameters (temperature, time, pressure) actually tracked the recipe/setpoints during each run, and confirm instrumentation used to control the batch wasn't out of calibration or drifting during the period in question.
