Thermodynamics & Fluid Mechanics

Baseline theory that comes up across HVAC, process and machine-design roles alike.
QuestionAnswer
State the first and second laws of thermodynamics in plain terms.First law: energy is conserved — heat in minus work out equals the change in internal energy. Second law: heat flows from hot to cold on its own, and no real process converts heat to work at 100% efficiency (entropy of an isolated system doesn't decrease).
What's the difference between enthalpy and internal energy?Internal energy (U) is the energy stored in a system; enthalpy (H = U + PV) also accounts for the flow work needed to push fluid into/out of a system — enthalpy is the more useful property for open, flow-based processes.
Explain the difference between laminar and turbulent flow.Laminar flow moves in smooth parallel layers (low Reynolds number, Re < ~2300 in pipes); turbulent flow is chaotic with mixing and eddies (Re > ~4000); the transition affects friction losses and heat transfer significantly.
What is NPSH and why does it matter for pump selection?Net Positive Suction Head is the pressure margin above vapor pressure available at the pump suction; if available NPSH falls below the pump's required NPSH, the fluid flashes to vapor and cavitates, damaging the impeller.
Explain the difference between heat transfer by conduction, convection and radiation.Conduction is transfer through direct contact in a solid/still fluid; convection is transfer via fluid motion (natural or forced); radiation is transfer via electromagnetic waves and needs no medium.
What is the difference between stress and strain?Stress is internal force per unit area (Pa) resisting an applied load; strain is the resulting fractional deformation (dimensionless) — the ratio of stress to strain in the elastic region is the material's modulus of elasticity.
What is the difference between an isothermal and adiabatic process?An isothermal process happens at constant temperature (heat is exchanged to keep temperature stable as pressure/volume change); an adiabatic process has no heat transfer at all (temperature changes purely from work done on/by the system).
Explain the difference between mass flow rate and volumetric flow rate.Mass flow rate (kg/s) is the mass passing a point per unit time; volumetric flow rate (m³/s) is the volume passing per unit time — they're related by density (mass rate = density × volumetric rate), so volumetric flow can change with temperature/pressure while mass flow stays constant in a closed system.
What is the continuity equation in fluid mechanics?For incompressible flow in a pipe, A1V1 = A2V2 — the product of cross-sectional area and velocity stays constant along the flow, so velocity increases where the pipe narrows.
What's the difference between a Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluid?A Newtonian fluid (water, air) has a constant viscosity regardless of shear rate; a non-Newtonian fluid's apparent viscosity changes with shear rate (e.g., some slurries thin under shear, others thicken).
What is thermal expansion and why does it matter in piping design?Materials expand/contract with temperature change (ΔL = L × α × ΔT); piping systems need expansion loops, joints or flexibility in the layout to absorb this movement, or they risk generating large stresses at fixed points and supports.
What is the difference between specific heat and latent heat?Specific heat is the energy needed to change a substance's temperature by one degree without a phase change; latent heat is the energy needed to change phase (e.g., liquid to vapor) at constant temperature, with no temperature change during the transition itself.
What is the Bernoulli equation and what does it assume?It states that total mechanical energy per unit volume (pressure, kinetic, and potential energy terms) stays constant along a streamline; it assumes incompressible, inviscid (frictionless), steady flow with no work or heat added — real systems apply a corrected form that accounts for friction losses.
What's the difference between a centrifugal pump and a positive displacement pump?A centrifugal pump imparts velocity via an impeller and converts it to pressure, giving flow that varies with system head (and needing NPSH margin); a positive displacement pump traps and moves a fixed volume per cycle, giving flow largely independent of pressure but requiring relief protection since it can generate very high pressure against a blocked outlet.
Explain the concept of thermal efficiency in a heat engine.The ratio of net work output to heat input; the Carnot efficiency (1 − Tcold/Thot, temperatures in absolute scale) sets the theoretical maximum for any heat engine operating between two temperatures, which is why raising the hot-side temperature or lowering the cold-side improves real-cycle efficiency.
What is the difference between creep and fatigue as failure mechanisms?Creep is slow, time-dependent permanent deformation under sustained load, usually at elevated temperature (a static-load, long-duration mechanism); fatigue is failure from repeated cyclic loading, often at stresses well below yield, driven by cycle count rather than sustained duration.

Machine Design & Materials

Selection, sizing and failure-mode questions for design-facing roles.
QuestionAnswer
How do you select a bearing for an application?Match the load type and magnitude (radial/axial), required life (L10), speed, alignment/misalignment tolerance, and environment (temperature, contamination), then check the manufacturer's rating and lubrication requirements.
What's the difference between fatigue failure and yield failure?Yield failure occurs from a single load exceeding the material's yield strength; fatigue failure builds up from repeated cyclic loading well below yield strength, through crack initiation and propagation, and can happen suddenly and without warning.
Explain the purpose of a factor of safety.It's a margin applied to account for uncertainty in loads, material properties, manufacturing defects, and consequences of failure — sized higher where failure is more critical or less predictable.
What's the difference between a bolt in shear versus in tension?In tension the bolt resists a pulling load along its axis (preload matters most); in shear the bolt resists a load perpendicular to its axis and relies on the bolt body (or friction from clamp-up) to resist sliding.
Why do we use welded versus bolted connections, and when would you pick one over the other?Welded joints are lighter, stiffer and permanent but harder to inspect/repair and can introduce residual stress; bolted joints are easier to inspect, disassemble and replace, and are preferred where maintenance or field assembly is needed.
How do the pump/fan affinity laws work?For a fixed system, flow varies directly with speed, head varies with speed squared, and power varies with speed cubed — small speed reductions give large power savings, which is why VFDs on pumps/fans save energy.
What's the difference between ductile and brittle failure?Ductile failure shows significant plastic deformation (necking, yielding) before fracture and gives visible warning; brittle failure happens with little to no plastic deformation, often suddenly and without warning — material selection and temperature both affect which mode dominates.
What is stress concentration and how do designers deal with it?Geometric features like holes, notches or sharp corners locally amplify stress well above the nominal average; designers reduce this with generous fillet radii, gradual section changes, and by avoiding unnecessary abrupt geometry changes in high-stress areas.
What's the difference between static and dynamic balancing of rotating equipment?Static balancing corrects a single-plane weight imbalance (as if the part isn't spinning); dynamic balancing corrects imbalance across two or more planes that only shows up as wobble/vibration during actual rotation — most real rotating machinery needs dynamic balancing.
What is the purpose of a relief valve, and how do you size one?It protects equipment/piping from overpressure by opening at a set pressure to relieve excess fluid; sizing is based on the worst-case relief scenario (e.g., fire, blocked outlet, control failure) and the required relieving capacity at the allowable accumulation pressure, per the applicable code (e.g., ASME).
What's the difference between forced and natural convection in heat exchanger design?Natural convection relies on buoyancy-driven fluid motion from density differences (slower, lower heat transfer coefficients); forced convection uses a pump or fan to actively move fluid across the surface, giving much higher, more controllable heat transfer rates.
How do you decide on a pipe material and schedule for a given service?Match the material to the fluid's chemical compatibility and temperature range, then size the wall thickness (schedule) to safely contain the design pressure with corrosion allowance, checking against the relevant piping code (e.g., ASME B31.3).
What's the difference between a shaft key, a spline, and an interference fit for transmitting torque?A key sits in a matched keyway and is simple/cheap but creates a stress concentration; a spline distributes torque across many teeth for higher capacity and self-centering; an interference (press) fit transmits torque purely through friction from radial clamping force, avoiding stress risers but requiring tight tolerance control and controlled assembly (heat/press).
How do you choose between a gear, belt, or chain drive for power transmission?Gears give precise, high-torque, compact transmission but need lubrication and rigid alignment; belts are quiet, tolerate misalignment and provide overload slip protection but transmit less torque and can stretch; chains handle higher torque than belts with less slip but need lubrication and are noisier — the choice weighs torque/speed needs, alignment tolerance, and maintenance access.
What is the purpose of preload in a bolted joint, and why is it usually more important than the bolt's ultimate strength?Preload clamps the joint members together so external loads are mostly absorbed as a reduction in clamp force rather than directly cycling the bolt, which is what prevents fatigue failure and joint separation — under-torquing (not exceeding ultimate strength) is the far more common real-world bolted joint failure mode.
What's the difference between a plain (sleeve) bearing and a rolling-element bearing?A plain bearing relies on a sliding surface (often with a hydrodynamic oil film) and tolerates higher loads/shock with simpler construction but has more friction at low speed/startup; a rolling-element bearing uses balls/rollers for lower friction and precise positioning but is more sensitive to contamination and shock loading.

Site & Practical Scenarios

How you apply the theory when something's actually running (or not).
QuestionAnswer
A pump is cavitating on startup — what would you check?Confirm suction line isn't blocked/throttled, check available NPSH versus required NPSH at the operating point, look for entrained air, and verify the fluid isn't near its vapor pressure due to temperature.
A rotating machine is vibrating more than usual — how do you approach it?Take a vibration reading and look at the frequency spectrum to narrow down imbalance, misalignment, looseness or bearing defects, then check coupling alignment, mounting bolts, and bearing condition before deeper teardown.
How would you troubleshoot a belt drive that keeps slipping or wearing fast?Check belt tension against the manufacturer's spec, pulley alignment, belt/pulley wear and groove condition, and confirm the drive isn't overloaded relative to the belt's rated capacity.
How do you approach specifying a heat exchanger for a new duty?Establish the process duty (flow rates, inlet/outlet temperatures, fluids), calculate required heat transfer area from LMTD and an appropriate overall heat transfer coefficient, then check pressure drop and fouling allowance against the vendor's selection.
What do you look at when a piping system has unexpectedly high pressure drop?Check for partially closed valves, fouling/scale build-up, wrong pipe sizing versus design flow, or entrained air/gas in the line; compare actual head loss to the design calculation to isolate where the discrepancy is.
How would you prioritize a maintenance backlog with limited time?Rank by safety/criticality first, then by likelihood and consequence of failure (a basic risk matrix), factoring in whether a failure would stop production versus just a single component.
A bearing keeps failing prematurely on the same machine — how would you investigate?Check lubrication (type, quantity, contamination), alignment and balance, load versus the bearing's rating, and whether the failure pattern (spalling, discoloration, brinelling) points to a specific cause rather than just replacing it and hoping.
How would you approach root-cause analysis after an equipment failure?Gather physical evidence before it's disturbed, build a timeline of events and any recent changes (maintenance, load, operating conditions), and work through a structured method (e.g., 5-Whys or fishbone) rather than jumping to the first plausible cause.
What would you check if a compressed air system isn't reaching design pressure?Check for leaks in the distribution system (a major and common cause), compressor capacity versus actual demand, filter/dryer pressure drop, and that no downstream valve or regulator is unintentionally restricting flow.
How do you decide whether a piece of equipment should be repaired or replaced?Weigh repair cost and expected remaining life against replacement cost, efficiency gains, downtime risk, and parts availability — a pattern of repeated failures often tips the balance toward replacement even if a single repair looks cheaper.
What's your approach to reading and interpreting a P&ID?Trace the process flow from source to destination through each piece of equipment, note instrumentation, valves and their fail-safe positions, and cross-check line numbers/specs against the piping class before doing any physical work.
How would you handle discovering a safety guard has been removed from a machine during your inspection?Stop the machine (or don't start it) until the guard is reinstated, report it immediately rather than reinstalling it quietly and moving on, and check whether it's a one-off or a recurring issue that points to a bigger process gap.
A gearbox is running hotter than usual — how would you troubleshoot it?Check oil level and condition (degraded or contaminated oil loses film strength), verify the load hasn't increased beyond design, inspect for a cooling airflow restriction or fan issue, and check bearing/gear mesh condition since surface wear increases friction and heat generation.
How would you approach commissioning a new pump into an existing piping system?Verify the system curve was calculated correctly and matches the pump's selected duty point, flush the piping before startup to remove debris that could damage the impeller/seals, confirm rotation direction before full flow, and check for cavitation/vibration once running at the actual operating point rather than assuming the datasheet duty point matches reality.
What would you check if a mechanical seal on a pump is leaking prematurely?Check for dry running or loss of flush/quench fluid, verify the seal faces weren't damaged during installation, confirm alignment and vibration are within tolerance (excess shaft movement destroys seal faces), and check the process fluid hasn't changed in a way that's incompatible with the seal material.
How would you approach vibration analysis to distinguish imbalance from misalignment?Imbalance typically shows a dominant peak at 1x running speed with a stable radial vibration pattern; misalignment typically shows strong 1x and 2x components with significant axial vibration as well — comparing the vibration signature across radial and axial directions is usually enough to separate the two before deeper diagnostics.
