Structural Fundamentals

Concepts that come up in almost every civil/structural screening interview.
QuestionAnswer
What's the difference between dead load and live load?Dead load is the permanent, fixed weight of the structure and fixed elements (self-weight, finishes); live load is variable/movable load from occupants, furniture, vehicles, etc., defined by code minimums for the building's use.
Explain the difference between a determinate and indeterminate structure.A determinate structure's reactions/internal forces can be solved from static equilibrium equations alone; an indeterminate structure has more unknowns than equations and needs compatibility (deflection) conditions or methods like moment distribution/stiffness to solve.
What is the difference between bending moment and shear force?Shear force is the internal force resisting sliding between two sections of a beam; bending moment is the internal moment resisting rotation/bending at a section — both vary along the beam and are linked (shear is the derivative of moment).
What is the purpose of reinforcement in concrete?Concrete is strong in compression but weak in tension; steel reinforcement is placed to resist the tensile stresses (and sometimes to help with compression, shear, and crack control), making the composite section handle both.
Explain the difference between one-way and two-way slabs.A one-way slab spans predominantly in one direction (typically when the long-to-short span ratio exceeds ~2) and is reinforced mainly in that direction; a two-way slab spans and is reinforced in both directions because both spans contribute meaningfully to carrying the load.
What is the factor of safety used for in structural design, and how does it show up in modern codes?It's a margin against uncertainty in loads and material strength; modern limit-state codes apply it as separate load factors (amplifying loads) and material/resistance factors (reducing capacity) rather than one lumped factor, so risk is allocated more precisely.
What's the difference between working stress design (WSD) and limit state design (LSD)?WSD keeps stresses within an allowable fraction of material strength using one global factor of safety on service loads; LSD (used in most modern codes) checks the structure against defined limit states (ultimate/strength and serviceability) using separate partial factors on loads and materials, generally giving a more consistent, economical design.
What is the difference between a beam and a column, structurally?A beam primarily resists transverse loads through bending and shear; a column primarily resists axial compressive load along its length, though most real columns also see some bending (making them beam-columns) from eccentricity or lateral loads.
Explain the concept of ductility and why it's important in seismic design.Ductility is a structure's ability to deform significantly beyond its elastic limit without losing much load-carrying capacity; in seismic design it lets the structure absorb and dissipate earthquake energy through controlled yielding rather than sudden brittle failure.
What's the difference between a pin (hinge), roller and fixed support?A pin resists translation in both directions but allows rotation; a roller resists translation in only one direction and allows rotation and movement in the other; a fixed support resists translation in both directions and rotation, so it's the only one that can carry a moment.
What is buckling, and what factors influence a column's buckling capacity?Buckling is a sudden sideways instability failure under compressive load, occurring before the material itself reaches its crushing strength; capacity depends on the column's slenderness ratio (length versus radius of gyration), end support conditions, and material stiffness (Euler buckling load).
What is the difference between shear wall and moment frame lateral systems in a building?A shear wall resists lateral loads primarily through in-plane wall stiffness (very stiff, efficient, but less architecturally flexible); a moment frame resists lateral loads through rigid beam-column connections that carry bending, giving more open floor plans but generally more flexibility (drift) for the same lateral load.

Materials & Construction

Concrete, steel and construction-sequencing questions.
QuestionAnswer
What factors affect concrete strength?Water-cement ratio (lower generally means higher strength), cement type/content, aggregate quality and grading, curing conditions (time, temperature, moisture), and proper compaction/placement without segregation.
Why is curing important for concrete?Curing keeps concrete moist and at a favorable temperature so cement hydration can continue, developing strength and reducing shrinkage cracking — inadequate curing can significantly reduce final strength and durability.
What's the difference between M20 and M25 concrete (or similar grade notations)?The number denotes the characteristic compressive strength in MPa at 28 days (e.g., M25 = 25 MPa) obtained from standard cube/cylinder tests, used to specify the mix design requirement.
What is the purpose of a construction joint versus an expansion joint?A construction joint is a planned stopping point in a continuous pour to allow work to proceed in stages, with reinforcement continuing through it; an expansion joint is a full separation that allows structures to move independently due to thermal expansion, shrinkage or settlement.
What is soil bearing capacity and why does it matter for foundation design?It's the maximum pressure the soil can support without excessive settlement or shear failure; foundation size is set so the applied pressure stays within the safe bearing capacity determined from geotechnical investigation.
What's the difference between a raft (mat) foundation and isolated footings?Isolated footings support individual columns separately and suit adequate, fairly uniform soil bearing; a raft foundation is one continuous slab supporting the whole structure, used where soil bearing is low, loads are heavy, or differential settlement needs to be minimized.
What's the difference between a pile foundation and a shallow foundation, and when would you use piles?Shallow (spread/raft) foundations transfer load directly to soil near the surface; piles transfer load deeper — either by end bearing on a strong stratum or by skin friction along the shaft — used when surface soil is too weak, settlement-prone, or loads are too heavy for a shallow foundation to handle economically.
What is the difference between segregation and bleeding in fresh concrete?Segregation is separation of coarse aggregate from the mortar/paste (often from over-vibration, poor mix design, or dropping concrete from too great a height); bleeding is water rising to the surface as heavier solids settle, which can weaken the top layer if not managed (e.g., by finishing timing).
Why is the water-cement ratio described as the single most important factor in concrete strength and durability?Excess water beyond what's needed for hydration leaves capillary pores after it evaporates, reducing both strength and durability (permeability to water/chlorides); keeping the ratio as low as workability allows generally gives stronger, more durable concrete.
What's the difference between mild steel and high-yield/deformed reinforcement bars?Mild steel bars are smooth and have lower yield strength; deformed (ribbed/high-yield) bars have surface ribs that improve bond with concrete and typically higher yield strength, allowing more efficient (thinner/fewer bars) reinforcement design — deformed bars are now the standard in most structural work.
What is the purpose of a damp-proof course (DPC) or damp-proof membrane (DPM)?It's a barrier (typically at foundation/wall level or under a slab) that stops rising or penetrating moisture from migrating into the structure above, protecting against dampness, material degradation and mold.
What's the difference between compaction and consolidation of soil?Compaction is a rapid, mechanical densification of soil (typically unsaturated) by expelling air, done during construction with rollers/tampers; consolidation is a slow, time-dependent process where saturated soil expels pore water under sustained load, causing gradual settlement over months or years.

Site & Practical Scenarios

How you handle real construction-stage problems.
QuestionAnswer
You find a discrepancy between the structural drawing and the site condition — what do you do?Stop work on that element if safety is in question, document the discrepancy (photos, measurements), raise a formal query (RFI) to the design team, and wait for a written instruction before proceeding rather than assuming.
How would you check that a concrete pour is ready to proceed?Verify formwork alignment/bracing, reinforcement placement, cover, spacing and ties against drawings, embedded items are in place, and that a pre-pour inspection/checklist has been signed off before concrete arrives.
A settlement crack appears in a completed structure — how would you investigate it?Record the crack pattern, width and whether it's still moving (crack monitors/gauges), review geotechnical data and as-built foundation info, and bring in a structural assessment if it's significant, rather than just patching it.
How do you handle a subcontractor falling behind schedule?Identify the actual cause (resources, sequencing, access, information), quantify the impact on the critical path, and address it early with a recovery plan and clear follow-up, escalating formally if it isn't resolved informally.
What would you check before signing off on formwork removal (striking)?Confirm concrete has reached the required strength for that element and stage (by test cube results or maturity method, not just elapsed time), and that removal sequence won't leave any part under-supported.
How do you prioritize safety observations on a busy site?Address anything with immediate risk of injury first (stop the activity if needed), then log and track lower-risk items through the site's normal reporting/closeout system so nothing gets lost.
How would you verify concrete cube/cylinder test results before accepting a pour as compliant?Confirm samples were taken, cured and tested per the specified standard, check results against the specified characteristic strength with the correct statistical acceptance criteria (not just a single low result in isolation), and flag any non-conformance through the proper process rather than accepting it informally.
What would you do if excavation reveals unexpected ground conditions different from the geotechnical report?Stop work in that area if it affects safety or design assumptions, document the actual conditions found, and notify the geotechnical engineer/designer for a revised assessment before continuing — proceeding on the original design assumptions could be unsafe.
How do you manage quality control for rebar placement before a pour?Check bar size, spacing, and cover against the drawings using spacers/chairs, verify laps and anchorage lengths meet the specified development length, and complete a documented pre-pour inspection checklist signed off before concrete is called for.
A crane lift is planned near an overhead service or a boundary — what would you check?Confirm exclusion/safe clearance distances from the service are maintained, verify the lift plan accounts for load, radius, ground conditions and wind limits, and ensure a banksman/lift supervisor is in place — don't proceed if any of these aren't confirmed in writing.
How would you handle a variation instruction that isn't accompanied by a cost or programme impact assessment?Flag the impact before proceeding wherever possible, keep records of instructions and any impact discussion, and follow the contract's formal variation process rather than just absorbing the change silently — undocumented changes are a common source of later disputes.
What's your approach to interpreting conflicting information between drawings and the specification?Check the contract's stated order of precedence for documents, raise a formal query (RFI) if it's still unclear, and don't proceed on your own interpretation for anything structurally or safety significant.
