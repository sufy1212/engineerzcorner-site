PV Fundamentals

Core PV theory used to check you understand how the cell and array actually behave, not just formulas.
QuestionAnswer
Explain how a PV cell converts sunlight into electricity.Photons striking the p-n junction free electrons, creating electron-hole pairs; the junction's built-in electric field separates them, driving a current through an external circuit — the photovoltaic effect.
What is the difference between STC and NOCT ratings?STC (Standard Test Conditions: 1000 W/m², 25°C cell temp, AM1.5 spectrum) is used for nameplate ratings; NOCT reflects a more realistic operating temperature (~45°C cell temp at 800 W/m², 20°C ambient), giving a better real-world power estimate.
Why does panel output drop as temperature rises?Higher cell temperature reduces the open-circuit voltage (negative temperature coefficient, typically around -0.3%/°C to -0.45%/°C for Voc), lowering power output even though current changes only slightly.
Explain the difference between series and parallel wiring of panels.Series strings add voltage while current stays the same (limited by the weakest panel in the string); parallel strings add current while voltage stays the same — combining both builds the array to the inverter's required voltage/current window.
What causes a hot spot on a PV module, and why is it dangerous?A shaded or faulty cell is forced to act as a load rather than a generator within its series string, dissipating power as heat instead of producing it; if severe/prolonged this can damage the encapsulant or backsheet, which is why bypass diodes exist to route current around the affected section.
What is the purpose of a bypass diode?It provides an alternate current path around a shaded or underperforming cell string within a module, preventing that section from being forced into reverse bias and limiting hot-spot damage and string-level power loss.
What is the difference between monocrystalline, polycrystalline and thin-film panels?Monocrystalline cells are cut from a single silicon crystal (highest efficiency, most uniform, priciest); polycrystalline uses multiple silicon fragments (slightly lower efficiency, lower cost); thin-film uses a different deposited material (lower efficiency, lighter/flexible, better shaded/high-temperature tolerance in some cases).
Explain the difference between a string inverter, microinverter and power optimizer setup.A string inverter converts DC to AC for a whole string at once (simplest, cheapest, most exposed to per-panel shading loss); microinverters convert at each panel (best per-panel optimization and monitoring, higher cost); optimizers do per-panel DC-DC conditioning but still centralize the DC-AC conversion at one inverter.
What is the fill factor of a solar cell?The ratio of the actual maximum power point (Vmp × Imp) to the theoretical maximum (Voc × Isc); it reflects how "square" the I-V curve is and is an indicator of cell quality/losses.
Why do panel datasheets list both Voc/Isc and Vmp/Imp?Voc/Isc are the open-circuit/short-circuit extremes used for safety and equipment-rating calculations (e.g., inverter max input voltage); Vmp/Imp describe the actual operating point at maximum power, which is what real energy-yield calculations use.
What is an MPPT and why is it needed?Maximum Power Point Tracking is the inverter/charge controller algorithm that continuously adjusts operating voltage to keep the array at its maximum-power point, since that point shifts with irradiance and temperature and isn't fixed.
What is the difference between irradiance and irradiation (insolation)?Irradiance is instantaneous power density (W/m²) at a given moment; irradiation (insolation) is that irradiance integrated over time (kWh/m²/day or /year), which is what's actually used to estimate energy yield.
Why does a module's temperature coefficient of power matter more day-to-day than its Voc coefficient alone?The power coefficient (typically around -0.3%/°C to -0.5%/°C) captures the combined effect of falling voltage and near-flat current on actual energy output, so it's the figure that predicts real yield loss on hot days, whereas the Voc coefficient alone is mainly used for cold-weather string voltage limits.
What determines a solar cell's theoretical efficiency limit?The semiconductor's band gap sets a trade-off between capturing more of the solar spectrum (lower band gap) and losing less energy to heat per absorbed photon (higher band gap); silicon's band gap puts its practical limit (Shockley-Queisser) around the low-to-mid 30s percent, well above what commercial cells currently achieve.
What is light-induced degradation (LID) and how does cell type affect it?LID is an initial power loss (often 1-3%) that occurs in the first hours/days of sunlight exposure due to boron-oxygen defects reacting with light in p-type silicon; n-type cells are largely immune to this mechanism, which is one reason premium modules increasingly use n-type wafers.
How does bifacial panel technology capture extra energy, and what limits the gain?Bifacial modules generate power from reflected/diffuse light striking the rear surface in addition to the front, with the gain (commonly 5-20%) depending heavily on ground albedo (reflectivity), mounting height off the surface, and row spacing — low-albedo rooftops see far less benefit than elevated ground-mounts over light-colored gravel.

System Design & Sizing

Array configuration, inverter matching and system-level sizing — the practical backbone of most solar interviews.
QuestionAnswer
How do you size a PV array for a given load?Start from the daily energy demand, divide by available peak sun hours for the site/season to get required array kW, then add losses (soiling, wiring, inverter efficiency, temperature) as a derate factor before selecting panel count.
How do you check a string is within an inverter's voltage window?Take the panel's Voc, correct it for the coldest expected temperature (Voc rises as temperature drops), multiply by the number of panels in series, and confirm it stays below the inverter's maximum DC input voltage — the reverse check (lowest expected Vmp at highest temperature) confirms the string stays above the inverter's MPPT minimum.
Why does panel orientation and tilt matter for system output?Tilt and azimuth determine how closely the panel faces the sun's path through the day/year, which directly affects the irradiance actually captured; the optimum is usually close to the site latitude tilted toward the equator, though real installs often trade off for roof pitch or aesthetics.
How would you approach a partially shaded roof in system design?Identify which strings are affected and when, favor microinverters/optimizers over a single string inverter if shading is significant, use bypass diodes' effect on module output in yield estimates, and consider splitting arrays into separate MPPT inputs to isolate shaded and unshaded sections.
What is the purpose of a combiner box in a PV array?It parallels multiple strings into a single feed to the inverter, typically housing fuses/breakers per string for overcurrent protection and providing a convenient disconnect and monitoring point.
How do you size a battery bank for an off-grid or hybrid system?Total the daily energy consumption, divide by the usable depth-of-discharge fraction of the battery chemistry, add autonomy days for cloudy-weather coverage, and check the bank can deliver the peak load current the inverter demands.
What's the difference between grid-tied, off-grid and hybrid PV systems?Grid-tied systems export/import with the utility and have no batteries (usually shut down in a grid outage unless designed otherwise); off-grid systems rely entirely on batteries/generator with no utility connection; hybrid systems combine grid connection with battery storage for backup and/or peak-shaving.
Why is DC cable sizing in a PV array different from a typical AC circuit?Voltage drop matters even more since it directly reduces energy harvest (not just efficiency), cables must handle the maximum possible short-circuit current from parallel strings, and outdoor/rooftop exposure requires appropriate UV and temperature-rated cable.
What is the role of an anti-islanding function in a grid-tied inverter?It detects loss of grid supply and automatically disconnects the inverter output, preventing the PV system from continuing to energize a de-energized grid section — a safety requirement for utility workers.
How do you estimate expected annual energy yield for a proposed system?Multiply array DC capacity by site-specific peak sun hours (from solar resource data for the location/tilt/azimuth), then apply an overall system derate (inverter efficiency, soiling, wiring losses, shading, temperature) — commonly expressed as a performance ratio.
How do you account for module mismatch losses in an array design?Even panels from the same production batch have small tolerance variations in current/voltage, and a series string is limited by its weakest panel; mismatch is typically budgeted as a small fixed derate (often 1-3%) unless the design deliberately sorts/bins modules by measured output.
What is DC-to-AC ratio (ILR) and why is it usually designed above 1.0?It's the ratio of array DC nameplate capacity to inverter AC output rating; sizing the array modestly larger than the inverter (commonly 1.1-1.3) is cost-effective because the array rarely hits full nameplate output, and the resulting clipping losses at peak conditions are usually smaller than the extra energy captured during low-light periods.
How do you decide between fixed-tilt and single-axis tracking for a ground-mount system?Tracking increases annual yield (often 15-25%) by keeping panels closer to perpendicular to the sun through the day, but adds mechanical complexity, maintenance, and cost, and needs more land spacing to avoid inter-row shading — the decision usually comes down to land cost/availability versus the value of the extra energy.
What factors drive AC conductor sizing from inverter to point of interconnection?Continuous current rating of the inverter output (with the standard safety factor), voltage drop over the run length to keep energy losses acceptable, conduit fill and ambient/grouping derating, and coordination with the AC disconnect and overcurrent protection ratings at each end.
What do you need to consider for snow and wind loading in mounting system design?Structural loading calculations must use local code-specified ground snow load and wind speed data (not just typical values), account for how tilt angle affects snow retention/shedding and wind uplift, and confirm racking and attachment points are rated for the combined dead, live, snow and wind load cases — not just the panel's own weight.

Site & Practical Scenarios

How you think on your feet during commissioning or troubleshooting — often matters more than textbook recall.
QuestionAnswer
An array is producing well below expected output — how do you troubleshoot it?Check inverter fault logs and per-string/per-MPPT readings first, compare actual irradiance to expected, inspect for soiling/shading/debris, measure string voltage and current against datasheet expectations, and check for a tripped/failed combiner fuse isolating a string.
What would you check before commissioning a new PV system?String polarity and voltage/current match design, insulation resistance test on DC wiring, correct MPPT/inverter configuration settings, ground continuity, and that labeling/signage meets code before energizing.
How do you approach arc-fault risk in DC PV wiring?Ensure DC arc-fault protection (where required) is installed and functioning, keep connections properly torqued and use matched connector types (never mixing brands), and inspect for chafed insulation or loose MC4 connectors during maintenance.
A customer reports lower-than-expected savings on their bill — how do you investigate?Compare actual production data (from inverter/monitoring) against the original design estimate, check for shading changes (new construction, tree growth), verify no strings/inverters are faulted or offline, and confirm the utility billing/net-metering setup matches what was designed.
How would you safely work on a PV array during daylight?Treat DC conductors as live any time panels are exposed to light (they can't simply be "switched off" like AC), use insulated tools and PPE, cover panels if isolating strings physically, and follow lockout procedures on the inverter/AC side in addition to DC precautions.
What would cause an inverter to intermittently trip on overvoltage?String Voc rising above the inverter's limit during cold, high-irradiance mornings is a common cause — worth rechecking the string sizing calculation against actual site temperature extremes rather than assuming the original design margin was sufficient.
How do you decide panel layout when roof space is limited?Prioritize orientation/tilt closest to optimal within the available roof planes, keep string lengths balanced across MPPT inputs, avoid mixing significantly different orientations on the same MPPT/string, and account for setback/fire-code clearances required by the authority having jurisdiction.
What maintenance does a typical grid-tied PV system need?Periodic panel cleaning depending on local soiling, visual inspection of connectors/mounting hardware, monitoring for underperforming strings via the inverter's data, and periodic torque/insulation checks per manufacturer or code interval.
How would you explain the value of monitoring/data logging to a client?It allows early detection of underperformance (a failed string, soiling, shading creep) that would otherwise go unnoticed for months, supports warranty claims with production evidence, and gives the client visibility into savings and system health.
A single string reads zero output while the rest of the array is fine — what would you check first?Start with the combiner box fuse/breaker for that string before assuming a panel fault, then check string voltage at the combiner (open-circuit reading confirms panels are alive but disconnected downstream, near-zero suggests a wiring fault or blown fuse), and inspect MC4 connectors along the string for a loose or corroded joint.
Actual production is consistently below the modeled estimate even after ruling out faults — what would you re-examine?Revisit the original assumptions: local shading obstacles that may have grown or changed since design (trees, new construction), soiling rate assumptions for the actual local environment, whether the system derate used realistic (not best-case) inverter and wiring losses, and whether the solar resource dataset used matched the actual site microclimate.
An inverter displays a ground fault alarm — what would you check?Isolate which string is affected using the inverter's fault reporting, inspect for damaged module or cable insulation exposing a conductor to a grounded surface (common after rodent damage or a pinched cable), and measure insulation resistance on the affected string to confirm before re-energizing — never simply reset and re-close without finding the cause.
How do you handle a module degradation warranty claim when field measurements are ambiguous?Take repeat I-V curve measurements under stable, near-STC conditions (or normalize readings properly) rather than relying on a single reading, compare against the manufacturer's published degradation curve for the module's age, and document soiling/shading/temperature conditions at time of test since these are common sources of an apparently low reading that isn't actually degradation.
What extra safety precautions apply when working around a battery bank in a hybrid system?Treat batteries as a separate energized hazard from the PV/AC side — remove metallic jewelry, use insulated tools near terminals, follow the manufacturer's disconnect sequence, be aware of arc-flash and explosive gas risk with some chemistries (e.g., hydrogen off-gassing from lead-acid), and never assume the battery is isolated just because the inverter is off.
