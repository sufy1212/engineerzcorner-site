PM Glossary

A–Z terms used on the team, contract and commercial side of engineering projects
PM Index PM.01 RACI Matrix PM.02 Risk Register PM.03 Task Tracker PM.04 Meeting Minutes PM.05 EVM PM.06 Team Directory PM.07 Commercial Terms PM.08 Incoterms PM.09 PMP / PMBOK PM.10 Punch List PM.11 Permit to Work PM.12 Glossary
Plain-language definitions for the acronyms and contract/schedule vocabulary that show up constantly on engineering projects — the words a spec, a payment certificate or a site meeting throws at you without ever explaining. Grouped alphabetically; jump to a letter range or scan the whole thing. For deeper coverage of the commercial terms specifically, see Commercial Terms of Project and Incoterms; for the formal process-group vocabulary, see the PMP / PMBOK Reference.
A – E F – K L – P Q – S T – Z

A – E

TermDefinition
Acceptance CriteriaThe conditions a deliverable must meet before the client or owner will formally sign it off as complete.
AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction)The body — a municipality, fire marshal, utility, or regulator — with legal authority to approve or inspect a design or installation.
As-Built DrawingA drawing updated after construction to show what was actually installed, including every deviation from the original design.
AssumptionSomething taken to be true for planning purposes without proof, logged so the plan can be revisited if it turns out false.
BaselineThe originally approved version of the schedule, budget or scope, kept fixed so actual progress can be measured against it.
Bid BondA guarantee submitted with a tender that compensates the owner if the winning bidder refuses to sign the contract.
BOQ (Bill of Quantities)An itemized list of materials, labor and quantities used to price a tender and later to value work done.
Buffer / Contingency ReserveExtra time or budget deliberately held back to absorb known risks, separate from management reserve for unknown ones.
Burn RateThe rate at which project budget is being spent over a given period, used to project when funds will run out.
Change OrderA formally approved amendment to the contract scope, price or schedule — the executed result of a change request.
Change RequestA formal proposal to alter scope, schedule, cost or a deliverable, submitted for review before it's approved or rejected.
ClaimA formal assertion by one contracting party seeking additional time or money, typically arising from a dispute over entitlement.
Close-outThe formal wrap-up phase of a project — final documentation, handover, releasing resources and closing contracts.
CommissioningThe process of testing and verifying that installed systems perform as designed before they're handed over for operation.
CPM (Critical Path Method)A scheduling technique that calculates the longest sequence of dependent tasks, which sets the shortest possible project duration.
Critical PathThe chain of tasks with zero float — any delay to one of them delays the whole project's finish date.
Defects Liability Period (DLP)A window after handover during which the contractor is obligated to return and fix defects at their own cost.
DeliverableAny tangible product, document or result that must be produced to complete a project or a phase of it.
DependencyA logical relationship between two tasks where one's start or finish controls the other's — the links a schedule is built from.
Dispute ResolutionThe contractually agreed mechanism — negotiation, mediation, adjudication or arbitration — for resolving disagreements without going straight to litigation.
Earned Value (EV)The budgeted value of the work actually completed so far, used with planned and actual cost to gauge performance.
EOT (Extension of Time)A contractual adjustment granting the contractor more time to finish, usually without penalty, due to an excusable delay.
EPC (Engineering, Procurement, Construction)A contract model where one contractor is responsible for design, sourcing materials and building — usually for a fixed price.
EscalationRaising an issue to a higher level of authority when it can't be resolved at the current level, or a contractual price adjustment tied to inflation/index changes.

F – K

TermDefinition
Fast-TrackingCompressing the schedule by running activities in parallel that were originally planned sequentially, at the cost of added risk.
FAT (Factory Acceptance Test)Testing equipment at the manufacturer's facility before it ships, to confirm it meets specification prior to delivery.
Float / SlackThe amount of time a task can slip without delaying the project finish date; zero float means the task is on the critical path.
Force MajeureA contract clause excusing a party from performance when an extraordinary event outside their control makes it impossible.
Gantt ChartA horizontal bar chart showing tasks against a timeline, commonly used to visualize schedule and dependencies.
GovernanceThe framework of roles, decision rights and controls that determine how a project is directed and held accountable.
HandoverThe formal transfer of a completed facility or system from the contractor to the owner or operations team.
Hold PointA specified stage in the work that cannot proceed until a required inspection or approval has taken place.
IFC (Issued For Construction) DrawingsThe finalized drawing set approved for actual construction, distinct from earlier for-review or for-tender revisions.
Issue LogA running record of problems affecting the project, who owns each one, and the status of its resolution.
Joint Venture (JV)A temporary partnership between two or more firms that jointly bid and deliver a project, sharing risk and reward.
Kickoff MeetingThe first formal meeting between the project team and stakeholders to align on scope, roles and objectives before work starts.
KPI (Key Performance Indicator)A measurable value — cost variance, schedule adherence, safety incidents — used to track project performance against targets.

L – P

TermDefinition
LagAn imposed delay between two dependent tasks — e.g. waiting for concrete to cure before formwork is stripped.
LeadAn overlap that lets a successor task start before its predecessor finishes, the opposite of lag.
Letter of Intent (LOI)A preliminary document signaling intent to award a contract, sometimes authorizing early mobilization before the full contract is signed.
LienA legal claim placed against a property by a contractor or supplier who hasn't been paid for work or materials.
Liquidated Damages (LD)A pre-agreed sum the contractor pays the owner for each unit of time the project runs late, set in the contract in advance.
Lump Sum ContractA contract for a single fixed price covering the defined scope, regardless of the contractor's actual cost to deliver it.
MilestoneA significant zero-duration point in the schedule marking the completion of a major phase or deliverable.
MobilizationThe setup phase where a contractor brings people, equipment and temporary facilities to site before work begins.
MOM (Minutes of Meeting)The written record of what was discussed, decided and assigned during a meeting.
NCR (Non-Conformance Report)A formal record raised when work or material fails to meet the specified requirements, tracked through to correction.
NovationThe formal transfer of a contract's rights and obligations from one party to another, with all parties' consent.
OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer)The company that originally designs and builds a piece of equipment, as distinct from a reseller or integrator.
Owner's EngineerAn engineer or firm appointed to represent the owner's technical interests, review designs and oversee the contractor's work.
Payment BondA guarantee that subcontractors and suppliers will be paid even if the main contractor defaults.
Performance BondA guarantee compensating the owner if the contractor fails to complete the contract according to its terms.
ProcurementThe process of sourcing and purchasing the goods, equipment and services needed to execute the project.
Progress ClaimA periodic invoice submitted by the contractor for work completed to date, assessed against the BOQ or schedule of values.
Project CharterThe document that formally authorizes a project, naming its objectives, sponsor and the project manager's authority.
Provisional SumAn estimated allowance in a contract for work that can't be precisely priced when the contract is signed, adjusted later against actuals.
Punch ListA list of minor outstanding items to be finished or corrected before final acceptance of the works.

Q – S

TermDefinition
QA (Quality Assurance)The proactive process of defining standards and procedures to prevent defects before they happen.
QC (Quality Control)The reactive process of inspecting and testing completed work to check it meets the required standard.
QS (Quantity Surveyor)A professional who manages costs, measures quantities and values work for construction contracts.
RACIA responsibility matrix marking each task as Responsible, Accountable, Consulted or Informed for every team member.
RetentionA percentage of each payment withheld by the owner until the works — or the defects liability period — are complete.
RFI (Request for Information)A formal query raised, usually by the contractor, to clarify ambiguous or missing information in the design or specification.
Risk RegisterA log of identified project risks, their likelihood, impact and the response plan for each.
Rolling Wave PlanningPlanning near-term work in detail while leaving future work at a coarser level, refined as it gets closer.
SAT (Site Acceptance Test)Testing performed after equipment is installed on site, confirming it works correctly in its final environment.
Schedule of ValuesA breakdown of the contract sum across line items, used as the basis for valuing progress payments.
Scope CreepThe uncontrolled expansion of project scope without corresponding adjustment to time, cost or resources.
Scope of Work (SOW)The document defining exactly what work is, and isn't, included under a contract.
Snag ListAnother term for punch list — minor defects or incomplete items to close out before handover.
StakeholderAny individual or group who can affect, or is affected by, the project's outcome.
SubcontractorA company hired by the main contractor to perform a specific portion of the work.
SubmittalDocumentation — shop drawings, product data, samples — a contractor submits for the designer's or owner's review and approval.
Sunk CostMoney already spent that cannot be recovered, and which shouldn't factor into decisions about what to do next.

T – Z

TermDefinition
TenderA formal offer to carry out work at a stated price, submitted in response to an invitation to bid.
Testing & Commissioning (T&C)The combined phase of verifying installed systems function correctly and are ready for operational handover.
Time and Material ContractA contract that pays the contractor for actual hours worked and materials used, rather than a fixed price.
TurnkeyA delivery model where the contractor hands over a fully complete, ready-to-operate facility for a single price.
Unit Price ContractA contract that pays a fixed rate per unit of work (per meter, per cubic yard), with total cost varying by measured quantity.
Value EngineeringA structured review of a design to reduce cost or improve function without compromising required performance.
VariationA change to the contracted scope of work, whether it increases, decreases or simply alters what's required.
Warranty PeriodThe time after handover during which the contractor or manufacturer guarantees to repair defects at no charge.
WBS (Work Breakdown Structure)A hierarchical decomposition of the total project scope into smaller, manageable work packages.
Weighted ProgressAn overall percent-complete figure calculated by weighting each activity's progress by its share of total value or effort.
General reference only — terminology and usage can vary by region, industry and contract form; always confirm definitions against the governing contract. ← PM Index
