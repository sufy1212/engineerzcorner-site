Award & Contract Formation

The documents and steps that typically precede a signed contract.
TermMeaning
LOILetter of Intent — a short document signaling the client's intent to award the contract, sometimes authorizing limited early work ahead of the full contract being signed.
LOALetter of Award — formal notice that a bid/tender has been accepted; often the point the contract is treated as binding, even before the full contract document is executed.
NovationTransferring a contract's rights and obligations from one party to another, with all parties' consent — common when a design consultant's contract is transferred from client to contractor on a design-build job.
Back-to-back contractA subcontract written to mirror the main contract's key terms and risk allocation, so obligations the main contractor owes the client are passed down to the subcontractor on matching terms.
Novation vs AssignmentNovation transfers obligations too (needs all parties' consent); assignment transfers only the right to receive a benefit (like payment), not the underlying obligation to perform.

Payment & Security

How money and risk move between client and contractor during the job.
TermMeaning
Advance paymentA payment made to the contractor before work starts, usually to fund mobilization — typically secured by an advance payment bank guarantee and recovered from later progress payments.
IPCInterim Payment Certificate — a periodic (often monthly) certified statement of work value completed, used as the basis for a progress payment.
RetentionA percentage (commonly 5-10%) withheld from each payment as security against defects, released in stages after practical and/or final completion.
Performance bond/guaranteeA bank or insurance guarantee the contractor provides, payable to the client if the contractor fails to perform — typically 5-10% of contract value.
Advance payment guaranteeA bank guarantee sized to the advance payment, protecting the client if the contractor doesn't deliver work equal to the advance received.
Payment certificateThe document (often issued by the engineer/consultant) that certifies the amount due, which then triggers the payment obligation under most standard contract forms.

Pricing & Valuation

How the contract price is built up and valued.
TermMeaning
BOQBill of Quantities — an itemized list of work items with measured quantities and unit rates, used to build up the contract price and value changes.
Provisional sumAn allowance included in the contract price for work that isn't fully defined at contract signing — instructed and valued later once scope is confirmed.
PC sumPrime Cost sum — similar to a provisional sum, but specifically for a nominated supplier or specialist subcontractor whose exact cost isn't known at contract signing.
Rate analysisA cost breakdown behind a unit rate (materials, labor, plant, overhead, margin) — used to price change orders consistently with the original tender rates.
Escalation clauseA contract clause allowing the price to adjust for input cost changes (steel, fuel, labor indices) over the contract duration, usually tied to a published index.

Change, Delay & Claims

The vocabulary around scope changes, time extensions and disputes.
TermMeaning
Variation order (VO)An instructed change to the contracted scope of work — additions, omissions or modifications — priced and agreed before or after execution depending on the contract.
ClaimA formal request for additional time and/or money, based on an event the contract entitles the contractor (or client) to recover for — distinct from a variation, which is an instructed change.
EOTExtension of Time — additional contract duration granted for a qualifying delay event, protecting the contractor from liquidated damages for that portion of the delay.
LD / liquidated damagesA pre-agreed daily/weekly rate the contractor pays the client for late completion, in lieu of the client having to prove actual loss.
Force majeureExceptional events outside either party's control (often defined in the contract) that excuse or delay performance without penalty.
Notice periodA contractually required window to formally notify the other party of a claim or event — missing it can forfeit an otherwise valid entitlement under many standard contract forms.

Completion & Close-Out

The stages a project passes through on the way to final close-out.
TermMeaning
Practical completionThe point the works are complete enough for the client to use them for their intended purpose, even if minor defects/snags remain — often starts the defects liability period.
DLPDefects Liability Period — a fixed window after practical completion during which the contractor must fix defects at their own cost; retention is often tied to it.
Final completionThe point all outstanding defects are resolved and the contract is fully discharged — typically triggers release of the remaining retention.
Snag list / punch listAn itemized list of minor defects or incomplete items identified at practical completion, to be closed out during the defects liability period.
As-built drawingsDrawings updated to reflect what was actually constructed, as opposed to what was originally designed — a standard close-out deliverable.

Common Contract Types

How the pricing basis changes what risk sits with which party.
TypeHow it's priced
Lump sumA single fixed price for defined scope — contractor carries most quantity/productivity risk, client carries scope-definition risk.
Unit price / remeasurementPriced per unit of measured work (BOQ rates × actual quantities) — quantity risk shifts toward the client since final quantities are remeasured.
Cost plusClient reimburses actual cost plus an agreed fee/margin — client carries most cost risk, useful when scope can't be well defined upfront.
EPC / turnkeyEngineering, Procurement and Construction — one contractor takes single-point responsibility for design and delivery of a complete, ready-to-operate facility.
Design-buildOne contractor is responsible for both design and construction, as opposed to a client-appointed designer with a separate construction contractor.
FIDIC formsA widely used family of standard international contract templates (e.g. the Red Book for construction, Yellow Book for design-build, Silver Book for EPC/turnkey) — each allocates risk differently.
