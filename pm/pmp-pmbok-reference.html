The Five Process Groups

The chronological "buckets" a project's processes fall into, per the PMBOK Guide — not phases, since all five can be active at once on a multi-phase project.
GroupWhat happens here
InitiatingFormally authorizing the project or a phase — developing the project charter, identifying initial stakeholders.
PlanningEstablishing scope, refining objectives, and defining the course of action to deliver them — the largest group by process count (schedule, cost, risk, quality and procurement planning all sit here).
ExecutingDoing the work defined in the plan — directing and managing project work, acquiring and developing the team, managing quality.
Monitoring & ControllingTracking, reviewing and regulating progress and performance — the home of the EVM metrics on PM.05, change control, and risk monitoring.
ClosingFormally finalizing all activities to close the project, phase, or contract — capturing lessons learned and releasing resources.

The Ten Knowledge Areas

The subject-matter groupings the PMBOK Guide organizes its processes into (PMBOK 6th edition structure — the 7th edition reorganized around principles and performance domains instead, but this breakdown is still the one most commonly taught and referenced day to day).
Knowledge areaCovers
Integration ManagementTying everything together — charter, project management plan, change control, closing the project.
Scope ManagementDefining and controlling what is and isn't included — requirements, WBS, scope verification and control.
Schedule ManagementDefining activities, sequencing, estimating durations, and developing/controlling the schedule.
Cost ManagementEstimating, budgeting and controlling cost — this is where EVM (PM.05) formally lives.
Quality ManagementPlanning, managing and controlling quality — see QA vs QC below.
Resource ManagementEstimating, acquiring, developing and managing the project team and physical resources.
Communications ManagementPlanning, managing and monitoring how information flows to stakeholders.
Risk ManagementIdentifying, analyzing, planning responses to, and monitoring risk — see PM.02 for a working risk register.
Procurement ManagementPlanning, conducting and controlling purchases and contracts with external vendors.
Stakeholder ManagementIdentifying stakeholders and planning, managing and monitoring their engagement.

Schedule & Scope Terms

Vocabulary that shows up in almost every scheduling conversation, and heavily on the exam.
TermMeaning
WBSWork Breakdown Structure — a hierarchical decomposition of the total scope into smaller, manageable work packages.
Critical pathThe longest sequence of dependent activities through the schedule — it determines the shortest possible project duration; delaying any activity on it delays the whole project.
Float / slackHow much an activity can slip without delaying the project (total float) or its immediate successor (free float). Critical path activities have zero total float.
Fast-trackingCompressing the schedule by doing activities in parallel that were originally planned in sequence — shortens duration but can add rework risk.
CrashingCompressing the schedule by adding resources to critical path activities — shortens duration but typically increases cost.
Precedence diagrammingThe standard method of showing activities as nodes connected by dependency arrows (finish-to-start, start-to-start, etc.) to build a network diagram.
Scope creepUncontrolled expansion of scope without corresponding adjustment to time, cost or resources — usually a sign of a missing or weak change control process.
Change control boardThe formal body that reviews and approves or rejects proposed changes to the project baseline.

Risk & Quality Terms

Distinctions that are easy to blur in conversation but mean different things formally.
TermMeaning
Contingency reserveBudget or time set aside for known risks (identified in the risk register) — owned and released by the project manager.
Management reserveBudget or time set aside for unknown/unforeseen risks — held above the project budget and typically requires sponsor/management approval to release.
Quality assurance (QA)Process-focused — proactively auditing whether the right processes are being followed to prevent defects.
Quality control (QC)Product-focused — inspecting completed deliverables to detect defects that already occurred.
Risk appetite / toleranceHow much risk an organization or stakeholder is willing to accept in pursuit of a given objective, before deciding a response is needed.
Positive vs negative riskPMBOK treats risk as any uncertain event that could affect objectives — including upside (opportunity) as well as downside (threat) risk.

PMP Credential Basics

High-level, generally known facts about the credential — PMI's own site is the authoritative source for current eligibility requirements and exam content, since both are updated periodically.
ItemSummary
Issuing bodyProject Management Institute (PMI).
Eligibility (typical)A combination of a specific amount of project management experience (fewer years required with a four-year degree, more with a secondary diploma) plus formal project management education hours — PMI's site has the exact current thresholds.
Exam domainsOrganized around three domains: People, Process, and Business Environment — reflecting a shift toward both predictive and agile/hybrid approaches rather than one methodology.
Certification maintenanceOngoing PDUs (Professional Development Units) are required within a multi-year cycle to keep the credential active.
