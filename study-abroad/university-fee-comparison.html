About this comparison

Comparing universities side by side on tuition alone is one of the most common ways prospective students underestimate the real cost difference between options, since tuition is often only part of the total out-of-pocket figure. This tool is built to pull in the other recurring costs — living expenses, insurance, and other fees — alongside tuition, so that a lower headline tuition number in an expensive city can be weighed fairly against a higher tuition number somewhere with a much lower cost of living.

It's still a simplified comparison: it won't capture scholarship or assistantship funding that might apply unevenly across the shortlisted universities, currency movement over the length of the program, or annual tuition increases that some universities apply to continuing students. Use the side-by-side total as a starting point for narrowing a shortlist, then check each university's own published, itemized cost of attendance before treating any single number here as final.

The cheapest total isn't automatically the right choice — factor in program reputation, post-study work rights, and scholarship renewal conditions before deciding.
