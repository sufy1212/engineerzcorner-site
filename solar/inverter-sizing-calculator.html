Continuous & motor-starting surge for off-grid/hybrid, or DC:AC ratio for grid-tie
Solar Index SO.01 Sizing Tools SO.16 Inverter Sizing SO.02 Notes
Off-grid and hybrid inverters are sized on load — a continuous rating for everything running at once, plus a higher surge rating to survive the inrush current when a motor or compressor starts. Grid-tie inverters are sized differently, checked instead against the PV array's power as a DC:AC ratio. Use whichever step matches your system type.

Off-Grid / Hybrid: Continuous & Surge Load

Continuous rating covers everything running at once. Surge rating covers the moment your largest single motor or compressor starts, while everything else keeps running — motors typically draw 2–3× their running current for a second or two on startup.

Grid-Tie: DC:AC Oversizing Ratio

Grid-tie inverters are commonly paired with a PV array rated somewhat higher than the inverter's AC output — the array rarely hits its full STC rating in the field, and modest oversizing improves early/late-day and cloudy-day production. Too much oversizing means the inverter clips (caps) output at midday.

Why Surge Rating and DC:AC Ratio Both Matter

An inverter that's rated high enough to run every appliance continuously can still trip or shut down the instant a well pump, air-conditioner compressor or refrigerator motor starts, because that brief inrush current can be two to three times the motor's running current. The surge rating — not the continuous rating — is usually what determines whether an off-grid or hybrid inverter can actually start your largest motor load.

Grid-tie sizing is a different question entirely: since the array almost never produces its full nameplate (STC) power in real conditions, a DC:AC ratio of roughly 1.1 to 1.3 is typical and lets the inverter run closer to full output more of the day without much clipping loss. Ratios pushed toward 1.4–1.5 are sometimes used in cooler or hazier climates where peak output rarely approaches STC; in consistently sunny, hot regions the same ratio would clip more noticeably at midday.
