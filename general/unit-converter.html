All conversions use standard exact or CODATA-rounded factors (e.g. 1 in = 25.4 mm exactly, 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg exactly). Temperature uses its own offset formulas, not a simple multiplier.

What's Covered

The converter spans eleven everyday and engineering categories — things like length, area, volume, mass, temperature, pressure, energy, power, and speed — moving between metric and imperial units. Within each category, every unit converts through a common base unit (for example, everything under length converts through meters), so results stay internally consistent no matter which two units you pick.

It's useful for reading a spec sheet from an overseas manufacturer, converting an imperial drawing dimension for a metric client, or sanity-checking a conversion by hand instead of trusting a remembered factor. It isn't a substitute for the exact regulatory or trade-specific definition of a unit where that distinction matters — US survey feet versus international feet, or fluid ounces which differ between US and UK measure — confirm the governing standard for anything with contractual or legal weight.
