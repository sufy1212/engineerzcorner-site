What BMI Does and Doesn't Tell You

BMI is weight divided by height squared — a simple screening ratio that the WHO and other health bodies use to sort adults into broad categories (underweight, normal, overweight, obese) at a population level. This calculator accepts either metric (kg/m²) or imperial inputs and converts automatically.

Its main limitation is that it can't distinguish muscle mass from fat mass and doesn't adjust for age, sex, frame size, or athletic build — a muscular athlete and a sedentary person can land on the same BMI with very different body compositions. Treat the result as one general data point rather than a diagnosis, and talk to a clinician for individual health guidance.
