Reading a Tape Measure in Fractions

Construction tape measures mark inches in fractions — usually down to 1/16", sometimes 1/32" — but drawings, spreadsheets, and CAD or CNC software almost always expect a decimal number instead. A reading like 5/16" needs to become 0.3125" before it's useful in most software, and converting that by hand for every dimension on a takeoff gets tedious fast. This tool goes both directions: type a feet-inches-fraction measurement and get its decimal equivalent, or type a decimal length and get the nearest sixteenth.

Results round to the nearest 1/16", which matches the resolution of a standard site tape measure and is fine for general layout, framing, and takeoff work. It isn't precise enough for machining or fine fabrication tolerances, which typically need 1/32", 1/64", or a direct decimal/thousandths spec — for that kind of work, read the dimension directly in decimal rather than rounding through a fraction.
