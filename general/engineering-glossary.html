A – E

TermDefinition
AmpacityElectricalThe maximum current a conductor can carry continuously without exceeding its temperature rating.
Arc FlashElectricalAn explosive release of energy from an electrical fault through air, capable of causing severe burns.
AzimuthSolarThe compass direction a solar panel faces, measured from true north.
Batch ProcessProcessA manufacturing method that runs a fixed quantity of material through a set of steps, rather than continuously.
Bearing CapacityCivilThe maximum pressure soil can support beneath a foundation without shear failure.
Bearing Life (L10)MechanicalThe number of cycles or hours that 90% of a batch of identical bearings are expected to survive before fatigue failure.
Bifacial ModuleSolarA solar panel that can generate power from both its front and rear faces, using reflected light on the back side.
Bus BarElectricalA rigid metal bar or strip used to carry heavy currents within a switchboard or panel.
CamberCivilA slight built-in upward curvature in a beam, intended to offset the downward deflection expected under load.
CavitationMechanicalThe formation and violent collapse of vapor bubbles in a liquid, which erodes pump and valve surfaces over time.
CFM (Cubic Feet per Minute)HVACA measure of volumetric airflow rate — how much air moves past a point every minute.
Compressive StrengthCivilThe capacity of a material, such as concrete, to withstand loads that tend to reduce its size.
DeadbandAutomationA range around a setpoint where no control action is taken, used to prevent a controller from chattering.
DeflectionCivilThe degree to which a structural element bends or displaces under an applied load.
Degradation RateSolarThe annual percentage decline in a solar panel's power output over its operating lifetime.
DeratingElectricalReducing a component's rated capacity to account for real-world conditions like ambient heat or cable grouping.
Dew PointHVACThe temperature at which air becomes saturated with moisture and condensation begins to form.
EnthalpyHVACThe total heat content of air, combining both sensible heat (temperature) and latent heat (moisture).

F – K

TermDefinition
Fail-SafeAutomationA design principle where a failure results in the safest possible state — e.g. a valve that fails closed.
Fatigue FailureMechanicalFailure of a material caused by repeated cyclic loading, often at stress levels well below its ultimate strength.
FieldbusAutomationA digital, two-way communication network connecting multiple field instruments to a control system over a shared cable.
FOS (Factor of Safety)MechanicalThe ratio between a material's strength and the actual stress applied to it, giving a margin against failure.
Ground FaultElectricalAn unintended connection between an energized conductor and ground, which a GFCI or earth-fault relay is designed to detect.
HarmonicsElectricalVoltage or current distortions occurring at whole-number multiples of the fundamental frequency, usually caused by nonlinear loads like VFDs.
HART ProtocolAutomationA hybrid communication protocol that layers digital data on top of a traditional 4-20mA analog signal.
HAZOP (Hazard and Operability Study)ProcessA structured team review that systematically identifies deviations from a process's design intent and the hazards they could cause.
IP RatingGeneralAn Ingress Protection code (e.g. IP65) rating how well an enclosure resists solid particles and water.
IrradianceSolarThe instantaneous power of sunlight received per unit area, measured in watts per square meter (W/m²).

L – P

TermDefinition
Latent HeatHVACHeat that changes the moisture content of air (evaporating or condensing water) without changing its temperature.
Live Load / Dead LoadCivilLive load is variable, temporary loading (people, furniture, snow); dead load is a structure's permanent self-weight.
Loop TuningAutomationAdjusting a controller's PID parameters so a control loop responds accurately to changes without becoming unstable.
LOTO (Lockout/Tagout)GeneralA safety procedure that isolates and locks out hazardous energy sources before maintenance or repair work begins.
Mass BalanceProcessAn accounting of all material entering and leaving a process, used to verify nothing is unaccounted for.
MOC (Management of Change)GeneralA formal process for reviewing, approving and documenting changes to equipment, procedures or materials before they're implemented.
Moment of InertiaCivilA geometric property of a cross-section that indicates its resistance to bending.
MPPT (Maximum Power Point Tracking)SolarA technique that continuously adjusts a solar array's electrical operating point to extract the maximum power available at any moment.
MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures)GeneralThe average operating time between one failure of a repairable system and the next, used as a reliability metric.
MTTR (Mean Time To Repair)GeneralThe average time it takes to repair a failed system and restore it to operation, from the moment the failure occurs.
N+1 RedundancyGeneralA design with one more unit than the minimum needed, so a single unit can fail without taking the system down.
NDT (Non-Destructive Testing)GeneralInspection methods — ultrasonic, radiographic, dye penetrant and others — that evaluate material integrity without damaging the part.
Net MeteringSolarA billing arrangement that credits a solar system owner for excess electricity exported back to the grid.
Nominal VoltageSolarA system's reference voltage rating (e.g. 12V, 48V), used for sizing and naming rather than as an exact operating value.
NPSH (Net Positive Suction Head)MechanicalThe pressure margin available at a pump's suction above the liquid's vapor pressure, needed to prevent cavitation.
P&ID (Piping and Instrumentation Diagram)ProcessA detailed schematic showing a process's piping, equipment and instrumentation, down to individual valves and sensors.
PFD (Process Flow Diagram)ProcessA simplified diagram showing a process's major steps and material flow, without the full instrumentation detail of a P&ID.
PID ControllerAutomationA control loop combining proportional, integral and derivative action to drive a process variable to its setpoint and hold it there.
Power FactorElectricalThe ratio of real power to apparent power in an AC circuit, indicating how effectively the supplied power is actually used.
Preventive MaintenanceMechanicalMaintenance performed on a scheduled basis to prevent failure, rather than in response to one that's already happened.
PsychrometricsHVACThe study of the physical and thermodynamic properties of air-water vapor mixtures.

Q – S

TermDefinition
Rangeability (Turndown)AutomationThe ratio between a device's maximum and minimum accurately measurable or controllable values.
RCA (Root Cause Analysis)GeneralA structured method for identifying the true underlying cause of a failure or incident, rather than just treating its symptoms.
Rebar (Reinforcing Bar)CivilSteel bars embedded in concrete to carry the tensile loads that concrete alone handles poorly.
Relief ValveProcessA safety device that automatically opens to release pressure once it exceeds a pre-set threshold, protecting equipment from overpressure.
Reynolds NumberMechanicalA dimensionless number that predicts whether fluid flow will be laminar (smooth) or turbulent.
SDS (Safety Data Sheet)GeneralA standardized document detailing a chemical's hazards, safe handling, storage and emergency-response information.
Selectivity (Discrimination)ElectricalCoordinating protective devices so that only the one nearest a fault trips, keeping the rest of the system energized.
Sensible HeatHVACHeat that changes air temperature without changing its moisture content — the heat you'd feel with a thermometer.
SetpointAutomationThe target value a control system attempts to maintain a process variable at.
SettlementCivilThe gradual sinking or movement of a structure's foundation as the underlying soil consolidates under load.
Shear ForceCivilAn internal force acting parallel to a structural cross-section, tending to slide one part past the other.
Short-Circuit CurrentElectricalThe current that flows when a fault bypasses normal load impedance — the value equipment fault ratings are based on.
SIL (Safety Integrity Level)AutomationA measure (SIL 1 to 4) of how much risk reduction a safety instrumented function is required to provide.
Single-Line Diagram (SLD)ElectricalA simplified schematic that represents a three-phase electrical system's major components and connections as a single line.
Slump TestCivilA field test that measures the workability and consistency of fresh concrete before it's placed.
SpanAutomationThe difference between the upper and lower range values an instrument is calibrated to measure.
Static PressureHVACThe resistance to airflow within ductwork, measured perpendicular to the direction of flow.
StringSolarA series-connected chain of solar panels forming a single circuit into an inverter's DC input.
SubcoolingHVACThe temperature of liquid refrigerant below its saturation temperature, used to verify a system's refrigerant charge.
SuperheatHVACThe temperature of refrigerant vapor above its saturation temperature, used to verify a system's refrigerant charge.

T – Z

TermDefinition
TAB (Testing, Adjusting & Balancing)HVACThe process of verifying and adjusting an HVAC system so it delivers its designed airflow and performance.
Tilt AngleSolarThe angle a fixed solar array is mounted at, measured from horizontal.
ToleranceMechanicalThe permissible variation allowed in a dimension or fit between mating parts.
Ton of RefrigerationHVACA unit of cooling capacity equal to 12,000 BTU/hr — the rate needed to melt one ton of ice in 24 hours.
TorqueMechanicalA rotational force that causes, or tends to cause, an object to twist about an axis.
TPM (Total Productive Maintenance)GeneralA maintenance philosophy that involves all employees, not just maintenance staff, in maximizing equipment effectiveness.
TransmitterAutomationA field device that converts a physical process measurement (pressure, level, temperature) into a standardized signal for transmission.
Turnaround (Shutdown)ProcessA planned period when a process unit is taken offline for maintenance, inspection and repairs that can't be done while running.
Utilities (Process)ProcessSupporting services — steam, cooling water, compressed air, nitrogen — that a process depends on but doesn't directly convert into product.
VAV (Variable Air Volume)HVACAn HVAC system that varies the volume of air delivered at a constant temperature to meet each zone's changing load.
Vibration AnalysisMechanicalMeasuring a machine's vibration signature to detect developing mechanical faults before they cause failure.
ViscosityMechanicalA fluid's resistance to flow — how thick or "sticky" it behaves under shear.
Voc (Open-Circuit Voltage)SolarThe voltage a panel or string produces with no current flowing — the worst-case (highest) voltage used in cold-weather string sizing.
Voltage DropElectricalThe reduction in voltage along a conductor, caused by its resistance and the current flowing through it.
Yield StrengthMechanicalThe stress level at which a material begins to deform permanently instead of springing back elastically.
Zero and Span CalibrationAutomationAdjusting an instrument's output to match known reference points at the low (zero) and high (span) ends of its range.
