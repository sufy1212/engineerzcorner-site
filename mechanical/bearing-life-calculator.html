Bearing L10 Life

Basic rating life from dynamic load rating, equivalent load and speed
L10 basic rating life is the number of revolutions (or hours, at a given speed) that 90% of a batch of identical bearings under identical conditions are expected to reach or exceed before the first sign of fatigue. It comes straight from the bearing's catalogue dynamic load rating and the equivalent dynamic load it actually sees in service — not from the static load rating, which is a separate check against permanent deformation.

How this is calculated
L10 = (C / P)p  million revolutions
L10h = 10⁶ × L10 / (60 × N)  hours
L10mh = a₁ × aISO × L10h
p = 3 for ball bearings, p = 10/3 for roller bearings. The "continuous 24/7" figure just converts L10mh into years assuming the shaft never stops — divide by your actual annual operating hours instead for a duty-cycle-adjusted figure.

About the Bearing L10 Life Calculator

Given a bearing's catalogue dynamic load rating, the equivalent dynamic load it carries in service, and its operating speed, this works out the L10 basic rating life — the point at which 90% of an identical batch of bearings would be expected to still be running fatigue-free. It's the standard first-pass check used to compare candidate bearings or confirm a selection meets a target service life.

The optional reliability factor (a₁) and life modification factor (aISO) adjust the basic figure for a target reliability above 90% and for real lubrication/contamination conditions respectively — both come from the bearing manufacturer's own published tables and charts rather than a general formula, since they depend on the specific bearing design and operating environment.

This is a rating-life calculation only. A complete bearing selection also confirms the static load rating isn't exceeded at peak load, checks the limiting speed for the chosen lubrication method, and specifies the correct shaft/housing fits — none of which this sheet covers.
