Full Mechanical Symbol Reference

Every symbol above, with its full meaning and typical usage in one scannable table — useful for a quick page search or side-by-side comparison instead of clicking through each tile individually.

SymbolCategoryMeaningWhere it's used
Centrifugal PumpRotating EquipmentA circle with an internal triangle pointing in the flow direction — the standard P&ID symbol for a centrifugal pump.Placed in-line on process and utility piping wherever a pump moves liquid through the system.
Gate ValveValvesA bowtie (hourglass) shape — the generic valve symbol; the body style at the bowtie indicates the specific valve type.General on/off isolation valves throughout piping and instrumentation diagrams.
Check (Non-Return) ValveValvesA triangle pointing in the permitted flow direction with a bar at its tip — allows flow one way only.Downstream of pumps and compressors to prevent backflow when the unit stops.
Heat ExchangerHeat TransferA shell outline with internal tube lines — represents any device transferring heat between two fluid streams.Shell-and-tube coolers, condensers and process heat exchangers on mechanical/process drawings.
CompressorRotating EquipmentA tapered body symbol distinguishing a gas compressor from a liquid pump.Refrigeration compressors, air compressors and process gas compression trains.
Tank / VesselStorageA rounded rectangle representing any storage or process vessel — proportions vary with the real vessel's aspect ratio.Storage tanks, surge vessels, and pressure vessels on process flow diagrams.
Strainer / FilterIn-Line EquipmentA funnel/basket outline — represents an in-line strainer or filter element that traps debris from the flow.Upstream of pumps, control valves and meters to protect them from debris.
Flow MeterInstrumentsA circle with a cross-shaped restriction — the generic in-line flow measurement device symbol.Orifice plates, venturi meters and other in-line flow measurement points.
Pressure GaugeInstrumentsA circular dial face with a needle — represents a local, direct-reading pressure indicator.Local pressure indication at pumps, vessels and along process lines.
BearingMachine ElementsTwo concentric circles — represents a rotating shaft support bearing in a machine section drawing.Shaft support points in pump, motor and gearbox cross-section drawings.
CouplingMachine ElementsTwo shaft ends joined by a coupling block — transmits rotation between two connected shafts.Pump-to-motor and other driver-to-driven shaft connections in mechanical layout drawings.
GearboxMachine ElementsAn enclosure containing two meshed gears — represents a speed-reducing or speed-increasing gearbox.Between a motor and a slower/faster driven load, such as a conveyor drive or large fan.
Globe ValveValvesThe generic bowtie valve symbol with a stem projecting from the body — a valve with good throttling control via an S-shaped flow path.Applications needing precise flow throttling rather than simple full-open/full-closed isolation.
Ball ValveValvesA bowtie shape with a circle at its center — a quarter-turn valve using a bored ball to control flow.General isolation service needing fast, low-torque quarter-turn operation.
Butterfly ValveValvesA circle (the body) with a diagonal line (the disc) — a compact quarter-turn valve using a rotating disc in the flow path.Large-diameter isolation on HVAC and low-pressure process lines where a compact valve is needed.
Relief / Safety ValveValvesA valve body with a spring-loaded cap and an arrow discharge — opens automatically above a set pressure to protect equipment.Vessel and piping overpressure protection, discharging to a safe location or flare/vent header.
Control ValveValvesA bowtie valve topped with an actuator box — automatically modulates flow in response to a control signal.Automated process loops where flow, pressure or level is continuously adjusted by a controller.
Solenoid ValveValvesA bowtie valve with a coil symbol above it — an electrically actuated on/off valve, open or closed with no intermediate position.Fast-acting on/off shutoff in pneumatic, hydraulic and utility fluid control circuits.
Positive Displacement PumpRotating EquipmentA casing enclosing two meshing gears or lobes — moves a fixed volume of fluid per revolution regardless of discharge pressure.Viscous fluid transfer, metering duties and applications needing constant flow independent of head.
TurbineRotating EquipmentA shaft with radiating blade lines — converts fluid or steam energy into rotating shaft power.Steam and gas turbine drivers on single-line mechanical drive diagrams.
BlowerRotating EquipmentA housing with an internal curved impeller line — a lower-pressure-rise cousin of a compressor, moving large air volumes.Combustion air supply, pneumatic conveying and low-pressure process air systems.
Agitator / MixerRotating EquipmentA shaft entering a vessel with paddle blades — mechanically mixes or agitates the vessel's contents.Mixing tanks, reactors and any vessel needing continuous blending of its contents.
Temperature GaugeInstrumentsA circle enclosing the letter T — a local, direct-reading temperature indicator.Local temperature indication at vessels, exchangers and along process lines.
Sight Glass / Level GaugeInstrumentsA narrow vertical tube alongside a vessel — provides direct visual confirmation of liquid level.Tanks and vessels needing local, power-free level verification alongside instrumented level measurement.
RotameterInstrumentsA tapered tube with a float symbol — a variable-area flow meter read directly from the float's position.Local flow indication on utility and low-pressure process lines needing simple visual confirmation.
Pipe Expansion JointIn-Line EquipmentA bellows-shaped section in a straight pipe run — absorbs thermal expansion and vibration without stressing the pipe.Long straight pipe runs and connections to rotating equipment needing vibration isolation.
Silencer / MufflerIn-Line EquipmentA tapered inline body — reduces noise generated by gas flow, typically at a compressor or relief discharge.Compressor intake/discharge lines and pressure relief valve outlets needing noise attenuation.
Steam TrapIn-Line EquipmentAn in-line device symbol — automatically drains condensate from a steam line while blocking live steam from escaping.Low points in steam distribution piping and at the outlet of every steam-heated exchanger or coil.
Y-StrainerIn-Line EquipmentA Y-shaped branch off the main line ending in a removable screen — catches debris with the line still in service for cleaning.Upstream of pumps, meters and control valves on liquid and gas service lines.
FlangeMachine ElementsA bolted circular joint symbol — the standard demountable connection between pipe spools and equipment nozzles.Every point on a piping isometric where two spools, or a spool and equipment, bolt together.
Shaft SealMachine ElementsA sealing element around a rotating shaft — prevents process fluid leakage where a shaft passes through a casing.Pump and mixer shaft penetrations through a pressurized or fluid-filled casing.
Compression SpringMachine ElementsA coiled zig-zag line — a mechanical element storing energy and resisting compressive displacement.Relief valve loading, vibration isolation mounts and mechanical return mechanisms.
Cooling TowerHeat TransferA tapered structure with internal fill lines — rejects process heat to atmosphere by evaporative cooling.Condenser water systems, rejecting chiller and process heat to the atmosphere.
RadiatorHeat TransferA finned rectangular core — rejects heat from a circulating fluid directly to ambient air.Engine cooling systems and unit heaters rejecting or delivering heat to open air.
Day TankStorageA small vessel with a marked working-level line — holds a short-duration local supply, typically fuel, between refills.Generator fuel systems, providing a buffered local fuel supply between bulk tank deliveries.
SumpStorageA tapered collection basin — gathers drainage or leakage at the low point of a system for pump-out.Pump station wet wells and equipment room floor drainage collection points.
Pipe SupportStructural ElementsA vertical member beneath a pipe run — carries the pipe's weight down to a structural floor or foundation.Piping isometric and layout drawings at every point a pipe needs vertical support.
Pipe HangerStructural ElementsA rod suspending a pipe from an overhead structure — carries pipe weight in tension rather than compression.Overhead piping routes hung from a ceiling or structural steel rather than floor-supported.
