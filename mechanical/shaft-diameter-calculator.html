About Shaft Diameter Sizing

A power-transmission shaft almost never sees pure torsion — belt pull, gear mesh forces and overhung couplings all add a bending moment on top of the torque the shaft is transmitting. The ASME code equation combines the two into a single equivalent stress check, and folds in a Kb/Kt factor pair that accounts for whether the load is steady or shock-loaded, since shock loading raises the effective stress well above what a static calculation alone would show.

This gives a minimum diameter for the section being checked — round up to the next available stock size, and re-check at every other section where the bending moment, torque, or a keyway/shoulder stress concentration might govern instead. Fatigue life, critical (whirling) speed, and shaft deflection at bearings and couplings are separate checks a complete shaft design should still go through.
